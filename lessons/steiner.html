‹ Dynamic programming on graphs

Few terminals, many ordinary graph vertices

Connect a few required terminals using a cheap shared network of edges. Why can three separate shortest paths cost too much?

Work through the choice

Paths to different terminals can share a branch. At a meeting vertex, merge solutions for two disjoint terminal groups, then allow the meeting point to move through the graph at shortest-path cost.

The rule you can reuse

To connect k terminals in a nonnegative undirected graph, combine two terminal sets at a common meeting vertex and then move that meeting vertex along a cheapest path. Only terminal membership requires an exponential axis.

D(M,v)←min⁡∅≠A⊊M{D(A,v)+D(M∖A,v)}D(M,v)\gets\min_{\varnothing\ne A\subsetneq M}\{D(A,v)+D(M\setminus A,v)\}D(M,v)←∅=A⊊Mmin​{D(A,v)+D(M∖A,v)}

D[M,v]=minimum tree cost connecting terminals M and v. First merge D[A,v]+D[M without A,v] for nonempty proper A; then run multi-source Dijkstra initialized by those merged values. Singletons start at their terminal.

Check the boundary. Process masks by increasing size. Relaxing shortest paths before subset merges alone misses paths after a new merge. A bitmask over every graph vertex usually wastes the small-terminal condition.

Cost O(3ᵏV+2ᵏ(E+V)log V), O(2ᵏV) memory. For grid or DAG movement a different closure step may be cheaper.

From idea to code

  • Enumerate submasks
    The loop visits every nonempty submask. Add the empty option separately if it is legal. Enumerating submasks for every mask costs three to the power n across all masks.
  • Dijkstra on an expanded graph
    First define what constitutes a vertex of the state graph. Construct an edge for each legal transition. All weights used by Dijkstra must be nonnegative.

In a tree, removing just one edge already separates two independent sides. Treat that edge as the place where information must cross.
