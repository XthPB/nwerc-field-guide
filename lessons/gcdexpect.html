Measure a random process · Lesson 122 of 235

When a random gcd can stay where it is

Start at gcd 4. Repeatedly draw uniformly from 1,2,3,4 and replace the current gcd by its gcd with the draw. How many further draws do you expect before reaching 1?

1. Work one example

At gcd 2, odd draws finish and even draws stay, so E(2)=1+E(2)/2=2. At 4, draws 1 and 3 finish, 2 moves to gcd 2, and 4 stays. Thus E(4)=1+(2+E(4))/4, giving E(4)=2.

2. What do we need to remember?

The entire history matters only through its current gcd. Smaller gcds are already solved. A draw that preserves the gcd must remain in the equation.

3. How do we get the next answer?

E(g)=M+∑d∣g, d<gc(g,d)E(d)M−c(g,g).E(g)=\frac{M+\sum_{d\mid g,\ d<g}c(g,d)E(d)}{M-c(g,g)}.E(g)=M−c(g,g)M+∑d∣g, d<g​c(g,d)E(d)​.

Here c(g,d) counts draws x with gcd(g,x)=d and M is the number of equally likely draws. Multiply the first-step equation by M and move self-loops to the left. For large inputs count multiples and invert over divisors instead of testing every draw for every g. If the initial gcd is itself sampled, average its expectation separately.

Check the boundary. If all possible draws preserve a nonterminal gcd, the denominator is zero and the expected hitting time is infinite. Uniform draws from 1 through M include 1, so the example always has a way out.

Cost O(M² log M) worst-case time and O(M) space for the transparent reference implementation. The contest-scale version also needs fast multiplicities.

Try one change

At gcd 2, uniform draws 1,2,3,4 either finish or leave the gcd unchanged. Why isn't the answer one draw?

See the reasoning

Only the odd draws finish. Each even draw costs one turn and restarts the same question, giving E=1+E/2 and E=2.
