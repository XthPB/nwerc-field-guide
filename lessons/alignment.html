Two prefixes and the last alignment column

How many edits turn ab into acb if inserting, deleting or replacing one letter costs one?

Work through the choice

Look at the final operation. The table cell for two prefixes can come from deleting the last source letter, inserting the last target letter, or aligning the two. Matching equal letters costs zero.

The rule you can reuse

When two ordered strings may skip, replace, or match characters, index the consumed prefix of each. An optimal alignment ends in a deletion, insertion or paired column. The operations partition the predecessor cases.

Ei,j=min⁡{Ei−1,j+d, Ei,j−1+a, Ei−1,j−1+sij}E_{i,j}=\min\{E_{i-1,j}+d,\ E_{i,j-1}+a,\ E_{i-1,j-1}+s_{ij}\}Ei,j​=min{Ei−1,j​+d, Ei,j−1​+a, Ei−1,j−1​+sij​}

Edit: E[i,j]=min(E[i−1,j]+del, E[i,j−1]+ins, E[i−1,j−1]+sub). For LCS keep max of two skips and a diagonal +1 on equality.

Check the boundary. Set E[i,0] and E[0,j] to the appropriate deletion/insertion prefix costs. A zero boundary is correct for LCS. For affine gap penalties add a state for which gap is open.

Cost O(nm) time, O(min(n,m)) memory for the value. A band of width k gives O(nk) for unit edit distance with a known threshold k.
