Equivalent suffixes under gcd, AND, or OR

For each right endpoint, count which gcd values occur among subarrays ending there.

1. Work one example

Extend every old suffix by one item and take its gcd with that item. Suffixes that now share a gcd respond identically to every later extension. Merge them and add their multiplicities.

2. What do we need to remember?

Many suffixes share the same gcd. When a new value is appended, merge entries that collapse to the same new gcd.

3. How do we get the next answer?

For subarrays ending at i, combine the new element with each distinct aggregate from i−1, then coalesce equal values. Future extensions act identically on equal aggregates. Store multiplicity, earliest start, or best score as required.

Check the boundary. The short-chain proof uses divisor shrinkage for positive gcds or bits that can flip only once. It does not transfer to arbitrary sums or XOR.

Cost O(n log A) aggregate states for positive gcds up to A, times the cost of gcd. AND/OR have O(bit width) distinct suffix states per endpoint.

From idea to code
