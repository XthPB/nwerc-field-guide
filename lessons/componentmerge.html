Split, merge, or temporarily omit · Lesson 204 of 235

Merge secondary structures from smaller subtree into larger

A tree vertex wants the cheapest line supplied by any solved descendant. Do you need to reinsert every line from scratch at every ancestor?

1. Work one example

Keep one line container per solved subtree. Merge smaller containers into the largest, query before inserting the current vertex's own line, and carry the result upward. Account for both container moves and line insertion cost.

2. What do we need to remember?

Keep the larger child container and insert the smaller one into it. Each moving entry must belong to a container that grows sufficiently.

3. How do we get the next answer?

A tree DP may need a whole set of lines, depths or colours from descendants. After all children are solved, reuse the largest child’s container and insert elements of smaller containers. Then query once at the parent.

For D[u]=min over proper descendants v of (aᵤbᵥ+D[v]), each solved descendant contributes a line of slope bᵥ and intercept D[v]. Leaves have no jump and cost zero.

Check the boundary. For the doubling proof use subtree/item counts or retain all original line records, even if a hull drops dominated lines. Merging only active envelopes can be valid for values, but its size bound needs its own proof.

Cost With each line reinserted O(log n) times into an O(log U) discrete Li Chao structure, O(n log n log U) time. Design ownership so obsolete hulls can be released.

Why move entries from a smaller map into a larger one rather than merging in a fixed child order?

See the reasoning

Under small-to-large merging, the destination size at least doubles for a moved surviving entry when key sets are disjoint. Overlap needs the usual insertion-count or amortization argument.

How a descendant becomes a line
  1. Suppose two leaf descendants charge 3x and 5x. Leaves have no future cost. At a parent with x=4 the two choices cost 12 and 20, so finishing from that parent costs 12.
  2. The parent itself can now be offered to its ancestors. If its slope is 1, its line is x+12. At an ancestor with x=10, this new option costs 22 and beats the leaf costs 30 and 50.
  3. Process children first and merge their line containers. Query that merged container before inserting the current vertex. This order prevents the vertex from choosing itself. Keeping the larger raw collection when merging bounds how many times each descendant moves.
