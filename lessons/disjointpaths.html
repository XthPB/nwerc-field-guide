Count paths without visiting every cell · Lesson 185 of 235

Count vertex-disjoint lattice paths using a determinant

How many ways can two directed paths connect two ordered starts to two ordered finishes without sharing a vertex?

1. Work one example

Count routes separately for every start and finish. Multiplying diagonal entries counts paired routes but includes intersections. Swapping the tails after a canonical first intersection pairs intersecting families of opposite endpoint permutation. Their signed contributions cancel.

2. What do we need to remember?

Count route families together and cancel intersecting ones by exchanging their tails. The ordering and planarity conditions matter.

3. How do we get the next answer?

N=det⁡ ⁣(P(si,tj))i,j=1kN=\det\!\left(P(s_i,t_j)\right)_{i,j=1}^{k}N=det(P(si​,tj​))i,j=1k​

Compute P by an ordinary DAG DP or lattice binomials, then evaluate the determinant in the required arithmetic. The combinatorial statement is the Lindström–Gessel–Viennot lemma.

Check the boundary. On a general graph or with interleaved endpoints, another permutation of disjoint paths may be possible. The determinant then gives a signed sum. Using ordinary floating elimination to recover a large exact integer can lose precision.

Cost O(k(V+E)+k³) using one path count per source and field elimination on k endpoints.

Try one change

Why does subtracting the count for swapped endpoints remove intersecting path pairs?

See the reasoning

At their first shared vertex swap the two tails. This pairs intersecting systems of opposite endpoint permutations. The endpoint order must rule out unwanted disjoint permutations.

From idea to code

  • Topological order and DAG DP
    Use a verified acyclic order. Process every outgoing transition after its source is final. Counting merges by addition and longest paths by maximum.
  • Determinant modulo a prime
    Use modular elimination for an exact residue over a prime field. The determinant changes sign when swapping rows. Build a path-count matrix for nonintersecting families before applying this snippet.
