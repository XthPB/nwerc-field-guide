‹ Knapsack and subset sum

Identical weights, distinguishable items, and multiplicity

Three tickets A, B and C each weigh 2. How many pairs reach weight 4?

Work through the choice

The pairs AB, AC and BC are different if the tickets have names. For three interchangeable coins there is only one choice of quantity, namely two. Decide what a distinct answer means before adding counts.

The rule you can reuse

Adding a one-use labeled item creates one new way per old way. With interchangeable copies of one type, the number of copies chosen identifies the choice. With distinct copies, choosing t of c carries a binomial weight.

f′(s)=∑t=0cf(s−tw)for one interchangeable typef'(s)=\sum_{t=0}^{c}f(s-tw)\qquad\text{for one interchangeable type}f′(s)=t=0∑c​f(s−tw)for one interchangeable type

Labeled: f′[s]=f[s]+f[s−w]. Identical type of stock c: f′[s]=Σₜ₌₀…c f[s−tw], with negative indices zero.

Check the boundary. Looping over coins outside counts unordered combinations for unlimited types. Looping over sum first and choosing the last coin counts ordered sequences when coins are positive.

Cost O(nW) for one-use or unlimited. Bounded sums are O(W) per type using a sliding sum on each residue class.
