Minimize trailing zeros on a path, including zero cells

Choose a right/down path with the fewest trailing zeros in its product. What can a grid DP add instead of multiplying enormous integers?

Work through the choice

Factor every positive cell into its counts of twos and fives. A path's trailing zeros equal the smaller total. The best possible smaller total is the smaller of the best two-total and best five-total, so optimize the two path costs separately.

The rule you can reuse

min⁡Pmin⁡(AP,BP)=min⁡ ⁣(min⁡PAP,min⁡PBP) \min_P\min(A_P,B_P)=\min\!\left(\min_P A_P,\min_P B_P\right)Pmin​min(AP​,BP​)=min(Pmin​AP​,Pmin​BP​)

Run two ordinary minimum-sum grid DPs with parents. If the problem treats a product of zero as having one trailing zero, a monotone path through a zero gives a separate candidate of cost one.

Check the boundary. Exclude zeros from prime-exponent paths. Dividing a zero repeatedly to count factors would never terminate. Apply the statement's convention for zero explicitly.

Cost O(HW) DP after extracting factors. O(HW) parents if a path is required.
