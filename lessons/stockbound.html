A cap on the maximum becomes bounded denomination stock

Divide a total into positive integer group sizes. A size s used c times incurs a penalty of s·c, and the score is the largest such penalty. Minimize that score. For a total of 8, first ask which group quantities would be allowed if every penalty were capped at 3.

1. Work one example

Translate the proposed cap into counts of each possible size. Add the sizes in sorted order and check whether the already reachable interval touches the next one. A continuous interval of reachable totals makes the feasibility check cheap.

2. What do we need to remember?

Try a cap on the largest denomination-times-count penalty. Dividing the cap by each denomination gives that denomination’s available stock.

3. How do we get the next answer?

When the penalty is maxₛ s·cₛ for multiplicities cₛ, testing cap C yields independent upper bounds floor(C/s). This looks like bounded subset sum. Consecutive denominations and enough ones can collapse the reachable set to one interval.

0≤cs≤⌊C/s⌋,H(C)=∑s=1Cs⌊C/s⌋0\le c_s\le\lfloor C/s\rfloor,\qquad H(C)=\sum_{s=1}^{C}s\lfloor C/s\rfloor0≤cs​≤⌊C/s⌋,H(C)=s=1∑C​s⌊C/s⌋

Use sizes 1…C with stock floor(C/s). All totals from 0 to H(C)=Σₛ s⌊C/s⌋ are attainable. At least C ones cover 0…C, so every larger denomination extends the interval without a gap.

Check the boundary. When reconstructing from largest denominations down, take as many as fit up to stock. Smaller denominations cover every remainder left by this choice. The number of nonempty bags is at most the number of items.

Cost O(C) per feasibility check, or precompute H for several bounds. Binary-search a cap from 1…n. CF 2273A, rated 1300.

Try one change

The largest denomination times its used count may be at most 10. How many value-3 coins can a solution use?

See the reasoning

At most floor(10/3)=3, further limited by actual stock. Repeat for every denomination to get a bounded knapsack feasibility test.
