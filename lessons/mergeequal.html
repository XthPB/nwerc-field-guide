‹ Interval and partition DP

Delete, paint, or merge matching endpoints

You may delete a whole consecutive run of equal letters in one move. How quickly can you erase aba?

Work through the choice

Deleting b first makes the two a letters neighbours. A good recurrence leaves room for this future saving by asking which later equal letter could share the first letter's deletion.

The rule you can reuse

Sometimes a matching operation can pay once for two separated equal symbols after the middle disappears. Condition on the later position k that shares the first symbol’s deletion turn. This changes the split formula.

D(l,r)=min⁡ ⁣(1+D(l+1,r), min⁡l<k≤rak=al{D(l+1,k−1)+D(k,r)})D(l,r)=\min\!\left(1+D(l+1,r),\ \min_{\substack{l<k\le r\\a_k=a_l}}\{D(l+1,k-1)+D(k,r)\}\right)D(l,r)=min(1+D(l+1,r), l<k≤rak​=al​​min​{D(l+1,k−1)+D(k,r)})

For deleting any monochromatic block, D[l,r]=min(1+D[l+1,r], min over k>l with a[k]=a[l] of D[l+1,k−1]+D[k,r]). Empty costs 0.

Check the boundary. With reward depending on block size, leaving extra equal symbols attached changes the future. Use F(l,r,t) with t attached copies; merging with k increases t. Plain interval endpoints then fail.

Cost O(n³), O(n²) for unit-cost deletion. The extra carry version can require O(n⁴) transitions and O(n³) states.
