Classify a large binary number by its popcount

How many positive integers up to binary N need exactly k applications of popcount to reach 1?

1. Work one example

After one step even a thousand-bit number shrinks to its count of ones. Precompute how long each small possible count needs to reach 1, then count binary strings under the bound by their number of ones.

2. What do we need to remember?

A number’s first popcount reduces it to a much smaller value. Count numbers with each possible number of set bits.

3. How do we get the next answer?

h(1)=0,h(x)=1+h(popcount⁡(x))(x>1)h(1)=0,\qquad h(x)=1+h(\operatorname{popcount}(x))\quad(x>1)h(1)=0,h(x)=1+h(popcount(x))(x>1)

Choose eligible popcounts whose depth is k−1. A tight binary DP counts each bucket, or a combinatorial scan counts free suffixes with a chosen number of ones.

Check the boundary. Handle x=1 separately when k=0 or 1. Zero never reaches 1 and has to be excluded.

Cost O(bits²) simple digit DP. Depth preprocessing is over the bit length, not over N.

Binary 1101 has three ones, 3 has two, and 2 has one. Counts grouped by their first popcount can use the shorter chain from that count.
