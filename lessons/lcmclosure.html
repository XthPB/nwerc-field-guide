‹ Algebraic and counting DP

Close a fixed divisor target under harmless additions

Choose the largest subsequence whose least common multiple is absent from the input.

Work through the choice

Try a target divisor. Every input dividing it is safe to add, since their combined LCM still divides the target. Take them all and check whether their LCM actually reaches it. First handle the easier case where the full-array LCM is already absent.

The rule you can reuse

Suppose a subsequence must have an LCM absent from the input. If the full-array LCM exceeds the largest value M, taking everything already works. Otherwise the full LCM must equal M, so every achievable nonempty LCM divides M.

L(d)=lcm⁡{ai:ai∣d},L(d)=dL(d)=\operatorname{lcm}\{a_i:a_i\mid d\},\qquad L(d)=dL(d)=lcm{ai​:ai​∣d},L(d)=d

For each absent divisor d of M, collect every input aᵢ dividing d. Their combined LCM L divides d. If L=d, all of them may be taken, achieving the largest cardinality with this target. If L≠d, no subset of these divisors can reach d.

Check the boundary. The objective rewards adding harmless items. If every chosen item costs money or exactly k are required, collecting everything no longer solves the inner optimization. Stop a full-array LCM calculation once it exceeds M, and divide by gcd before multiplying.

Cost O(n log M + √M + nτ(M)log M) for trial divisor listing and gcd/LCM scans. Avoid overflow by checking whether a/gcd(a,b)>M/b before multiplication.
