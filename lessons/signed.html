‹ Knapsack and subset sum

Negative weights, balance, residues, and nonempty subsets

Can you choose at least one of +5, −3 and −2 and finish with balance zero?

Work through the choice

Taking all three returns to zero, which is also the starting state. Keep a flag indicating that some item was taken, or start the nonempty table from singletons. Allow intermediate positive and negative balances.

The rule you can reuse

Rewrite a ratio or equal-sum condition as a signed sum of per-item contributions. Offset the range or store sparse maps. If only a congruence matters, replace exact sums by residues and keep the best remaining objective per residue.

∑i∈SAi=k∑i∈SBi⟺∑i∈S(Ai−kBi)=0\sum_{i\in S} A_i=k\sum_{i\in S}B_i\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{i\in S}(A_i-kB_i)=0i∈S∑​Ai​=ki∈S∑​Bi​⟺i∈S∑​(Ai​−kBi​)=0

For signed aᵢ use old[s] → new[s] and new[s+aᵢ]. For balance A/B=k, index the sum of Aᵢ−kBᵢ when the denominator is positive.

Check the boundary. A modular collision certifies equal residue only. For signed sums do not discard a temporary overshoot. Positive and negative shifts go in opposite directions.

Cost O(n(Σ positive − Σ negative +1)) scalar time or O(nm) for modulus m. Normalise negative remainders.
