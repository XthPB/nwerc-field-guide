Turn a reachability cone into coordinate inequalities

Which events at different times and positions can one walker attend at unit speed?

Work through the choice

Going from one event to the next requires both enough time to move right and enough time to move left. Adding and subtracting position from time turns these into two coordinate comparisons. A chain DP can then select events.

The rule you can reuse

An event at time t and position x is reachable from (s,y) on an unobstructed line of unit speed with waiting precisely when t−s≥|x−y|. Writing out both signs gives an ordered-pair dominance test.

t−s≥∣x−y∣  ⟺  t+x≥s+y ∧ t−x≥s−yt-s\ge|x-y|\iff t+x\ge s+y\ \land\ t-x\ge s-yt−s≥∣x−y∣⟺t+x≥s+y ∧ t−x≥s−y

Map (t,x) to (t+x,t−x). Event j precedes i exactly when both transformed coordinates for j are at most those for i. Longest or heaviest chains become dominance DP.

Check the boundary. Aggregate simultaneous colocated rewards if the rules let you collect them together. In the AtCoder extension with monotone y and a time budget |Δx|+Δy, the three keys are y, t−y+x and t−y−x. Verify reachability from the origin as well.

Cost For the two-key form use sorting and a Fenwick maximum in O(n log n). Three keys commonly need offline 3D dominance, for example O(n log² n).

From idea to code

  • Bottom-up segment tree
    The original combine is addition over a half-open interval. For a min, max, witness or matrix payload, update the stored type, combine and empty identity together. Preserve combination order for matrices.
  • Coordinate compression
    Compression preserves order and equality. Convert open and closed query bounds with lower_bound and upper_bound. Original distances must still use the uncompressed coordinates.
