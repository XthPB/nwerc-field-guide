Four directional path tables can meet around a crossing

Two walkers cross a grid along different monotone directions. How can you evaluate the best routes around their meeting?

Work through the choice

Precompute the best route into each cell from each corner. Around an internal meeting, the four arms must use one of two local arrangements of its neighbours. Combine the four compatible table entries and maximize.

The rule you can reuse

Two monotone travellers on a rectangular grid may be required to meet exactly once, with the meeting cell yielding no reward. Their routes approach from opposite pairs of corners. At the meeting they must pass straight through on perpendicular axes, since sharing an incident edge would create another common cell.

Let A,B,C,D be best inclusive path scores from top-left, bottom-right, bottom-left and top-right. For interior (r,c), first try A[r,c−1]+B[r,c+1]+C[r+1,c]+D[r−1,c]. The other layout uses A[r−1,c]+B[r+1,c]+C[r,c−1]+D[r,c+1].

Check the boundary. The four pieces for a fixed layout can be chosen independently because their monotone regions lie in disjoint quadrants around the crossing. If both walkers share start/end or may overlap elsewhere, use synchronized joint positions or a different disjoint-path model.

Cost O(HW) time and O(HW) storage for four tables. With negative gains or walls, mark unreachable cells explicitly before combining.
