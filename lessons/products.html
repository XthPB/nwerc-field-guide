Expand a graph by the small missing state

A route has edges costing 4 and 9. One coupon makes one edge free. How do you find the cheapest trip?

Work through the choice

Two arrivals at the same vertex have different futures if only one still has a coupon. Create one version of every vertex for each coupon status. A paid edge stays in the same layer, while a free edge consumes the coupon.

Build the expanded graph one layer at a time

1 / 3

Paid movement stays in a layer

The horizontal edges have prices 4 then 9 in each row. They preserve the coupon state.

A free movement crosses layers

The diagonal choices cost zero. They consume the coupon and cannot return to the clean layer.

Choose where to cross

Pay 4 on the upper first edge and cross diagonally on the second. The lower-right destination is reached for 4.

The rule you can reuse

If the next edge cost depends on a coupon, the last colour or a partially paid pair, add that information to the vertex. Then examine the expanded transitions to choose DAG DP, BFS, 0–1 BFS or Dijkstra.

D(v,b)=shortest distance to vertex v with coupon state bD(v,b)=\text{shortest distance to vertex }v\text{ with coupon state }bD(v,b)=shortest distance to vertex v with coupon state b

A one-use free edge gives states (v,unused/used). A paid edge stays in the same layer; a free transition goes unused→used with cost zero.

Check the boundary. Acyclicity of the original graph is sufficient but unnecessary. Dijkstra needs nonnegative expanded edge costs. For cycles, “memoize and mark visited” does not solve shortest paths in general.

Cost Two coupon layers have 2V states and O(E) edges, O((V+E)log V) with a heap. A remembered weight can multiply state count by the number of distinct weights.

    First define what constitutes a vertex of the state graph. Construct an edge for each legal transition. All weights used by Dijkstra must be nonnegative.
