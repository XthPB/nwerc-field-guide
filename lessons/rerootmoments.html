Reroot a sum of squared distances

Suppose you can build a station at any vertex of a tree. For a candidate location, each destination contributes the square of its distance from that location. Compute the total for every possible station. Moving the station one edge changes the distances of two groups of destinations.

1. Work one example

Moving the root across a unit edge changes each distance by plus or minus one. Squaring makes the change depend on the old distance too. Keep the total first moment and the old first moment on the side becoming closer. These determine the change in the second moment.

2. What do we need to remember?

Moving a root shifts all distances on each side of an edge by one. Expand the square before aggregating the change.

3. How do we get the next answer?

Q(v)=Q(u)+2S(u)−4Schild side(u)+nQ(v)=Q(u)+2S(u)-4S_{\mathrm{child\ side}}(u)+nQ(v)=Q(u)+2S(u)−4Schild side​(u)+n

Postorder gives subtree sizes and sums of downward distances. For child v, its side's old distance sum as seen from u equals its downward sum plus its size. Reroot the first moment in parallel with the second.

Check the boundary. This displayed update assumes unit edges and one unit of weight per vertex. For weighted edges expand (d±w)² before coding. Squared-distance sums often need a wider integer type.

Cost O(n) time and O(n) storage.

From idea to code

  • Rooted tree DP and rerooting
    Use reverse traversal for bottom-up child messages. Use forward traversal for the parent contribution. Derive a new message when the objective differs from distance or subtree size.
