Make geometry part of the state · Lesson 192 of 235

Two monotone chains replace a visited set

You’re given points with distinct x coordinates. A tour must go from the leftmost point to the rightmost along one chain of increasing x, then return along another, visiting every point once before closing. Find the shortest tour obeying those restrictions.

1. Work one example

Read points from left to right across both chains at once. Every earlier point is already assigned to a chain, so only the two open endpoints matter. Put the next point on either endpoint and eventually join the chains.

2. What do we need to remember?

After sorting points, maintain the endpoints of the two partial chains. Extending a chain must respect the chosen monotone order.

3. How do we get the next answer?

A bitonic tour consists of two chains running from the leftmost to the rightmost point in increasing x. After sorting distinct x coordinates, the visited points form a prefix. Its two open endpoints are the remaining information.

B(j−1,j)=min⁡0≤k<j−1{B(k,j−1)+d(k,j)}B(j-1,j)=\min_{0\le k<j-1}\{B(k,j-1)+d(k,j)\}B(j−1,j)=0≤k<j−1min​{B(k,j−1)+d(k,j)}

For i<j let B[i,j] be the total length of two chains covering 0…j with endpoints i,j and common start 0. B[0,1]=d(0,1). For i<j−1 use B[i,j−1]+d(j−1,j). For adjacent endpoints use B[j−1,j]=minₖ<ⱼ₋₁(B[k,j−1]+d(k,j)).

Check the boundary. When j−1 remains an endpoint, the new point can attach to any earlier open endpoint. Otherwise j must extend the chain from j−1. Crossing-free Euclidean tours in general need not be bitonic, so the monotonicity restriction requires support in the problem.

Cost O(n²) time, O(n²) for a full table and parents, O(n) working space for the cost alone. These formulas assume at least two points.

Try one change

Points are sorted left to right. Why can two endpoints replace a visited-set mask for a bitonic tour?

See the reasoning

The two monotone chains together cover an entire sorted prefix. Only their open endpoints determine how the next point can extend a chain.

  • Exact integer geometry
    Use dot and cross products for structural tests. For route costs use the distance definition required by the problem and a sufficiently wide type. Exact squared distance and Euclidean length may give different optima.
