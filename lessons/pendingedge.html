A pairwise edge price needs a pending first edge

Edges are charged in pairs by squaring the sum of their weights. What should you remember halfway through a pair?

Work through the choice

A pending 1 followed by 2 costs 9, whereas a pending 5 followed by 2 costs 49. The current vertex alone hides this future difference. Use a separate state for the pending weight and clear it when you pay.

The rule you can reuse

If two consecutive edges cost a joint function of their weights, the graph vertex alone loses necessary history. Enter a pending state with the first edge weight, charge the pair when the next edge is traversed, then return to the clean layer.

(u,∅)→0(v,a)→(a+b)2(w,∅)(u,\varnothing)\xrightarrow{0}(v,a)\xrightarrow{(a+b)^2}(w,\varnothing)(u,∅)0​(v,a)(a+b)2​(w,∅)

From (u,clean) follow edge weight a to (v,a) at zero cost. From (v,a) follow weight b to (w,clean) at cost (a+b)². Only clean arrivals answer an even-edge route.

Check the boundary. The expanded graph contains cycles. Its edge weights are nonnegative in this model, so use Dijkstra. If you change the cost law or allow finishing halfway through a pair, update both the solver assumptions and acceptable terminal states.
