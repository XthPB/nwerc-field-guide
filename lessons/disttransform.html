‹ Geometry inside a DP

Absolute distance can turn all predecessors into two sweeps

At every point i on a line, buy from the source minimizing its starting price plus travel distance to i.

Work through the choice

Split possible suppliers into those on the left and on the right. Moving one unit makes every supplier behind you one unit more expensive. Two sweeps carry the cheapest delivered price from each direction.

The rule you can reuse

Suppose position i may inherit from any j at cost f[j]+c|xᵢ−xⱼ| for nonnegative c. Sort the coordinates. Left and right candidates have opposite fixed slopes. Each direction therefore needs just one running minimum.

g(x)=min⁡ ⁣(cx+min⁡y≤x(f(y)−cy), −cx+min⁡y≥x(f(y)+cy))g(x)=\min\!\left(cx+\min_{y\le x}(f(y)-cy),\ -cx+\min_{y\ge x}(f(y)+cy)\right)g(x)=min(cx+y≤xmin​(f(y)−cy), −cx+y≥xmin​(f(y)+cy))

Left-to-right maintain min(f[j]−c·xⱼ) over j≤i and add c·xᵢ. Right-to-left maintain min(f[j]+c·xⱼ) over j≥i and subtract c·xᵢ. Take the smaller answer.

Check the boundary. On a complete rectangular Manhattan grid with additive per-axis cost, transform each row, then each column. Obstructed routes, squared distance, or location-dependent movement can require a different envelope or shortest-path algorithm.

Cost O(n) per sorted 1D layer and O(HW) per dense 2D Manhattan layer. An initial sort costs O(n log n).
