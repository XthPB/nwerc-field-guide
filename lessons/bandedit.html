Track more than the last character · Lesson 137 of 235

Restrict edit distance to a diagonal band when only k edits matter

Given two strings and a limit k, decide whether at most k single-character insertions, deletions or substitutions can turn one into the other. For kitten and sitten let k=1. The strings in the larger version may be long even when the edit budget is small.

1. Work one example

Along any alignment costing at most k unit edits, the consumed prefix lengths can never differ by more than k. Each unit of imbalance would require an insertion or deletion. Compute only cells within that band.

2. What do we need to remember?

If at most k edits are allowed, a successful alignment cannot wander arbitrarily far from the main diagonal.

3. How do we get the next answer?

D(i,j)>kwhenever∣i−j∣>kD(i,j)>k\quad\text{whenever}\quad\lvert i-j\rvert>kD(i,j)>kwhenever∣i−j∣>k

Initialize outside-band cells to k+1, cap larger costs at k+1, and run the ordinary three edit transitions on the band. Use separate rows or reset obsolete slots when the band shifts.

Check the boundary. If the true distance exceeds k, the banded computation only needs to report that fact. Zero-cost insertions or deletions invalidate the width argument.

Cost O((n+1)(2k+1)) time and O(k) rolling space with band offsets.

Try one change

Two prefixes have lengths 12 and 8. Can an alignment of those prefixes use at most three single-character insertions, deletions or substitutions?

See the reasoning

No. Their length difference alone requires at least four insertions or deletions, so the cell lies outside a width-three edit band.
