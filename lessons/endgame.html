Two ends, score difference, and forced turns

The numbers 8, 15, 3 and 7 lie in a row. Two players take turns removing either end and adding it to their own score until the row is empty. Both play optimally. How many points can the first player guarantee?

1. Work one example

Write the remaining position from the next player's perspective. Taking an end gains its value now, then gives the opponent their best advantage on the smaller interval. Subtract that advantage when comparing your two moves.

2. What do we need to remember?

Store the score advantage of whoever moves next. Taking an endpoint gives its value minus the opponent’s remaining advantage.

3. How do we get the next answer?

If players alternate and the total payoff is fixed, store current player’s advantage. Taking an endpoint swaps who optimizes on the remaining interval. This removes a turn dimension.

D(l,r)=max⁡{al−D(l+1,r), ar−D(l,r−1)}D(l,r)=\max\{a_l-D(l+1,r),\,a_r-D(l,r-1)\}D(l,r)=max{al​−D(l+1,r),ar​−D(l,r−1)}

D[l,r]=max(a[l]−D[l+1,r], a[r]−D[l,r−1]); D[empty]=0. First player score = (sum+D[0,n−1])/2.

Check the boundary. This sign flip requires a zero-sum advantage and alternating optimal play. Extra turns, scores depending on player identity, or stochastic opponents change the transition.

Cost O(n²) time and memory, or O(n) for the value by keeping neighbouring diagonals.

Try one change

If taking 7 hands the opponent an advantage of 3 in the remainder, what is your final advantage from that move?

See the reasoning

Four. Your immediate 7 is offset by the opponent's 3. This is why the recurrence subtracts the next player's value.
