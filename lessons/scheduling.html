Sort by finish and jump to the last compatible predecessor

Three jobs pay 5, 8 and 6. Their time intervals are [0,3), [1,4) and [3,5). Which compatible jobs pay the most?

1. Work one example

Sort by finish time. If you take the third job, look back only as far as jobs that finished by time 3. The best of those pays 5, so that branch earns 11. Compare it with skipping the third job.

2. What do we need to remember?

Sort jobs by finish time. If the last job is used, the previous job must already finish before it starts.

3. How do we get the next answer?

For intervals with value, consider the last job in a finish-sorted prefix. Skipping removes one job. Taking it makes every earlier job after its start irrelevant.

D(i)=max⁡{D(i−1), vi+D(p(i))}D(i)=\max\{D(i-1),\,v_i+D(p(i))\}D(i)=max{D(i−1),vi​+D(p(i))}

Let p(i) be the last earlier job with finish ≤ startᵢ for half-open intervals. best[i]=max(best[i−1],valueᵢ+best[p(i)]).

Check the boundary. If touching endpoints conflict, change ≤ to <. If job compatibility depends on resource type, p(i) can become a set of predecessor states.

Cost O(n log n) including sorting and binary search. A time-indexed scan is an alternative on a small integer timeline.

Try one change

Job B starts at 7. Earlier jobs finish at 4, 7 and 9. Which prefix can precede B when touching endpoints are allowed?

Use the best schedule through the job finishing at 7. Then compare taking B with the best schedule that skips it.
