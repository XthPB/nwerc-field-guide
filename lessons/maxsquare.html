Walk across a grid · Lesson 24 of 235

Find the largest all-filled square by its bottom-right corner

A binary image is a grid of zeros and ones. Find the greatest side length of an axis-aligned square made entirely of ones. A single zero inside a candidate square invalidates the whole square, even if its corners are ones.

1. Work one example

If a cell is zero no filled square can end there. At a one cell, ask how large the best squares immediately above, left and diagonally above-left were. The smallest of those three sizes limits the extension by one.

2. What do we need to remember?

Ask for the largest all-good square ending at this corner. To grow it, its three neighboring corners must all support the smaller square.

3. How do we get the next answer?

s[r,c]={0a[r,c]=01+min⁡(s[r−1,c],s[r,c−1],s[r−1,c−1])a[r,c]=1s[r,c]=\begin{cases}0&a[r,c]=0\\1+\min(s[r-1,c],s[r,c-1],s[r-1,c-1])&a[r,c]=1\end{cases}s[r,c]={01+min(s[r−1,c],s[r,c−1],s[r−1,c−1])​a[r,c]=0a[r,c]=1​

Track the maximum side as you sweep. If the question counts all filled squares, add s[r,c] over all cells since every smaller positive side ending here also works.

Check the boundary. A rectangle can have unrelated height and width. For a largest all-filled rectangle build a histogram per row and use nearest-smaller boundaries.

Cost O(HW) time and O(W) memory.

Try one change

The upper, left and diagonal corners support squares of sizes 3, 2 and 4. The current cell is filled. What size can end here?

See the reasoning

Three. The smallest neighbor supports size 2, and the new corner extends it by one. All three neighbors must support the same inner size.
