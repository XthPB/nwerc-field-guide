Exact nearest-point distances turn into clauses on adjacent surviving sites

A clue says the nearest treasure is exactly two sites away. How do you turn an exact distance into conditions on individual sites?

Work through the choice

Forbid every site strictly closer than two. At least one site exactly two away must contain treasure. Once all forbidden sites are removed, the surviving endpoints of each two-sided clue are neighbours in the remaining order. A two-state scan can enforce that local condition.

The rule you can reuse

An observed distance d at location i forbids every site strictly nearer than d and requires at least one site exactly d away. On a line there are at most two such sites. Mark forbidden intervals globally with a difference array, then resolve each clue against the sites that survive.

With no legal boundary site the clues contradict. One site forces its bit to 1. Two give a clause xₗ OR xᵣ. The sites are consecutive in the surviving order because this clue itself forbids every site between them. Count binary assignments by last bit, disallowing 00 across constrained edges.

Check the boundary. For d=0 mark an empty interior and force the single boundary site i. Out-of-board endpoints cannot be selected. With all distances unknown subtract the all-zero assignment because at least one treasure is required.

Cost O(n) time and space for the difference array, surviving ranks and two-state path DP. CF 2259H, rated 2200.
