Jump to the n-th term of a short linear recurrence

Find the trillionth term when a sequence obeys a short fixed linear recurrence.

1. Work one example

The recurrence expresses a high power of a shift using smaller powers. Exponentiate the shift polynomial, reducing after each multiplication. The resulting coefficients tell you which initial terms to combine.

2. What do we need to remember?

If distant terms obey a fixed linear rule, reduce large powers modulo the polynomial that represents that rule.

3. How do we get the next answer?

For a scalar order-k constant-coefficient recurrence, xᵏ is equivalent to its characteristic polynomial remainder. Multiply polynomials and reduce so high powers reuse known coefficients. If a recurrence is unknown, a proved dimension bound can justify learning it over a field.

Check the boundary. Berlekamp–Massey over a field recovers a minimal recurrence from enough exact terms (2k suffice with a known bound k). Fitting a short prefix without a bound is conjectural. Ordinary polynomial reduction works for a given monic recurrence over a ring.

Cost O(k² log n) with naive polynomial multiplication/reduction. For large k, faster polynomial methods such as Bostan–Mori may help.
