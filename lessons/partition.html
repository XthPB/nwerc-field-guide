‹ Interval and partition DP

Cut the last piece · Lesson 45 of 235

Exactly k consecutive pieces

Cut 1,2,1 into exactly two nonempty consecutive groups. Minimize the sum of squared group totals.

1. Work one example

Guess the last cut. Everything after it is the final group and its cost comes from two prefix sums. Everything before it must already have used exactly one fewer group.

2. What do we need to remember?

Pretend you already know the last cut. The prefix before it has an optimal answer you can reuse.

3. How do we get the next answer?

Here one side of the cut is a completed prefix and the other is a single new segment. This is a layered prefix DP. The split has a different role from the two recursive children in interval merging.

D(g,i)=min⁡g−1≤j<i{D(g−1,j)+C(j,i)}D(g,i)=\min_{g-1\le j<i}\{D(g-1,j)+C(j,i)\}D(g,i)=g−1≤j<imin​{D(g−1,j)+C(j,i)}

D[g,i]=min over g−1≤j<i of (D[g−1,j]+C(j,i)), where C(j,i) costs items j+1…i. D[0,0]=0; other zero-group prefixes are unreachable.

Check the boundary. Nonempty groups force j<i and i≥g. If the optimum split index reverses direction on a tiny test, divide-and-conquer optimization is invalid for that cost.

Cost O(Kn²) time and O(n) per layer. Monotone split, range updates, convex hull, and a penalty on group count are separate possible improvements.

Try one change

You need exactly three pieces ending at position 8. If the final piece starts at 6, which answer do you reuse?

See the reasoning

The best split of positions 1 through 5 into exactly two pieces. Add the cost of positions 6 through 8, then try other final starts.

From idea to code

  • Divide-and-conquer partition DP
    Use the recursion once optimal cuts are proven monotone. For exactly k nonempty groups, compute only i ≥ k and permit j ≥ k−1. Use the reachable prefix range on each layer.
  • Prefix sums and difference arrays
    A prefix answers a range sum by subtraction. A difference array reverses the operation by marking the two ends of an interval update. The sheet includes the two-dimensional inclusion–exclusion form.
