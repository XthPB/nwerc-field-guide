Measure a random process · Lesson 123 of 235

Track only the moments a nonlinear reward reads

A reward involves the square of a random run length. Is its mean length enough to calculate the reward?

1. Work one example

Two distributions can share the same mean and have different spreads. Expand the reward after the next success. Squaring a length increased by one requires its old square, its old value and a constant, so carry those moments.

2. What do we need to remember?

For variance you also need a second moment. Square the one-step reward plus the future reward before taking expectations.

3. How do we get the next answer?

A reward involving a square of a run or a repeated random operation may need more than E[X]. Expand the update symbolically. If the resulting formulas use only a small set of moments, carry those moments instead of every value.

E[(X′)2]=p(E[X2]+2E[X]+1)\mathbb E[(X')^2]=p\bigl(\mathbb E[X^2]+2\mathbb E[X]+1\bigr)E[(X′)2]=p(E[X2]+2E[X]+1)

If X gains one on success of independent probability p and resets otherwise, E[X′]=p(E[X]+1), E[X′²]=p(E[X²]+2E[X]+1).

Check the boundary. E[X²] is generally unequal to E[X]². Similarly E[max(X,Y)] needs more information than the two means. If p depends on X, condition on X or track the necessary joint statistic.

Cost O(n) for a constant number of moments. For d moments, expansion can cost O(d²) per step.

Try one change

A score is 0 or 2 with equal probability. Is its expected square equal to its expected score squared?

See the reasoning

The expected square is 2. The expected score is 1, whose square is 1. Their difference gives variance 1.
