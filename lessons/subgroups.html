Choose a set and its endpoint · Lesson 81 of 235

Partition a set into compatible groups

Seat four guests in as few compatible groups as possible. Guests a and b refuse to sit together.

1. Work one example

Pick the group containing a and solve recursively for everyone outside it. Every partition has exactly one such group. Testing just groups containing that anchor also prevents accidentally counting different orders of the same tables.

2. What do we need to remember?

Choose one whole group from the remaining mask. Requiring its smallest member to be present gives each unordered partition one construction.

3. How do we get the next answer?

Precompute valid[S] or the reward for one group. Remove a canonical block and recurse on the remainder. For unlabeled partitions force the lowest remaining member into the next group.

Check the boundary. Enumerate S=(S−1)&M to visit submasks. After processing zero, stop explicitly if zero is included. A sum-over-subsets transform does not compute arbitrary cost[S]+F[M without S].

Cost O(3ⁿ) mask/submask pairs since each bit is outside M, inside S, or in M without S. O(2ⁿ) DP space.
