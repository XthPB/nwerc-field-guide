Walk across a grid · Lesson 28 of 235

Keep an entire diagonal of equally good prefixes

Cross a letter grid by moving right or down, spelling one letter per cell. How do you find the smallest possible word without keeping a long string at every cell?

1. Work one example

Every route on one diagonal has spelled the same number of letters. Among routes with the smallest prefix so far, examine all next cells. Append the smallest available letter and keep every position spelling that letter, because their future suffixes can differ.

2. What do we need to remember?

Among paths with the best prefix string so far, keep all endpoints that could extend it by the smallest next letter.

3. How do we get the next answer?

Ft+1={v:∃u∈Ft, u→v, av=min⁡u′∈Ft, u′→waw}F_{t+1}=\{v:\exists u\in F_t,\ u\to v,\ a_v=\min_{u'\in F_t,\ u'\to w}a_w\}Ft+1​={v:∃u∈Ft​, u→v, av​=u′∈Ft​, u′→wmin​aw​}

Deduplicate cells while collecting the next frontier. Output one letter per layer. If some legal moves lead to dead ends, first restrict the frontier to cells that can reach the target.

Check the boundary. Picking any one of two equal next letters can throw away the best suffix. A set of frontier cells is enough to represent all optimal prefixes of that length.

Cost O(HW) work with per-layer deduplication and O(min(H,W)) active cells on a rectangle.

Try one change

Two frontier cells spell the same best prefix. Why keep both?

See the reasoning

One might offer a smaller next letter. Look at every legal neighbor of both cells, keep the smallest letter, and keep every endpoint achieving it.
