‹ Digit DP, binary bounds and carries

Follow tight bounds digit by digit · Lesson 110 of 235

Change direction for arithmetic carries

Choose an ordered pair (a,b) of nonnegative two-bit integers, so each lies between 0 and 3. Count pairs with ordinary sum equal to binary 10, which is decimal 2. Align their bits by position just as in written addition.

1. Work one example

Read columns from the units side, where incoming carry is known. Choose the two input bits, check their sum bit against the target, and pass the carry to the next column. At the end check any leftover carry.

2. What do we need to remember?

Read columns in the direction a carry travels. Keep the carry and only the extra constraints that the next column can still observe.

3. How do we get the next answer?

Constraints such as A+B=C couple digits locally from least to most significant. Store incoming carry or borrow. If upper bounds are essential, compare from the low side with a relation that more significant unequal digits overwrite, or split counting carefully.

t=ai+bi+ci,bout=t mod 2,ci+1=⌊t/2⌋t=a_i+b_i+c_i,\qquad b_{\rm out}=t\bmod 2,\quad c_{i+1}=\lfloor t/2\rfloort=ai​+bi​+ci​,bout​=tmod2,ci+1​=⌊t/2⌋

For binary addition t=aᵢ+bᵢ+carry; emit t mod 2 and advance with floor(t/2). Start carry=0 and check the final carry.

Check the boundary. A left-to-right tight flag cannot be transplanted unchanged into a low-to-high scan. Use enough digits for the final carry and distinguish fixed-width bit strings from ordinary numbers.

Cost O(bits × carry states × local choices × comparison states), often linear in bit length when the number of operands is fixed.
