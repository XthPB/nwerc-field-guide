Turn costs into lines or slopes · Lesson 169 of 235

Turn each predecessor into a line

Each landing at height h pays a fixed fee plus the square of its height change. How can you find the best earlier jump?

1. Work one example

Expand the square. The term involving only the new height is shared by all candidates. Every earlier height now gives a straight line evaluated at the new height. Maintaining the lower envelope replaces trying all earlier heights.

2. What do we need to remember?

Expand the cost until a previous choice contributes a slope and intercept. The new state provides the coordinate at which to compare the lines.

3. How do we get the next answer?

Expand C(j,i) algebraically. If every j contributes mⱼxᵢ+bⱼ, query an envelope of lines. With monotone inserted slopes and monotone query x in a compatible direction, a deque processes the envelope in linear total time.

Di=hi2+c+min⁡j<i((−2hj)hi+Dj+hj2)D_i=h_i^2+c+\min_{j<i}\bigl((-2h_j)h_i+D_j+h_j^2\bigr)Di​=hi2​+c+j<imin​((−2hj​)hi​+Dj​+hj2​)

D[i]=hᵢ²+c+minⱼ((-2hⱼ)hᵢ+D[j]+hⱼ²) for quadratic jump cost. Insert j only after D[j] is known.

Check the boundary. Equal slopes keep the better intercept. Use a widened integer before products and subtractions. With monotone slopes and arbitrary x, binary search the hull; with arbitrary slopes consider Li Chao.

Cost O(n) amortized for monotone insertions and queries; O(n log n) with binary-search queries on an ordered hull.

Try one change

A predecessor contributes 3x+2. At x=4 how much does it offer, and why represent it as a line?

See the reasoning

It offers 14. Its slope and intercept stay fixed for future x, so querying an envelope can compare many such predecessors at once.
