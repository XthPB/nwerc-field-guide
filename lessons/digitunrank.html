‹ Digit DP, binary bounds and carries

Follow tight bounds digit by digit · Lesson 105 of 235

Construct the k-th number satisfying a digit rule

Find the fourth positive integer up to 30 with strictly increasing decimal digits. Can you skip whole groups of candidates?

1. Work one example

Use suffix counts to answer how many legal completions follow a proposed digit. For numerical order, compare equal-width padded representations or process lengths in increasing order. Skip an entire branch when k is larger than its count.

2. What do we need to remember?

Count valid completions under each possible next digit. Their block sizes tell you where the requested rank lies.

3. How do we get the next answer?

k←k−c if k>c,otherwise descend into the branchk\gets k-c\ \text{if }k>c,\qquad\text{otherwise descend into the branch}k←k−c if k>c,otherwise descend into the branch

Memoize counts by position, previous real digit, started and bound flags. Cap nonnegative sums at the requested rank to prevent overflow. Reject a rank larger than the total number of valid completions.

Check the boundary. Counts modulo a prime lose order information. Ranks in this example start at one.

Cost One count DP plus O(10·digits) choices to reconstruct one number.
