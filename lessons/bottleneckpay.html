Use a second algorithm inside DP · Lesson 210 of 235

A threshold leaves an additive budget and a sliding predecessor interval

You may mark positions in an array of nonnegative costs as blocked. You pay the sum of all blocked costs. The unblocked positions form contiguous gaps, each with its own sum. Minimize the largest of your payment and all gap sums.

1. Work one example

Guess an upper limit. Nonnegative weights make the legal preceding blocked positions a sliding interval. A DP minimizes blocked cost for that fixed limit, then a monotone search finds the smallest feasible limit.

2. What do we need to remember?

Fix a trial cap for both kinds of cost. Each remaining gap must obey that cap and the selected positions must still fit an additive budget.

3. How do we get the next answer?

A score may be the maximum of two very different costs, such as total weight of blocked positions and largest sum of any unblocked gap. Guess a threshold X. The gap condition now restricts legal predecessors, while the sum of blocked weights becomes a minimization DP.

With positive a and prefix sums P, let d[i] be minimum blocked cost when i is blocked last. Then d[i]=aᵢ+min d[j] over j<i with P[i−1]−P[j]≤X. Add zero-cost blocked sentinels at 0 and n+1 and accept iff d[n+1]≤X.

Check the boundary. Testing only the longest gap ignores the total cost of the blocked positions. Positivity makes the left boundary move forward as i grows. Signed inputs may need a different range query even if the outer yes/no predicate is still monotone.

Cost Each threshold takes O(n) via a monotone deque of d[j]. Binary search over integer X takes O(n log(Σa+1)) time and O(n) storage.

Try one change

At a trial cap all individual gaps fit. Does that alone certify the route when paid checkpoints also have a total budget?

See the reasoning

No. Among paths respecting the gap cap, compute the least total checkpoint payment and compare it with the allowed budget.
