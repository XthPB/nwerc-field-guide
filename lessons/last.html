One decision at a time · Lesson 2 of 235

A fixed number of recent decisions

You can collect coins from houses worth 4, 5 and 4, but adjacent houses have an alarm. What is the most you can collect?

1. Work one example

Decide what happens at the last house. Skipping keeps the previous optimum. Taking it adds its value to the optimum two houses back, because its neighbour must be skipped.

2. What do we need to remember?

At the end of the prefix, keep the last choice that changes what you may do next.

3. How do we get the next answer?

When a choice depends only on the last symbol, action, or a cooldown, turn that information into a small state machine. Draw every legal arrow. Prefix index gives a topological order even if the machine has cycles.

Di(q′)=max⁡q→q′{Di−1(q)+wi(q,q′)}D_i(q')=\max_{q\to q'}\{D_{i-1}(q)+w_i(q,q')\}Di​(q′)=q→q′max​{Di−1​(q)+wi​(q,q′)}

dᵢ[q′] = max over transitions q→q′ of (dᵢ₋₁[q]+reward(i,q,q′)).

Check the boundary. The scalar shortcut best[i]=max(best[i−1],best[i−2]+aᵢ) assumes the only conflict is the adjacent index. A two-day cooldown changes the predecessor.

Cost O(nE) for E machine transitions. A constant-size automaton often needs only two short rows.
