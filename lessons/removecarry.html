‹ Interval and partition DP

Split, merge, or temporarily omit · Lesson 199 of 235

Keep the group that will merge with an interval boundary

A row initially reads a,b,a,a. A move removes a contiguous block of equal colors and pays the square of the number removed. The remaining blocks slide together. Choose moves that maximize the total score, possibly saving matching colors for a later, larger removal.

1. Work one example

If you remove b first, all three a entries meet and earn nine together. An interval's best answer changes when equal neighbours are already waiting just outside it. Add a third coordinate for how many equal entries are attached to its boundary.

2. What do we need to remember?

An interval may still touch an equal-colored group outside it. Remember the size of that group until scoring their joint removal.

3. How do we get the next answer?

Let k count extra copies of a[r] attached on the right. Either remove the right group now, or clear an interior interval so its final colour joins an earlier equal value. Empty intervals score zero.

Check the boundary. For an arbitrary payoff by group length, splitting a uniform run may pay better. Precompute the best reward for partitioning such a run before using a recurrence that always deletes it as one.

Cost O(n³) states and O(n⁴) straightforward time, with memoization often visiting fewer states.

Try one change

Two red blocks are separated by other colors. Why remember how many red blocks are already attached to the boundary?

See the reasoning

Clearing the middle can merge the red blocks. If the score is nonlinear in group size, the value of deleting the boundary depends on the attached count.
