One decision at a time · Lesson 4 of 235

Find a quantity that always moves forward

You’re given a road map. Each road goes one way, and following the arrows can never take you back to a city you’ve left. You want the cheapest trip from A to C. The direct road costs 8. The road from A to B costs 4, and the road from B to C gives you a rebate of 3. Add the fares along the trip, counting the rebate as −3.

1. Work one example

If you stop when you first see C from A, you get 8. But B also has a road into C. Visiting B first lets you compare both ways into C and keep the cheaper one.

2. What do we need to remember?

Write D[v] for the cheapest fare from A to vertex v. Start with D[A] = 0. Give every other vertex a temporary value of ∞, meaning you have yet to find a route to it.

3. How do we get the next answer?

Every trip into C has a last road. If that road is B → C, its best possible price is the cheapest fare to B plus the price of B → C. Repeat this comparison for every incoming road. You can do that once per vertex if all its incoming roads start earlier in your order. Such an order is called a topological order. It exists because the graph has no directed cycle.

D(v)=min⁡u→v{D(u)+w(u,v)}D(v)=\min_{u\to v}\{D(u)+w(u,v)\}D(v)=u→vmin​{D(u)+w(u,v)}

Here u is the previous vertex and w(u,v) is the fare on its road to v. At C the two candidates are 0 + 8 and 4 + (−3). The smaller is 1.

In another DP, the quantity that moves forward might be the length of a prefix, the number of items chosen, or the time elapsed. Find an order in which every answer reads only finished answers.

Check the boundary. A zero-length transition can keep the same rank. Topologically order those transitions, solve simultaneous equations, or switch to a graph algorithm as appropriate.

Cost O(V+E) for an explicit DAG. Acyclicity of the expanded state graph is what matters.

Try one change

An edge can go from vertex 9 to vertex 2. Can you fill the table in vertex-number order?

See the reasoning

Only if the recurrence does not depend on that edge. For a DAG, place a vertex after all predecessors it reads. Arbitrary vertex numbers give no such guarantee.
