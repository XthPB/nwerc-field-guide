‹ Combining and compressing techniques

Separate frequent and rare transition sizes

Many positions send their DP value to later positions in jumps of a fixed length. How can you avoid updating every multiple every time?

Work through the choice

For short jump sizes cache the total already promised to each residue class. For long jumps, walk through the few actual destinations. Choose a cutoff by balancing the number of residue lookups and explicit updates.

The rule you can reuse

When a transition uses a step k repeatedly, small k can be batched by residue and large k gives few reachable multiples. Choose B by balancing the two costs. Write both cost bounds before adopting √n.

T(B)=O ⁣(nB+n2B),B≈nT(B)=O\!\left(nB+\frac{n^2}{B}\right),\qquad B\approx\sqrt nT(B)=O(nB+Bn2​),B≈n​

For propagation from i to i+k,i+2k,… with k=a[i], keep pending contributions by (k,i mod k) for k≤B as a left-to-right sweep runs. For larger k, explicitly visit at most n/B destinations.

Check the boundary. This aggregation requires contributions from previous positions to persist uniformly at each later position in the same residue class. If there is a cutoff, schedule a corresponding removal.

Cost O(nB+n²/B) time and O(B²+n) space, choosing B≈√n. The bound is on all transitions, not a single step.

  • Prefix sums and difference arrays
    A prefix answers a range sum by subtraction. A difference array reverses the operation by marking the two ends of an interval update. The sheet includes the two-dimensional inclusion–exclusion form.
