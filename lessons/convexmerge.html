Turn costs into lines or slopes · Lesson 174 of 235

Represent a convex tree DP by its sorted marginal costs

Each child of a tree has a cost for selecting 0, 1 or 2 units. How can you merge their costs when each extra unit costs at least as much as the previous one?

1. Work one example

Write each child's list of marginal costs. To buy k units, take the cheapest k marginal steps across both children. Because each list is sorted, earlier steps of a child can be taken before later ones. Then account for the parent's own penalty.

2. What do we need to remember?

If consecutive units become steadily more expensive, merge the children’s marginal costs in order to allocate the parent’s budget.

3. How do we get the next answer?

If taking k identical units in a child costs F(k) with nondecreasing first differences, distributing the parent budget across children is a min-plus convolution of discrete convex functions. Its slopes are the sorted multiset union of child slopes.

ΔF(k)=F(k)−F(k−1),Δ(F⋆min⁡,+G)=sortedMerge(ΔF,ΔG)\Delta F(k)=F(k)-F(k-1),\qquad \Delta(F\star_{\min,+}G)=\mathrm{sortedMerge}(\Delta F,\Delta G)ΔF(k)=F(k)−F(k−1),Δ(F⋆min,+​G)=sortedMerge(ΔF,ΔG)

Store F(0) and increments Δₖ=F(k)−F(k−1). The cheapest k increments in the union can be taken as prefixes in each child. A free ±1 vertex adds one zero marginal before its local penalty.

Check the boundary. Add |L| to the base and |L+2k|−|L+2k−2| to slope k. This increment is −2, possibly 0 at a crossing, then +2. Split the ordered multiset by rank, apply lazy shifts, rejoin. The minimum at the root is its base plus all negative slopes.

Cost Reinserting all slopes of the smaller bag into the larger and using a balanced order-statistic tree gives O(n log² n) with logarithmic rank splits/joins. The argument requires convex child curves. CF 2258F, rated 3000.

Try one change

One child has successive extra costs 2,7 and another has 3,5. Which two units are cheapest together?

See the reasoning

Take 2 and 3. Each child's marginal costs are nondecreasing, so the globally cheapest increments can be chosen in each child's required prefix order.
