Trade a prescribed count for a penalty

A cheap optimizer chooses its own number of groups, but the judge demands exactly two. Can a price per group steer it?

Work through the choice

Add a penalty for each group. Larger penalties favour fewer groups. Track the chosen count through ties and prove the exact-count cost has the required convexity. Subtract the penalty contribution to recover the unpenalized value.

The rule you can reuse

Let f(k) be the minimum original cost with exactly k groups. Adding λ per group removes k from the state if the unrestricted penalized DP is easy. As λ rises, a minimizing count cannot rise. Recovering an exact count additionally needs a supporting line at that k.

g(λ)=min⁡t{f(t)+λt},f(k)=g(λ)−λk(k supported)g(\lambda)=\min_t\{f(t)+\lambda t\},\quad f(k)=g(\lambda)-\lambda k\quad(k\text{ supported})g(λ)=tmin​{f(t)+λt},f(k)=g(λ)−λk(k supported)

g(λ)=minₜ(f(t)+λt). If k minimizes at λ, f(k)=g(λ)−λk. Discrete convexity (nondecreasing f(t+1)−f(t)) ensures support for all interior feasible counts on a consecutive domain.

Check the boundary. Carry (penalized cost,chosen count) and resolve ties toward more or fewer as required. When opt jumps over k, verify both tie boundaries or a proved interpolation rule. Integer λ is sufficient for integer discrete-convex costs on consecutive counts; other settings may need rationals.

Cost O(T log penaltyRange) for an integer search with DP cost T. Prove convexity or an equivalent exchange property for the particular problem.
