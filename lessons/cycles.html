‹ Probability, expectations and games

Several mutually dependent expectations

From A you always move to B. From B a fair coin sends you home or back to A. What is the expected number of moves until home?

Work through the choice

One unknown per state gives two equations. Substitute the expression for B into the one for A, or solve the linear system when many states interact. Verify that the process can reach absorption with finite mean.

The rule you can reuse

When multiple states refer to each other with no ranking, assemble the linear system on transient states. Exploit structure first: a line often gives a tridiagonal system, and all resets to one hub may share one unknown.

(I−Q)E=c(I-Q)E=c(I−Q)E=c

(I−Q)E=c. With one reset hub H and an otherwise acyclic graph, write E[s]=aₛ+bₛH and solve H=a_H/(1−b_H) when the denominator is nonzero.

Check the boundary. Numerical stability matters near an absorbing probability of zero. An optimal decision inside the process introduces min/max and can make the equation piecewise linear.

Cost Dense elimination O(s³); a nonsingular tridiagonal system may take O(s) with suitable elimination. One-hub affine propagation costs one DAG pass.

From idea to code

  • Gaussian elimination
    Put one row per expectation equation and one column per unknown state. Handle absorbing states separately. A tridiagonal row system admits a cheaper specialized solver.
