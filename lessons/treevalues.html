‹ Dynamic programming on trees

Collapse an interval of allowed labels to its endpoints

Each vertex may pick a value from an interval. Maximize the sum of absolute differences across tree edges.

Work through the choice

Hold all neighbouring values fixed. As you slide one value within its interval, the sum of its absolute distances is convex. At least one endpoint is optimal. Then a child needs to report only two alternatives to its parent.

The rule you can reuse

A vertex may choose any integer in [L,R]. When maximizing a sum of absolute differences on edges, fix all neighbours first. The contribution as a function of this one label is convex, so an endpoint attains its maximum. Fix endpoints one vertex at a time while preserving an optimum.

Du(q)=∑v∈child(u) max⁡p∈{Lv,Rv}{Dv(p)+∣q−p∣}D_u(q)=\sum_{v\in\mathrm{child}(u)}\ \max_{p\in\{L_v,R_v\}}\{D_v(p)+\lvert q-p\rvert\}Du​(q)=v∈child(u)∑​ p∈{Lv​,Rv​}max​{Dv​(p)+∣q−p∣}

For q∈{Lᵤ,Rᵤ}, let Dᵤ(q)=Σ over children v of max over p∈{Lᵥ,Rᵥ} of Dᵥ(p)+|q−p|. Leaf values are zero.

Check the boundary. Minimizing the same sum has different geometry. Neighbours fixed at 3 and 5 give an interior optimum on [0,10]. Convexity justifies the endpoint reduction for the maximum.

Cost O(n) time and space, with two local states and four comparisons per edge up to constants.
