Add one more coordinate · Lesson 21 of 235

Saturate, sparsify or pack a large product of dimensions

Each bundle contains a known number of red and blue tokens. You may take each bundle once. What is the fewest bundles that give you at least 3 red and 2 blue tokens? One available bundle contains 9 red and 1 blue, so going over a target is possible.

1. Work one example

A bundle of 9 red and 1 blue leaves the same remaining task as 3 red and 1 blue. Cap each coordinate at its target while all later operations only add tokens. This shrinks the table without changing any future decision.

2. What do we need to remember?

Past a threshold, combine larger values only after showing that every future decision treats them alike.

3. How do we get the next answer?

When future transitions only ask whether a monotone counter has reached a threshold, replace larger values by that threshold. For unmatched dimensions, generate just reachable tuples or retain a Pareto frontier with a proved dominance order.

(x,y)⟼(min⁡(A,x+u), min⁡(B,y+v))(x,y)\longmapsto\bigl(\min(A,x+u),\,\min(B,y+v)\bigr)(x,y)⟼(min(A,x+u),min(B,y+v))

For nonnegative increments and a goal of at least A and at least B, (x,y) goes to (min(A,x+u),min(B,y+v)). For a minimum number of one-use items initialize d[0,0]=0 and other states to ∞ in the previous layer.

Check the boundary. With exact totals, saturation can produce false positives. With identically distributed objects, merge by type counts only when each future transition has the same total multiplicity from every member of a merged class.

Cost Two capped axes cost O(n(A+1)(B+1)). A sparse map costs proportional to visited states and generated moves but can become dense. For four 100-valued independent coordinates even one 32-bit layer needs about 381 MiB for 100⁴ cells.

Try one change

The goal asks for at least 5 points. Is it safe to store every larger total as 5?

See the reasoning

If future increments are nonnegative and only crossing 5 matters, yes. If you might later spend 2 points, totals 5 and 7 can lead to different outcomes.
