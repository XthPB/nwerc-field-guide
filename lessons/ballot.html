‹ Grid and lattice paths, tilings and frontiers

Count lattice paths that stay below a diagonal

A walk uses three right steps and three up steps. How many walks never have more up steps than right steps?

Work through the choice

Start with all 20 orders of the six steps. For each bad walk, reflect its prefix through the first illegal step by swapping right and up. That changes its totals to four right and two up. The first time the transformed path has one extra right step tells you exactly what to reflect back.

The rule you can reuse

Cn=(2nn)−(2nn−1)C_n=\binom{2n}{n}-\binom{2n}{n-1}Cn​=(n2n​)−(n−12n​)

For a balanced-bracket prefix, a right step opens a bracket and an up step closes it. Reject a prefix that already went negative. For simple counts a grid DP can also forbid cells above the diagonal.

Check the boundary. Reflection needs the step set and boundary used by the bijection. Obstacles can destroy it. A fixed nonempty prefix changes the remaining counts and the reflected endpoint.

Cost O(n²) grid cells or O(1) binomial lookups after factorial preprocessing under a suitable modulus.

From idea to code

  • Binomial coefficients and modular arithmetic
    Use modular binomial coefficients for independent choices and unrestricted grid paths. Check that the modulus and factorial range make the inverses used by the template valid.
  • Prefix sums and difference arrays
    A prefix answers a range sum by subtraction. A difference array reverses the operation by marking the two ends of an interval update. The sheet includes the two-dimensional inclusion–exclusion form.
