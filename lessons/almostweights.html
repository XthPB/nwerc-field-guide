Remove a shared weight before indexing the capacity

There are many items with massive weights, but all weights lie between W₀ and W₀+3. How can knapsack avoid a billion capacity columns?

Work through the choice

If you select k items, exactly kW₀ of their total weight is known. Only the excess above that common part varies, and it is at most 3k. Index by the item count and this small excess, and store the best value.

The rule you can reuse

wtotal=kW0+e,0≤e≤3kw_{\rm total}=kW_0+e,\qquad 0\le e\le3kwtotal​=kW0​+e,0≤e≤3k

On taking weight w and value v, increase k by one, increase e by w−W₀ and add v. Read only old choices, then maximize over affordable (k,e).

Check the boundary. The saving comes from a proved narrow weight spread. If the spread grows with capacity, the excess table can be just as large as the original.

Cost O(n³Δ) straightforward time and O(n²Δ) memory for spread Δ, with Δ=3 in the example family.

From idea to code

  • 0/1 knapsack
    Use the descending capacity loop for one-use items. The initial zeros in this implementation mean “at most this capacity.” For exact weight, mark initially unreachable cells before the first update.
  • Knapsack by profit
    Choose the total profit as the array index. Store the minimum weight for each exact profit and scan for the largest affordable one. These two lines are the original sheet's design note.
