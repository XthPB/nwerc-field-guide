‹ Multidimensional states, from 2D to 4D

Delete a coordinate using a conservation law

Distribute every item among three bags. Must your table remember all three current loads?

Work through the choice

After processing weights 2 and 3 you placed exactly 5 units somewhere. If the first two bags hold 2 and 0, subtraction tells you the third holds 3. Every transition can recover this coordinate for free.

The rule you can reuse

Write an explicit equation linking coordinates before allocating storage. A fixed elapsed time, total consumed weight, number of chosen objects, or grid diagonal can determine one coordinate exactly.

w3=Pi−w1−w2,c1=t−r1,c2=t−r2w_3=P_i-w_1-w_2,\qquad c_1=t-r_1,\quad c_2=t-r_2w3​=Pi​−w1​−w2​,c1​=t−r1​,c2​=t−r2​

If every processed item goes into one of three bags and processed mass is Pᵢ, w₃=Pᵢ−w₁−w₂. A state (i,w₁,w₂) recovers the third bag’s weight. Check all three capacities after a transition.

Check the boundary. For two synchronous right/down walkers, c₁=t−r₁ and c₂=t−r₂, so four positions reduce to (t,r₁,r₂). Waiting, unequal move lengths, or discarded items can destroy the equality. Symmetry under relabeling bags requires identical future rules too.

Cost The three-bag feasibility example uses O(nC₁C₂) time and O(C₁C₂) rolling space with inclusive axes. Choose the two smallest capacities when labels can be permuted with their capacities.
