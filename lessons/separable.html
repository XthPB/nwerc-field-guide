Expand a square and factor the coefficient of a pair

Sum a squared rectangle score over many rectangles of a grid. How can you avoid visiting every pair of grid cells for every rectangle?

Work through the choice

Expand the square into contributions of pairs of cells. Count the rectangles that contain a pair. The row contribution and column contribution separate, so one-dimensional weighted sweeps can be applied one axis at a time.

The rule you can reuse

A sum over every rectangle of signed (rectangle sum)² looks four-dimensional. Expand the square into ordered pairs of cells. The signed number of rectangles containing a pair factors into independent row and column factors.

K(i,j)=−(∑l≤min⁡(i,j)(−1)l)(∑r≥max⁡(i,j)(−1)r)K(i,j)=-\left(\sum_{l\le\min(i,j)}(-1)^l\right)\left(\sum_{r\ge\max(i,j)}(-1)^r\right)K(i,j)=−​l≤min(i,j)∑​(−1)l​​r≥max(i,j)∑​(−1)r​

On one axis let u=min(i,j), v=max(i,j). The factor K(i,j)=Σ_{l≤u,r≥v}(−1)^(r−l+1) equals −(Σ_{l≤u}(−1)^l)(Σ_{r≥v}(−1)^r). The 2D pair coefficient is Krow·Kcol.

Check the boundary. Apply the 1D operator to all rows and then all columns, and take a dot product with the original grid. The square expansion uses ordered pairs, so adding another factor of two would double-count off-diagonal contributions.

Cost O(nm) arithmetic and O(nm) storage, with modular normalization for negative signs. CF 2275H, rated 2400.
