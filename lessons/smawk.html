Recognize a cost matrix · Lesson 168 of 235

A totally monotone transition matrix

You need the cheapest column in each row of a large table, and you can ask for the cost of any cell. There is a promise about every subtable you can get by deleting rows and columns. Going down its rows, the leftmost cheapest column can stay put or move right. How many cells must you actually inspect?

1. Work one example

Compare two columns across ordered rows. Total monotonicity guarantees their preference cannot reverse in the forbidden direction, even after rows or columns are removed. SMAWK uses that stronger property to eliminate candidates and interpolate remaining row minima.

2. What do we need to remember?

A candidate that loses a comparison can be discarded only under total monotonicity. Then find minima in a smaller set of rows and interpolate the rest.

3. How do we get the next answer?

View each destination as a row and predecessor as a column. If row minima are monotone in every submatrix, the full rectangular matrix is totally monotone and SMAWK finds all argmins with a linear number of entry evaluations.

argmin⁡jAi,j≤argmin⁡jAi+1,jin every submatrix, with consistent ties\operatorname{argmin}_j A_{i,j}\le\operatorname{argmin}_j A_{i+1,j}\qquad\text{in every submatrix, with consistent ties}argminj​Ai,j​≤argminj​Ai+1,j​in every submatrix, with consistent ties

Reduce columns by comparing a candidate at the row indexed by the current stack depth. Recurse on alternate rows; search each remaining row only between the argmins of its neighbouring solved rows.

Check the boundary. Monotone minima of the full matrix alone justify ordinary divide-and-conquer, but may fail after deleting rows or columns. A triangular feasible domain needs a proof for the chosen padding or a partial-matrix algorithm.

Cost O(rows+columns) oracle evaluations for a full totally monotone matrix. Cost evaluation must also be efficient.
