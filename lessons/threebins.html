Read two strings together · Lesson 42 of 235

Three independently depleted piles give three prefix indices

You may pair two numbers of different colours and earn their product. What is the best score from red 7,3, blue 5 and green 4?

1. Work one example

After sorting positive values within each colour, try which two colours to pair next. Each choice advances two indices. Remember how much of every colour is gone because it determines which pairs still exist.

2. What do we need to remember?

The next choice uses the exposed ends of two piles. Three consumed lengths identify all exposed items.

3. How do we get the next answer?

Pair items of different colours for a product reward with positive values. Sorting each colour in decreasing order can turn arbitrary subsets into three consumed prefixes. Prove why an optimum can expose a legal pair at the fronts before allocating the table.

F[i,j,k] is the best additional score from three suffixes. Among nonempty colour pairs choose their two fronts, add their product, and advance just their two indices. With fewer than two colours left, the value is zero.

Check the boundary. Take a globally largest available item x. An optimum can use x if a pair remains, by replacing a no-larger endpoint. If x’s partner b has a larger same-colour replacement b′ paired with c, swapping partners gains (x−c)(b′−b)≥0. An unmatched b′ is even simpler. Thus some optimal first pair consists of two fronts.

Cost O((R+1)(G+1)(B+1)) time and cells. A reverse fill can roll one axis if dependencies within a fixed layer are also visited in reverse.
