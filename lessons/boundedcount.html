Count bounded allocations with one sliding sum

Two children may receive at most two and three candies, respectively. In how many ways can you give out exactly three indistinguishable candies?

Work through the choice

Once the second child's amount is known the rest belongs to earlier children. For total three, the earlier row is queried at zero, one, two and three. For total four that whole interval moves by one. A running sum can reuse almost all of the work.

The rule you can reuse

Fi(s)=∑j=max⁡(0,s−ai)sFi−1(j)F_i(s)=\sum_{j=\max(0,s-a_i)}^s F_{i-1}(j)Fi​(s)=j=max(0,s−ai​)∑s​Fi−1​(j)

Initialize the empty allocation with a single way to give total zero. Add the next old cell when the window moves and subtract the one beyond its left boundary.

Check the boundary. Keep a separate old row and correct a negative modular difference. For denominations of weight w, use one sliding sum on each residue modulo w.

Cost O(nK) time and O(K) memory for target K.

From idea to code

  • 0/1 knapsack
    Use the descending capacity loop for one-use items. The initial zeros in this implementation mean “at most this capacity.” For exact weight, mark initially unreachable cells before the first update.
  • Prefix sums and difference arrays
    A prefix answers a range sum by subtraction. A difference array reverses the operation by marking the two ends of an interval update. The sheet includes the two-dimensional inclusion–exclusion form.
