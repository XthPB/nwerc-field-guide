When one move splits a game in two

A player splits one pile into two unequal positive piles. Players alternate. Does splitting a pile of five give a winning move?

Work through the choice

After a split either remaining pile can be played independently. XOR their Grundy values to get the successor's value. The pile before choosing a split receives the mex of all those successor values.

The rule you can reuse

g(n)=mex⁡{g(a)⊕g(n−a):1≤a<n−a}g(n)=\operatorname{mex}\{g(a)\mathbin{\oplus}g(n-a):1\le a<n-a\}g(n)=mex{g(a)⊕g(n−a):1≤a<n−a}

Process n in increasing order. Compute successor XORs before taking mex. If the whole input already contains multiple independent piles, XOR their finished values.

Check the boundary. This calculation requires finite impartial normal play. If a rule couples the two child piles, their Grundy numbers no longer combine independently.

Cost O(n²) elementary precomputation up to n. For enormous n a new proof or a verified stronger property is needed.

From idea to code

  • Grundy values on a finite game
    Use mex for a finite impartial normal-play game and XOR for its independent disjoint components. Weighted score games need minimax, while cyclic games may also have draws.
