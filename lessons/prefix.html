‹ Range queries inside DP

Query an interval of previous values

Count routes to square 4 when each jump may advance by 1, 2 or 3. What if there are thousands of allowed lengths in a consecutive range?

Work through the choice

At destination i, every legal starting square lies in a consecutive interval. Summing its route counts gives the answer for i. Store running totals so subtracting two of them gives the whole interval in constant time.

The rule you can reuse

Translate the legal predecessor condition into explicit lower and upper indices. When combining a sum, prefix sums subtract an interval in O(1). Prefix maxima work only for prefixes since maxima cannot be subtracted.

D(i)=P(i−L)−P(i−R−1)D(i)=P(i-L)-P(i-R-1)D(i)=P(i−L)−P(i−R−1)

If d[i]=Σ_{j=i−R…i−L} d[j], maintain P[t]=Σ_{j≤t}d[j]. Then d[i]=P[i−L]−P[i−R−1], with zero outside the prefix.

Check the boundary. For a maximum or minimum over arbitrary intervals use a matching data structure. A difference array supports range additions by scheduling one positive and one negative event, including the event after the range.

Cost O(n) for sequential prefix or difference sums. With interleaved updates and queries, consider a Fenwick tree or segment tree.
