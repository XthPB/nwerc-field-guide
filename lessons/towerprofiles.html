Cross a narrow boundary · Lesson 144 of 235

Count towers by the seam at their top

Stack integer-sized rectangles to fill an area two cells wide and n cells high. How many different block boundaries can the finished tower have?

1. Work one example

At the current top, the two cells belong either to a single wide block or to two narrow blocks. To add one row, decide which old blocks continue upwards and which new ones begin. Those two top states distinguish all future choices.

2. What do we need to remember?

Count partial towers by the shape exposed at their top. Enumerate how the next layer changes that shape.

3. How do we get the next answer?

W′=2W+S,S′=W+4SW'=2W+S,\qquad S'=W+4SW′=2W+S,S′=W+4S

Start with W=S=1 at height one. A wide top stays wide by extending its block or starting a new wide block. The other transitions follow by inspecting the seam. Precompute up to the largest requested height.

Check the boundary. A 2-by-n domino-tiling recurrence counts different objects. Rectangles here may continue for many rows and a seam distinguishes two towers that cover the same cells.

Cost O(max n + queries) time and O(max n) saved answers. Matrix power handles a single extremely large height.

Try one change

Two towers have the same height but different seams at their tops. Can their extension counts be merged?

See the reasoning

Only if the same next layers are possible with the same multiplicities. Otherwise count them as different top profiles.

From idea to code

  • Matrix multiplication and power
    The sheet multiplies with ordinary modular addition and multiplication. For minimum-cost transitions replace those by min and plus, use an infinite unreachable value, and skip unreachable additions.
  • Two-state DP with rolling layers
    Track the best score when the preceding house was skipped or taken. Each step reads the old pair. Adapt it by naming the histories a future move can distinguish.
See the idea in contest problems
  • CSES · Counting Towers

    Distinguish a single wide block across the top from two separate narrow blocks. Their continuation choices give a two-state linear recurrence.
