What must a state remember?

You finish one round with 7 points and a red card, or 9 and a blue card. Which result should you keep if the next round pays 10 for red?

Work through the choice

Imagine throwing the red route away. You just threw away the only route to 17. Keep a separate best score for each last colour. Earlier moves can be forgotten once their colour and score are recorded.

Two arrivals at the same vertex

1 / 3

One road leads to two histories

On s→a costing 4, paying now reaches the upper state for 4. Using the coupon reaches the lower state for 0.

Try the upper continuation

The upper state spends its coupon on a→t, normally priced 9. The final cost is 4.

Compare both continuations

The lower state already spent the coupon, so its final cost is 9. Merging the two arrivals at a solely by paid cost would discard the winner.

The rule you can reuse

Two partial solutions may share a cell when every possible continuation has the same legality and the same incremental reward. Start from a decision tree. At one depth, try to tell two histories apart using a single future item. If you succeed, add the missing state.

h1∼h2⟹same legal suffixes and future contributionsh_1\sim h_2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{same legal suffixes and future contributions}h1​∼h2​⟹same legal suffixes and future contributions

F(i,s) = best score after i decisions whose observable history is s.

Check the boundary. For counting, add multiplicities of equivalent histories. For maximization, keep the best prefix only when extending it adds the same future reward.
