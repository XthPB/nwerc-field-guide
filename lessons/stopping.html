‹ Probability, expectations and games

Decide whether to keep a reward or try once more

A fair six-sided die pays its face value. You may keep the first result or reroll once and must take the second result. What is this option worth?

Work through the choice

If you reroll, your future expected reward is 3.5 regardless of the first result. After observing a face, compare it with 3.5. Average these six optimal choices only after making the decision for each face.

The rule you can reuse

Vt+1=E ⁣[max⁡(X,Vt)]V_{t+1}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\max(X,V_t)\right]Vt+1​=E[max(X,Vt​)]

Use V₁=E[X] when exactly one mandatory observation remains. Deduct any retry fee or discount from the continuation branch before comparing.

Check the boundary. If an action must be chosen before seeing X, the maximum and expectation occur in the opposite order. If attempts are dependent, the continuation value needs the relevant history.

Cost O(remaining trials × outcomes), or less when a distribution admits prefix evaluation.
