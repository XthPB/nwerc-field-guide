‹ Geometry inside a DP

Make geometry part of the state · Lesson 191 of 235

Choose the triangle incident to a boundary chord

You’re given the vertices of a convex polygon in boundary order. Split it into triangles using noncrossing diagonals. A triangulation’s score is the area of its largest triangle. Find the smallest possible score over all triangulations.

1. Work one example

Pick a boundary edge. Exactly one triangle touches it. Trying its third vertex divides the polygon into two independent subpolygons. The cost of this choice is the largest of the new triangle and the two subproblem costs.

Try the two diagonals of a quadrilateral

1 / 3

Fix boundary side 0–1

Every triangulation contains exactly one triangle touching this boundary side. Its third vertex is either 2 or 3.

Try third vertex 2

This draws diagonal 0–2, of length √20. The two triangle perimeters together contain it twice.

Try third vertex 3

This draws diagonal 1–3, of length √17. Boundary length is identical, so this choice minimizes total triangle perimeter.

2. What do we need to remember?

Fix a boundary edge. Choosing its incident triangle separates a polygon interval into two smaller intervals.

3. How do we get the next answer?

List polygon vertices in boundary order. In a triangulation, a given boundary edge has one incident triangle. Fixing its third vertex k separates the remaining polygon into two chains. This identifies both a recurrence and a unique decomposition for counting.

D(i,j)=min⁡i<k<j{D(i,k)+D(k,j)+w(i,k,j)}D(i,j)=\min_{i<k<j}\{D(i,k)+D(k,j)+w(i,k,j)\}D(i,j)=i<k<jmin​{D(i,k)+D(k,j)+w(i,k,j)}

For a convex polygon, D[i,j]=min over i<k<j of D[i,k]+D[k,j]+w(i,k,j), with an edge costing zero. For a minimax objective replace the sum by max of the two child values and the current triangle score.

Check the boundary. For nonconvex inputs test diagonal visibility and triangle interior validity, respecting the definition of boundary contact. A triangle outside the polygon can otherwise look artificially cheap. Use doubled area from exact cross products for area comparisons on integral points.

Cost O(n³) candidate triangles and O(n²) DP cells for a convex polygon. For nonconvex inputs include preprocessing or the actual per-candidate validity cost. Save k to recover the triangles.

Try one change

A convex polygon has fixed boundary edge LR. Once its incident triangle LKR is chosen, what remains?

See the reasoning

Two smaller polygons on the two sides of K. In a nonconvex polygon the same split needs visibility and legality checks.

From idea to code

  • Interval DP in increasing length
    Use the increasing-length loop for two smaller intervals. Supply the base cases, valid split positions, immediate cost and choice of min, max or sum from the problem.
  • Exact integer geometry
    Use dot and cross products for structural tests. For route costs use the distance definition required by the problem and a sufficiently wide type. Exact squared distance and Euclidean length may give different optima.
