Cut the last piece · Lesson 46 of 235

Know when a segment contains every copy of its values

In 4,1,4,2,1,3, you may take disjoint segments. Each segment must contain every copy of each value it touches. How do you decide where a segment starting at the first 4 may end?

1. Work one example

The first 4 forces you to reach position 3. Passing the first 1 pushes the required end to position 5. Position 4 adds a 2 with no later copy. At position 5 every obligation has been met, so positions 1 through 5 form a valid segment.

2. What do we need to remember?

During a scan from l, remember the furthest last occurrence you still owe. A value with an occurrence before l rules out every extension from this start.

3. How do we get the next answer?

B[r+1]←max⁡(B[r+1], B[l]+score(l,r)).B[r+1]\gets\max\bigl(B[r+1],\,B[l]+\mathrm{score}(l,r)\bigr).B[r+1]←max(B[r+1],B[l]+score(l,r)).

B[l] is the best score before l. Always allow skipping l. Precompute first and last occurrences. For fixed l scan r rightward, expanding the required end to the largest last occurrence seen. A cut is valid exactly when r reaches that end and no seen value began before l. Maintain the XOR of distinct values with a seen marker per start. A valid early cut can still be extended into another valid interval.

Check the boundary. A value whose first occurrence is before l invalidates the entire scan for this l. Merely checking whether the current right endpoint is its last occurrence misses this case.

Cost O(n²) scan time and O(n) memory after coordinate compression. Suitable for a few thousand positions. Treat larger inputs as a new optimization question.

Try one change

In 4,1,4,2,1, can the block starting at the first 4 stop at the second 4?

See the reasoning

No. On the way to the second 4 it touched 1, which occurs again at the end. The required right boundary moves whenever a new obligation appears.
