Summarize a deterministic subtree traversal that resets its own memory

A deterministic walker explores child branches in a fixed cyclic order. How many steps pass before it returns through its parent edge?

1. Work one example

When a full excursion restores the relevant local memory, later visits can reuse its measured duration. Count the child trips and the final move up once per subtree. An arbitrary starting vertex may first have to finish an incomplete excursion.

2. What do we need to remember?

After returning from a subtree, check whether the traversal has reset its memory. If so, reuse the parent’s answer as a fresh start.

3. How do we get the next answer?

When a local traversal writes state inside the tree, verify the state left at the moment it returns through the parent edge. If an excursion restores the entire subtree to its blank state, its duration becomes composable and repeated visits need no simulation.

In the binary rotor example a fresh leaf takes one step to its parent. A fresh internal vertex goes left and returns, goes right and returns, then moves to its parent. Thus T(u)=3+T(left)+T(right). Time to the top from u is T(u)+time(parent).

Check the boundary. Prove the reset inductively, including erasing the parent marker after the second child. Without reset, the message must record both the entering and exiting local state; adding independent excursion times would be incorrect.

Cost O(n) with a postorder for durations and a preorder for rootward totals. CF 2195E, rated 1500.
