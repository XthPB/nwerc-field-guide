‹ Randomization and DP

Fix random decisions one at a time while preserving an expectation

Split graph vertices into two sides while guaranteeing at least half the edges cross.

Work through the choice

A uniformly random assignment has expected cut size equal to half the edges. When fixing one vertex, the average of its two conditional expectations equals your current promise. Choose the side whose expectation meets or exceeds that average.

The rule you can reuse

An existence proof by random construction can yield a deterministic construction. At each prefix, condition on each possible next choice. Their probability-weighted average equals the current expected value, so at least one choice attains it for maximization.

V(p)=∑bPr⁡[b∣p]V(pb)≤max⁡bV(pb)V(p)=\sum_b\Pr[b\mid p]V(pb)\le\max_b V(pb)V(p)=b∑​Pr[b∣p]V(pb)≤bmax​V(pb)

Let V(p)=E[score | fixed prefix p]. Choose b maximizing V(pb) among positive-probability options. After all decisions the realized score is at least the original expectation. A suffix DP can evaluate V when local counting does not suffice.

Check the boundary. A Monte Carlo simulation of V(pb) introduces error into the decision. Exact conditional values or rigorous sufficient bounds are needed for a deterministic guarantee. This method guarantees an achieved lower bound, which can be below the true optimum.

Cost For the cut example O(V+E) suffices by updating fixed-neighbour counts. In general budget the number of prefixes times the cost of the conditional DP, with incremental updates when possible.
