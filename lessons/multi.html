Shrink the knapsack axis · Lesson 60 of 235

Cardinality, two budgets, and resource coupling

You have items weighing 2, 3 and 5. Can you reach weight 5 using exactly two items? Then consider a version with a separate money budget as well as a weight limit. Each condition must hold for the same selection.

1. Work one example

Weights 2 and 3 satisfy both requirements. A lone 5 satisfies only the first. First write down what the judge can distinguish. For a small count, keep one sum-bitset per count. For two independent budgets, index both or retain undominated pairs.

2. What do we need to remember?

Two independent resource limits require a pair of used amounts. Test whether both still fit before taking an item.

3. How do we get the next answer?

If future feasibility depends on how many items were chosen as well as their weight, carry both. Sometimes two numerical resources have a special objective that lets you optimize one while indexing the other.

Rk←RkOR(Rk−1≪w)R_k\gets R_k\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}(R_{k-1}\ll w)Rk​←Rk​OR(Rk−1​≪w)

R[k] ← R[k] OR (R[k−1] ≪ w), descending in k. For trailing zeros, factor 2s and 5s, keep maximum twos for each (k,total fives).

Check the boundary. A single weighted sum of two budgets generally merges incomparable states. Also distinguish “at most k” from “exactly k” at initialization and output.

Cost Boolean cardinality O(nK⌈W/word⌉). Two dense budgets O(nAB), often the cue to seek an invariant or a frontier.

A bag has two free kilograms and one free slot. Can you take two one-kilogram objects?

From idea to code

  • Bitset subset sum
    Set only bit zero at the start. A shifted copy represents taking one item. OR retains the outcomes of skipping it. Choose MAXS large enough for the actual largest queried sum.
  • 0/1 knapsack
    Use the descending capacity loop for one-use items. The initial zeros in this implementation mean “at most this capacity.” For exact weight, mark initially unreachable cells before the first update.
