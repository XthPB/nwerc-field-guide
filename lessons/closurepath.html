A transitive closure can make the entire SCC collectible

In a directed graph you may jump from u to v whenever v is reachable from u. Construct a route visiting distinct vertices. First maximize the number visited, then minimize their total vertex weight among routes of that length. A strongly connected group lets you choose the order in which you collect its vertices.

1. Work one example

Under a transitive-closure rule you can visit all vertices of a component in a chosen order. Summarize that gain at one node and decide which outgoing component to follow. Check the problem's move rule before making this compression.

2. What do we need to remember?

Before treating a whole component as freely traversable, determine what may be collected between each legal entry and exit.

3. How do we get the next answer?

When an input graph is completed with all reachability edges, every strongly connected component becomes a complete directed graph on its vertices. A simple path may traverse them in any order before moving forward to another component. This gives an exact component summary.

For a longest path with minimum sum as tie-break, give component C score (|C|,−Σaᵢ). Add scores componentwise along the condensation DAG and take the lexicographic maximum. Initialize at any component if any start is allowed.

Check the boundary. Ordinary SCC contraction for a longest simple path may overcount. Here the closure makes arbitrary internal order legal and supplies direct edges to reachable downstream vertices. Visiting extra intermediate components improves the primary objective, so original condensation arcs suffice.

Cost O(V+E) after a linear SCC decomposition plus topological DP. The quadratic transitive closure is never materialized.

Try one change

A walk may collect each reward once but may revisit vertices freely. Why can all rewards in one strongly connected component be harvested?

See the reasoning

You can route between required vertices and then to a chosen exit. This argument changes if revisiting vertices is forbidden or travel has an unaccounted cost.

From idea to code

  • Strongly connected components
    Build a condensation DAG and define what one component contributes. A simple path with restricted entry and exit may need its own internal subproblem.
  • Topological order and DAG DP
    Use a verified acyclic order. Process every outgoing transition after its source is final. Counting merges by addition and longest paths by maximum.
