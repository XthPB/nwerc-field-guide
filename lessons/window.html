Restrict the previous choices · Lesson 148 of 235

A moving predecessor window with a monotone deque

Keep the best score among the last two positions as you advance. What happens when yesterday's best leaves the window?

1. Work one example

Keep indices in order, discarding an older candidate whenever a newer one scores at least as well and expires later. The front then gives the best live score. Expire indices by position as the window moves.

2. What do we need to remember?

A new endpoint changes the eligible predecessors by adding and dropping a few indices. Maintain their best value as the window slides.

3. How do we get the next answer?

Suppose transitions take maximum of scores b[j] over a window [Lᵢ,Rᵢ] whose endpoints never move left. Later indices with higher scores dominate earlier lower ones until expiry. Keep indices with decreasing scores.

D(i)=max⁡Li≤j≤Rib(j)D(i)=\max_{L_i\le j\le R_i} b(j)D(i)=Li​≤j≤Ri​max​b(j)

Before answering i, insert all new j≤Rᵢ, popping back while b[back]≤b[j]; pop front while front<Lᵢ. Then b[front] is the maximum.

Check the boundary. Indices enter only once and in order. If the score is a function of both i and j, subtract a separable i-term first or show comparisons remain stable. Changing candidate order defeats the deque.

Cost O(n+number of insertions) for an ordered sweep, O(window size) space.

Try one change

A new candidate has a larger score and expires later than an older candidate. Can the older one ever be best in a future maximum window?

See the reasoning

No, if both are eligible now and eligibility expires in that order. Remove the dominated older candidate from the deque.
