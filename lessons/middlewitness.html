Follow a prefix · Lesson 10 of 235

Count choices on both sides of a middle position

You’re given a string of Qs and As. Choose three increasing positions that spell QAQ. The letters need not be consecutive, and two choices count separately if they use different positions. How many choices are there in QAQAQ?

1. Work one example

For the first A there is one Q before it and two after it, giving two triples. For the second A there are two before it and one after it, giving two more. The middle positions differ, so the total is four.

2. What do we need to remember?

Fix the unique middle position. Count the compatible left choices and right choices, then multiply when those choices are independent.

3. How do we get the next answer?

answer=∑i: si=ALiRi.\mathrm{answer}=\sum_{i:\,s_i=A} L_i R_i.answer=i:si​=A∑​Li​Ri​.

Here L counts earlier Q positions and R counts later ones. Initialize R with all Q positions. As you pass a Q, move it from right to left. For a longer fixed pattern, the corresponding prefix and suffix DPs can count multi-character embeddings on either side. For stride subsequences, apply the same reasoning inside each residue class.

Check the boundary. If the left and right choices share a budget or a required value, their counts cannot simply be multiplied. Split by the shared state before multiplying.

Cost O(n) time and O(1) auxiliary memory for QAQ. A general fixed pattern of length m can be counted with O(nm) time.

Try one change

In QAQAQ, how many triples use the first A as their middle?

See the reasoning

One Q lies before it and two lie after it. Pair the left Q with either right Q for two triples. Repeat at the second A to get four altogether.
