Winning positions, minimax, and sums of independent games

Two heaps allow removing one or two stones from exactly one heap per turn. Who can force the last move?

1. Work one example

First assign each single heap the smallest nonnegative value absent from its legal successors. Independent impartial heaps combine by XOR. Two individually winning heaps can cancel each other out.

2. What do we need to remember?

From a player’s turn, compare legal moves after accounting for what the opponent will choose in reply.

3. How do we get the next answer?

In a finite acyclic impartial normal-play game, losing positions have no move to a losing position, so every successor is winning. For a disjoint sum where each turn changes just one component, Grundy numbers combine by XOR.

g(s)=mex⁡{g(t):s→t},g(G1+G2)=g(G1)⊕g(G2)g(s)=\operatorname{mex}\{g(t):s\to t\},\qquad g(G_1+G_2)=g(G_1)\oplus g(G_2)g(s)=mex{g(t):s→t},g(G1​+G2​)=g(G1​)⊕g(G2​)

g(s)=mex{g(t): s→t}; no moves gives 0. A sum of independent components is losing exactly when their XOR is 0.

Check the boundary. A game with draws, player-specific moves, simultaneous moves or misère termination needs different rules. For a score difference, use minimax with signed reward rather than mex.

Cost O(states + moves) for simple win/loss on a DAG. Mex can be linear in outdegree with a timestamp array.

Try one change

One legal move hands the opponent a losing state. Is your state winning under normal play?

See the reasoning

Yes. Choose that move. A position is losing only when every legal move gives the opponent a win, or no move is available under normal play.
