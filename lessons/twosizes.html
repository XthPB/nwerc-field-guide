Quotient rounding can leave only two states per depth

Repeatedly split an integer pile into two halves rounded down and up. Can a descendant of 19 have size 5?

Work through the choice

At any given depth the largest and smallest possible sizes differ by at most one. Remember just these sizes. The floor and ceiling of the next split determine the next layer, so individual branches do not have to be visited.

The rule you can reuse

Repeatedly splitting a positive size into its floor and ceiling halves sounds like a binary tree of possibilities. At depth d every positive part is either floor(n/2ᵈ) or ceil(n/2ᵈ). A chosen lineage is all that needs to be split to reach a target size.

Sd⊆{⌊n2d⌋,⌈n2d⌉}S_d\subseteq\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2^d}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil\frac{n}{2^d}\right\rceil\right\}Sd​⊆{⌊2dn​⌋,⌈2dn​⌉}

Check the two positive possibilities at increasing d and stop at the first depth containing k. One split changes the depth of the chosen lineage by exactly one.

Check the boundary. The invariant comes from repeated halves of consecutive integer sizes. If children use unrelated ratios the reachable set may have many gaps. Stop once the largest size is 1.

Cost O(log n) time and O(1) extra space. Update the two extrema directly to avoid overflowing a shifted denominator. CF 2184C, rated 1100.
