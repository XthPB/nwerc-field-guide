A count-generating function can support item deletion

A one-use weight can be inserted and later removed. Can the counts for each sum be repaired without replaying the whole history?

Work through the choice

Adding weight w multiplies a count polynomial by 1+xᵂ. For removal recover each old coefficient from the current coefficient and the already recovered coefficient w places earlier. This explains the ascending deletion loop.

The rule you can reuse

For labeled one-use positive weights, inserting w multiplies the truncated count polynomial by 1+zʷ. Its constant coefficient is 1, so there is a unique inverse for truncated power series over any modulus.

As=Cs−As−w,C(z)=(1+zw)A(z)A_s=C_s-A_{s-w},\qquad C(z)=(1+z^w)A(z)As​=Cs​−As−w​,C(z)=(1+zw)A(z)

Insert with descending sums. To delete a present item, scan ascending and set old[s]=current[s]−old[s−w]. Normalize modulo the requested modulus.

Check the boundary. Boolean OR and max-profit tables lose information and cannot be undone this way. w=0 multiplies by 2; division by 2 may be impossible modulo an even number.

Cost O(W) per insertion or deletion, O(W) memory. Offline lifetimes or rollback work for many noninvertible objectives.
