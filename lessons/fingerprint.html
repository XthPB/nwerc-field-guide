‹ Randomization and DP

Evaluate a counting polynomial when only identity matters

Two enormous count polynomials are costly to expand. Can a quick evaluation establish that they differ?

Work through the choice

Evaluate both at one random field element. A disagreement proves the polynomials differ. A nonzero difference polynomial can have at most its degree many roots, which bounds the chance of an accidental match under a uniform field sample.

The rule you can reuse

A DP can represent ways by a polynomial too large to store. If only equality of two such polynomials over a field matters, evaluating all transitions at the same random argument preserves addition and multiplication. A nonzero difference of degree d has at most d roots.

Draw a uniformly random r in the prime field Fₚ. Compute A(r) and B(r) through their DPs. Different values prove inequality. For equal values the false-equality probability on a fixed unequal pair is at most min(1,d/p).

Check the boundary. The bound requires the difference to be nonzero modulo p. If integer coefficients can all vanish modulo p, supply a coefficient bound or an additional justified modulus scheme. A union bound over q fixed tests costs at most qd/p per trial. Adaptively chosen tests need a separate argument.

Cost One scalar per DP state per independent evaluation. Use a deterministic comparison when exactness is required and affordable. The path-class lesson shows an exact dominator alternative for a universal co-occurrence problem.
