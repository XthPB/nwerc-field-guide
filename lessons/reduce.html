‹ Knapsack and subset sum

Partition, complements, gcd, and smallest missing sum

Which positive total is the first you cannot pay with coins 1, 2, 2 and 7?

Work through the choice

Suppose every total through 5 is possible. To make 6, you must use another item or stay inside that old interval. The next item is already 7. Sorting turns this first gap into a proof that later coins cannot help.

The rule you can reuse

Normalize before paying for a table. For a two-way partition of nonnegative integers, fixing one side fixes the other. For positive integer sums, a common divisor shrinks the axis. For the smallest missing sum, sorted prefix coverage can collapse to one interval.

min⁡s∈R, s≤S/2∣S−2s∣\min_{s\in R,\ s\le S/2}\lvert S-2s\rverts∈R, s≤S/2min​∣S−2s∣

Minimize |S−2s| over reachable s≤S/2. If all sums 0…r exist and next weight a≤r+1, coverage becomes 0…r+a.

Check the boundary. The interval invariant needs strictly positive items and gap-free coverage from zero. It cannot list arbitrary reachable sums for weights 3,5. Count partitions carefully when complementary sides are equivalent.

Cost O(n log n) for the coverage greedy. The partition table typically needs only floor(S/2)+1 columns.

From idea to code

  • Bitset subset sum
    Set only bit zero at the start. A shifted copy represents taking one item. OR retains the outcomes of skipping it. Choose MAXS large enough for the actual largest queried sum.
