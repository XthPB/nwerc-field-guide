Grid and lattice paths, tilings and frontiers

Start with one walker and one count per cell. Add a turn or residue when it affects the next move. Add another walker for interactions, then shrink a wide history to the narrow frontier that a tiling can still see.

Start this topic in reading order → · See the whole reading path

  • Separate a monotone walk from a general shortest pathYou can only move right or down across a 2 by 3 grid. How many routes reach the far corner if the top middle cell is blocked?
  • Find the largest all-filled square by its bottom-right cornerWhere is the largest square of 1s in a binary image? Checking each possible side length at every corner repeats most of the work.
  • Count paths with a limited number of turnsA walker crosses a 3 by 3 board from corner to corner, going right or down. How many paths use at most one turn?
  • Carry a remainder along a constrained grid walkMove diagonally up from one bottom-row cell per turn. What is the largest digit sum divisible by 3 at the top?
  • Minimize trailing zeros on a path, including zero cellsChoose a right/down path with the fewest trailing zeros in its product. What can a grid DP add instead of multiplying enormous integers?
  • Keep an entire diagonal of equally good prefixesCross a letter grid by moving right or down, spelling one letter per cell. How do you find the smallest possible word without keeping a long string at every cell?
  • Synchronize two paths by elapsed movesSend two walkers across a grid together and collect each visited reward once. Do you need four independent coordinates?
  • Paint column runs with a minimum and maximum widthRepaint marked and blank pixels so equal-colour vertical stripes have widths from x to y. What part of the two-dimensional picture still affects the cost?
  • A narrow frontier remembers occupied cellsHow many ways tile a 2 by 3 board with dominoes? What must survive after you finish one column?
  • Count towers by the seam at their topStack integer-sized rectangles to fill an area two cells wide and n cells high. How many different block boundaries can the finished tower have?
  • Occupancy fails when the frontier must remain connectedA narrow grid must contain one connected loop. Is it enough to remember which frontier edges are occupied?
  • Replace a narrow strip by a small separatorChoose a maximum independent set in a graph built from small overlapping bags of vertices.
  • Count lattice paths that stay below a diagonalA walk uses three right steps and three up steps. How many walks never have more up steps than right steps?
  • Sparse obstacles and a first-bad-event subtractionA huge board contains a few blocked cells. Count monotone paths without storing the entire board.
  • Index a lattice DP by move counts when coordinates are hugeA robot has three fixed jump vectors and must take exactly n jumps without landing on a forbidden point. Coordinates can reach billions. What else can identify a landing?
  • Replace a huge symmetric board by four probability or count statesA rook must end on a target square after exactly k moves. On an empty rectangle, do you need a DP cell for every square?
  • Count vertex-disjoint lattice paths using a determinantHow many ways can two directed paths connect two ordered starts to two ordered finishes without sharing a vertex?

Test your recognition
A problem on a 6×100000 grid requires one connected loop. Which side should the frontier cross and what crosses it?

Sweep along the long side. Preserve occupancy, degree obligations and how frontier ends are connected across the six-cell width. Enforce the rule about premature loops.

Could a sparse-obstacle monotone path DP subtract the unrestricted paths through every obstacle independently?

A path visiting two obstacles would be subtracted twice. Subtract ways whose first obstacle is the earlier one, using the already-corrected count for each predecessor.
