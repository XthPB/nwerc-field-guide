Grid and lattice paths, tilings and frontiers

Cover a board two cells high with dominoes, each covering two neighboring cells. No cell may be missed or covered twice. How many complete tilings are possible as you extend the board one column at a time?

Start this topic in reading order → · See the whole reading path

  • Separate a monotone walk from a general shortest pathA board has two rows and three columns. You start in the upper-left corner and want to reach the lower-right corner. You may step one cell right or down, and the top middle cell is blocked. Count the different routes that avoid it.
  • Find the largest all-filled square by its bottom-right cornerA binary image is a grid of zeros and ones. Find the greatest side length of an axis-aligned square made entirely of ones. A single zero inside a candidate square invalidates the whole square, even if its corners are ones.
  • Count paths with a limited number of turnsStart in the upper-left cell of a 3 × 3 board and finish in its lower-right cell. You may move right or down. Changing from right to down or from down to right counts as a turn. Count the routes with at most one turn.
  • Carry a remainder along a constrained grid walkEach grid cell contains a digit. Start anywhere in the bottom row and step diagonally up-left or up-right until you reach the top row. Add every digit you visit, including your starting digit. Find the largest path sum divisible by 3.
  • Minimize trailing zeros on a path, including zero cellsEach cell contains a nonnegative integer. Walk from the upper-left corner to the lower-right using only right and down steps. Multiply the visited values and minimize the number of trailing decimal zeros. In this problem a product of zero is scored as having one trailing zero.
  • Keep an entire diagonal of equally good prefixesA rectangular grid contains letters. From the upper-left cell, move only right or down to the opposite corner and read every visited letter in order. Find the smallest resulting word in dictionary order, including both corner letters.
  • Synchronize two paths by elapsed movesTwo walkers start in the top-left corner of a reward grid and both finish at the opposite corner. On each tick each walker moves right or down. A cell pays its reward only once even if both walkers visit it. Maximize the total reward they collect.
  • Paint column runs with a minimum and maximum widthA black-and-white grid may be repainted one cell at a time, each repaint costing 1. Make every column a single color. Each maximal stripe of consecutive same-colored columns must have width between x and y inclusive. Find the fewest cells you must repaint.
  • A narrow frontier remembers occupied cellsFill every cell of a 2 × 3 board using dominoes that each cover two neighboring cells. A domino may be horizontal or vertical, and tiles cannot overlap. Count distinct complete tilings. A horizontal tile can extend into the next column while you are still filling this one.
  • Count towers by the seam at their topBuild a solid rectangle of width 2 and height n out of axis-aligned rectangular blocks of positive integer dimensions. Count different arrangements of block boundaries. A horizontal layer can continue blocks from below or start new ones.
  • Occupancy fails when the frontier must remain connectedOn a narrow grid, select grid connections so the final drawing is one connected loop satisfying the cell rules. Two partial drawings can cross the boundary to the next column in exactly the same places yet connect those loose ends differently. Which ones can finish as a single loop?
  • Replace a narrow strip by a small separatorYou’re given a graph together with a tree of small overlapping vertex sets, called bags. Choose as many vertices as possible without choosing both ends of an edge. Parts of the graph processed separately must agree on the vertices they share.
  • Count lattice paths that stay below a diagonalFrom the origin, make three right steps and three up steps in some order. Along the entire route, the number of up steps taken so far must never exceed the number of right steps. Count the routes that obey the rule, allowing a return to equality.
  • Sparse obstacles and a first-bad-event subtractionYou must cross a very large grid from its upper-left corner to its lower-right corner, moving one cell right or down each time. Only a short list of cells is blocked. Count valid paths when the full grid is too large to store.
  • Index a lattice DP by move counts when coordinates are hugeA robot starts at the origin. On each of exactly n moves, it chooses one of three given displacement vectors. It may never land on a forbidden coordinate. Count the legal move sequences, even when the coordinates reachable after n jumps are extremely large.
  • Replace a huge symmetric board by four probability or count statesA rook stands on an empty rectangular chessboard. Each move goes to a different square in the same row or column. Given a target and k, count the ways to finish on that target after exactly k moves. On a 3 × 4 board, try returning to the starting square in two moves.
  • Count vertex-disjoint lattice paths using a determinantA directed acyclic network has two starting vertices and two designated finishing vertices. Count pairs of directed paths that connect the prescribed pairs and share no vertex. The order of the terminals matters when distinguishing the desired pairing from the crossed pairing.

Test your recognition
A problem on a 6×100000 grid requires one connected loop. Which side should the frontier cross and what crosses it?

Sweep along the long side. Preserve occupancy, degree obligations and how frontier ends are connected across the six-cell width. Enforce the rule about premature loops.

Could a sparse-obstacle monotone path DP subtract the unrestricted paths through every obstacle independently?

A path visiting two obstacles would be subtracted twice. Subtract ways whose first obstacle is the earlier one, using the already-corrected count for each predecessor.
