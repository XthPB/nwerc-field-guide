Greedy, sorting and search · BAPC 2021-B

BnPC

Spend a limited attribute-upgrade budget to pass all challenges and maximise the total score; an attribute equal to a challenge threshold passes but contributes zero for that challenge.

The observation

First buy all mandatory upgrades to the maximum threshold per attribute. Then each attribute has one possibly large first marginal gain, followed by a constant gain equal to its challenge count. The marginal sequence never increases.

Why it works

At the feasible baseline b, let e challenges have threshold exactly b and l have a lower threshold. The first extra unit gains e(b+1)+l; every subsequent unit gains e+l. Taking the greatest currently available marginal gain is optimal by exchanging any smaller chosen gain with it. Once the maximum is a constant tail, spend the whole remaining budget there.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Enumerate upgrade allocations

Distribute the budget across attributes and score all challenges. This directly expresses the optimisation problem.

Tiny-instance oracle · O((k+1)^n) time · O(n) space

Knapsack over the budget

For each attribute and possible upgrade amount, compute its score. The budget bound makes this infeasible, but it exposes the separable score functions.

Pseudo-polynomial baseline · O(nk²) time · O(k) space

Greedy one upgrade at a time

A max-heap of current marginal gains is valid because each attribute’s gains are nonincreasing. Huge k prevents using it literally.

Correct but too slow · O(k log n) time · O(n) space

Heap with constant-tail batching

At most one special first gain exists per attribute. If the popped item is already its constant tail, no future gain can exceed it; consume all remaining budget at once.

Recommended · O((n+events) log n) time · O(n+events) space

Sort the first gains

Let M be the largest constant slope. Buy the sorted special gains above M while budget remains; all remaining units earn M. A first gain that unlocks slope M is at least M, so this batching does not use an unavailable better tail.

Alternative · O(events+n log n) time · O(n) space

Boundary checks

Failing any mandatory threshold makes the total score zero. Do not loop once per budget unit: k can be huge. Distinguish equality from strict superiority when computing the baseline score and first bonus.
