Probability, expectation and randomization · BAPC 2024-F

Failing Factory

Operations in a factory can fail independently. For an operation to work, it and everything it depends on, directly or indirectly, must succeed. Given the failure probabilities and dependencies, find an operation with the greatest chance of working.

The observation

Collapse cycles of mutual dependency. A component with additional dependencies cannot be more reliable than one of the sink components it depends on.

Why it works

Every operation in an SCC requires all operations in that SCC and the same reachable components. Multiplying more success probabilities in [0,1] never increases the result. From any non-sink component, follow dependency edges to a sink; that sink has no smaller success probability. Consequently the optimum is the largest product of success probabilities inside a sink SCC. Shared dependencies must be counted once, which is why naïve recursive multiplication is wrong.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

DFS the full dependency closure for each node

Mark visited operations per start and multiply each reachable operation exactly once. Correct, but repeats the same graph exploration many times.

Small inputs only · O(n(n+m)) time · O(n+m) space

Bitset transitive closure

Represent reachability by bitsets and use Warshall-style propagation. Count distinct dependencies, then multiply. Useful for small dense graphs, not the large contest instance.

Dense small-graph alternative · O(n³/w) time · O(n²/w) space

Kosaraju and sink products

Use two DFS passes, one on the reverse graph, to identify SCCs. Scan cross-component edges to mark nonsinks; multiply success probabilities per component.

Recommended · O(n+m) time · O(n+m) space

Tarjan in one DFS pass

Tarjan tracks discovery times, low links, and an active stack. The optimization after SCC discovery is identical; the benefit is a single pass and no explicit reversed adjacency.

Advanced alternative · O(n+m) time · O(n+m) space

Log-domain SCC aggregation

Compute sums of log(1−p) per sink, take the largest sum, and exponentiate once. This prevents products becoming zero prematurely and extends to very large systems.

Numerical refinement · O(n+m) time · O(n+m) space

Boundary checks

Orient edges from an operation to its dependencies. Multiply 1−p, not failure p. Do not multiply a shared dependency repeatedly. Underflow can be handled using log probabilities; zero-success cases need care.
