Greedy, sorting and search · BAPC 2024-C

Concurrent Contests

Assign contestants with different skills to prize contests so nobody can improve expected winnings by switching alone. A contestant’s win probability is their skill divided by the total skill in their chosen contest.

The observation

Process contestants from strongest to weakest. Compare prize/(current total skill + this skill); the contestant’s own skill factor cancels.

Why it works

Suppose after the greedy assignment a contestant of skill s wants to leave contest a for b. Let z be the last contestant inserted into a; its skill is at most s. At its insertion, a already reached its final total, while b’s total was no larger than its final total. Therefore b would have offered z at least the prize-per-skill value that now attracts s. This contradicts z choosing a. This last-arrival argument proves stability, not global maximization of total prize.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Enumerate assignments and check deviations

Try every contest for every person and verify no profitable unilateral switch. This directly encodes the equilibrium definition and makes a reliable oracle for tiny n,m.

Tiny inputs only · O(m^n·nm) time · O(n+m) space

Repeated best-response moves

Start anywhere and move a contestant whenever they improve. The archive contains such variants, but a few successful runs are not a worst-case runtime proof. Prefer the ordered construction.

Heuristic runtime · No useful simple worst-case bound time · O(n+m) space

Greedy in input order

Picking the currently most attractive contest is not enough; the proof needs later entrants to have no larger skill. This stage isolates the role of the descending sort.

Incorrect ordering · O(nm) time · O(n+m) space

Descending-skill greedy with exact comparisons

Sort participants by descending skill and place each into the best contest according to the exact rational score. Apply the last-arrival proof above.

Recommended · O(n log n+nm) time · O(n+m) space

Independent exact equilibrium checker

Recompute final totals and test every possible switch using rational comparisons. It does not construct a solution, but is a powerful companion to a non-obvious greedy proof and works as a custom output validator.

Validation method · O(nm) time · O(m) space

Boundary checks

Use cross products in __int128 to compare fractions. Preserve original participant indices after sorting. A stable assignment is not necessarily unique and is not an ordinary load-balancing objective.
