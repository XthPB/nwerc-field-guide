Graphs, connectivity and flow · BAPC 2022-H

House Numbering

Every street joins two intersections and has a fixed number of houses. You may choose from which end each street is numbered. Choose the directions so the house numbers arriving at any one intersection are all different, or determine that this is impossible.

The observation

Each street contributes exactly one endpoint numbered 1. There are n such endpoints and n intersections, each accepting at most one, so every intersection must have exactly one. The connected graph is unicyclic.

Why it works

At a leaf, its sole street must start with 1 there. Removing leaves forces every tree edge to point from the outer tree towards the cycle (start=outer endpoint). The cycle must be oriented consistently and has only two choices. Try both, checking that all incoming high house numbers are distinct at each vertex. This exhausts every possible solution.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Enumerate all street orientations

Assign each street’s starting endpoint, then validate the incident labels. Useful for fuzzing the structural solution.

Small-instance oracle · O(n·2^n) time · O(n) space

General 2-SAT encoding

Each edge is Boolean. If two choices would place equal endpoint numbers at a vertex, forbid their conjunction with a clause. Kosaraju or Tarjan solves the implication graph, but pairwise conflicts at a hub can be quadratic.

General but potentially too slow · O(Σdeg(v)²) time · O(Σdeg(v)²) space

Peel leaves and try the cycle

Use a degree queue to force tree-edge starts. Collect the remaining cycle, orient it both ways, and sort each vertex’s endpoint labels to detect duplicates.

Recommended · O(n log n), or expected O(n) with hashing time · O(n) space

DFS cycle extraction

Find the unique back edge with DFS, reconstruct the cycle, then orient all off-cycle trees toward it. This has the same proof as peeling but a different implementation route.

Alternative structural implementation · O(n log n) time · O(n) space

Forced-choice propagation

A chosen 1 at a vertex forces all other edges to put their high endpoint there. A chosen high label forces any other edge with that same label to put 1 there. Group by label to propagate efficiently; the jury contains this approach. Track contradictions and resolve the remaining cycle choice.

Constraint-solving alternative · O(n) expected with grouped labels time · O(n) space

Boundary checks

The input guarantees connectivity, a simple graph, and h≥2. Duplicate high labels can invalidate an orientation even when every vertex gets exactly one 1. Use iterative leaf peeling to avoid a deep recursive call stack.
