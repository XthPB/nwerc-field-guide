Funicular Frenzy

A cable car carries c passengers each minute for n minutes. You know every minute’s arrivals and join behind everyone arriving with you. Choose the earliest arrival time giving minimum waiting time, or report no ride possible.

The observation

Your waiting time depends only on how many people are ahead of you when you arrive. Future arrivals stand behind you.

Why it works

Maintain backlog before each minute. Add a_i to obtain q people ahead of a new arrival. They occupy floor(q/c) departures before yours, so wait=q/c and the ride exists iff i+wait<n. Then update the ordinary backlog to max(0,q−c). Scan times in order and update only on a strictly smaller waiting time to enforce the tie rule.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Simulate separately for every arrival minute

Insert a marked passenger at each candidate time and simulate the queue until boarding or closing.

Small n only · O(n²) time · O(n) space

Explicit passenger queue

Model all people and departures literally. Large arrival counts make this infeasible, but it clarifies FIFO ordering.

Value-dependent baseline · O(total arrivals) time · O(total arrivals) space

Aggregate queue simulation

A count replaces individual passengers. Evaluate the candidate before subtracting the current departure capacity.

Recommended · O(n) time · O(1) space

Prefix-sum reflected random walk

Let S_i sum arrivals minus capacity through minute i. Backlog after service equals S_i minus the minimum of zero and all earlier S values. This is the standard reflection formula for a queue.

Alternative derivation · O(n) time · O(n), or O(1) space

Range-query version for many what-if queries

For an extended task with range updates to arrival counts, prefix sums and minimum-prefix summaries become segment-tree data. A node stores total surplus and minimum prefix; composition is associative. The original one-pass simulation is still preferable.

Advanced extension · O(n) preprocessing; O(log n) queries with a tree time · O(n) space

Boundary checks

Compare waiting minutes, not raw queue size: several queue sizes have the same quotient. Include arrivals in your chosen minute before you join. Later arrivals do not delay you.
