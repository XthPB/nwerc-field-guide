Equalising Audio

Multiply all audio amplitudes by one common factor so their mean squared amplitude is x, while preserving total silence.

The observation

Scaling amplitudes by f scales their squared mean by f². Thus f=sqrt(n x / Σa_i²), unless all samples are zero.

Why it works

Substitute f into (Σ(fa_i)²)/n=f²Σa_i²/n. For nonzero input this equals x. The statement explicitly exempts all-zero audio; output zero there. When x=0, output zero even though the prose otherwise describes a positive factor.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Search a scaling factor

Binary search a nonnegative factor against the target squared mean. A suitable upper bound is needed; repeated rescanning wastes work.

Correct but unnecessary · O(nI) time · O(n) space

Precompute energy, then search

The whole signal affects feasibility only through its sum of squares S. This is a first example of finding a sufficient statistic.

Simplified alternative · O(n+I) time · O(n) space

Solve the equation

Use the square root directly and handle silence before dividing.

Recommended · O(n) time · O(n) space

Scale before squaring

For a broader problem with enormous floating-point amplitudes, divide by the largest absolute amplitude first. This avoids overflowing a_i². The contest’s integer bounds do not require it.

Numerically robust generalisation · O(n) time · O(n) space

Streaming and verification

The first pass computes energy; the second emits scaled amplitudes. Verify both energy and proportionality, rather than merely checking a few printed values.

Engineering extension · O(n) time · O(n), or two input passes space

Boundary checks

Signed amplitudes must keep their signs. Squaring a 32-bit integer before casting can overflow. The sum can reach 10^17, which fits 64-bit; use long double for the final scaling and enough output digits.
