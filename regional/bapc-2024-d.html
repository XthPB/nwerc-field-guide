Graphs, connectivity and flow · BAPC 2024-D

Disgruntled Diner

You have a list of possible orders and a board of tickets showing just one side of each. Every ticket has a table number and a menu letter. Determine whether the visible evidence already settles the claim that every completed order for table t was menu m. Otherwise choose the fewest tickets to flip to settle it for sure.

The observation

The logical counterexample is a ticket with table t and menu ≠m. Before deciding which sides matter, check whether all visible tickets can be explained using only legal orders.

Why it works

Match pinned positions to distinct legal orders that show the same visible symbol. If no complete matching exists, every possible board contains an illegal order, so the claim is false. Otherwise a legal realization exists. Any visible position compatible with an illegal order must be flipped: starting from a legal realization, replace its assigned legal order by that unused illegal order, leaving all other visible positions unchanged. A position incompatible with every illegal order cannot falsify the claim. Thus exactly the compatible positions form a necessary and sufficient inspection set.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Enumerate all hidden-ticket assignments

Enumerate injective assignments of real orders to the k visible positions. Compare the truth of the claim across possibilities and determine which positions distinguish them.

Tiny inputs only · O(n!/(n−k)!) times checking time · O(n+k) space

Bitmask assignment DP

After removing illegal orders, represent used legal tickets by a subset mask. This gives exact feasibility but is unsuitable for n up to 500.

Small n only · O(k·n·2^n) time · O(2^n) space

Kuhn augmenting-path matching

Build the legal bipartite graph, then find an augmenting path for each pinned position. A full matching settles the feasibility question; the logical proof determines flips.

Efficient baseline · O(kE) time · O(n+k+E) space

Hopcroft–Karp

Batch shortest augmenting paths with BFS layers and DFS. The logic is unchanged; this is useful practice for larger bipartite instances.

Advanced matching alternative · O(E√(n+k)) time · O(n+k+E) space

Compress visible symbols into a flow network

There are only 26 letters and 10 digits. Connect source→legal order with capacity 1, order→its two symbols, and symbol→sink with capacity equal to its visible count. Max flow k proves legal feasibility without k·n compatibility edges.

Recommended modelling alternative · Polynomial; Dinic O(V²E) general bound time · O(V+E), V=O(n+36) space

Boundary checks

Duplicate orders are separate resources. Match all pinned positions, not all orders. A visible non-t digit cannot violate the claim. The logical implication is one-way: menu m does not imply table t.
