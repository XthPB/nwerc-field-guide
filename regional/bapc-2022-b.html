Geometry and numerical methods · BAPC 2022-B

Bellevue

A cross-section of an island is drawn as straight segments between points. From a position on the profile, nearby land can block part of your view of the sea. Find the largest unobstructed sea-view angle attainable on the island.

The observation

A blocking peak can replace the current viewpoint without making the relevant sea angle worse. The optimum is captured by a maximum slope towards the left coast or towards the right coast.

Why it works

Follow an obstructed sightline to its blocking vertex. Its angle towards that same coast is at least as large. Repeating ends at an unblocked maximiser. Thus scan atan2(y_i,x_i−x_first) and atan2(y_i,x_last−x_i), and take their maximum as specified by the viewing-angle reduction.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Test all visibility lines

For each candidate viewpoint, inspect every other vertex for obstruction and compute its sea-facing limits. This exposes the geometric meaning but does too much repeated work.

Baseline · O(n²) time · O(1) space

Maintain horizon envelopes

Upper convex chains compact the points that can obstruct a query. Tangent searches answer horizon queries. Useful if the task asked for the answer at every viewpoint, although more machinery is needed than here.

General visibility alternative · O(n log n) time · O(n) space

Exchange to a blocking peak

Use the exchange argument to discard interior visibility checks. Only the two coast-to-vertex slopes can attain the optimum.

Proof-first reduction · O(n) time · O(1) space

Compare slopes exactly

All candidate denominators are positive. Compare fractions by cross multiplication; call atan2 only for the winning fraction. This makes ties and rounding easier to reason about.

Recommended · O(n) time · O(1) space

Streaming the two extrema

If the shoreline endpoints are known, the scan needs only two running slopes. If the right endpoint arrives last, either store the profile or do a second pass. Recognise the difference between an O(1)-state algorithm and an input format that prevents streaming.

Memory refinement · O(n) time · O(1) beyond input space

Boundary checks

Use the actual shoreline x-coordinates, not an assumed origin. The coast vertices have height zero; avoid 0/0. Output degrees. A convex hull is a useful generalisation but unnecessary for this particular maximum.
