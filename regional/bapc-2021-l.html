Greedy, sorting and search · BAPC 2021-L

Lopsided Lineup

You know the synergy score of every pair of players. Split the players into two equally large teams. Each team scores the synergy of pairs within it. Choose the teams to make the difference between their scores as large as possible.

The observation

Sum each row of the symmetric synergy matrix. Cross-team terms appear equally in the two row-sum totals and cancel in their difference. Choosing the team with larger row sums therefore optimises the requested imbalance.

Why it works

Let S and W be the sums of internal matrix entries for the two teams and X the cross-block sum. Their row totals are S+X and W+X; subtracting gives S−W. Since S and W count each unordered internal pair twice, the requested score difference is (S−W)/2. Hence the objective becomes a fixed-cardinality sum of independent row weights, solved by selecting the largest n/2. A player’s row sum is not literally their team’s internal score; cancellation holds for the difference.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Enumerate equal-size teams

Compute both internal scores for each partition. This verifies the algebra on small random symmetric matrices.

Small-instance oracle · O(n²·binomial(n,n/2)) time · O(n) space

Recompute gains for swaps

Swap a low-row-weight player on the strong team with a higher-row-weight player on the weak team. After the cancellation identity, every such swap has a directly known positive gain.

Slow improvement method · O(n³) or worse if implemented naively time · O(n²) space

Sort row sums

Read the matrix and accumulate each row. Sort (weight,index), then select the top half.

Recommended · O(n²+n log n)=O(n²) time · O(n) beyond streamed matrix space

Selection with nth_element

Only membership in the top half matters, not their internal ordering. Partition at the median weight, sum the two halves, and divide their difference by two.

Selection alternative · O(n²) input, expected O(n) selection time · O(n) space

Maintain the strongest half with a heap

A min-heap of size n/2 keeps the selected players while row sums arrive. Matrix input already dominates runtime; this variant teaches fixed-cardinality streaming selection.

Streaming alternative · O(n²+n log n) time · O(n) space

Boundary checks

The output is the maximum numeric difference, not a team assignment. Divide the difference of selected and unselected row sums by two, because the statement counts unordered pairs. Use 64-bit sums; symmetry and zero diagonal make the identity valid.
