Knowing the Clock

Given integer clockwise angles h and m of an hour and minute hand, determine whether they can describe the same time.

The observation

The minute hand moves twelve times as fast as the hour hand. Angles live modulo a full turn.

Why it works

When the hour hand has advanced h degrees from midnight, the minute hand has advanced 12h degrees, modulo 360. Thus m≡12h (mod 360) is necessary. It is sufficient because choosing elapsed time 120h seconds realizes the given hour angle and exactly that minute angle.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Simulate every second of twelve hours

Represent angles in half-degree units or as integer numerators so simulation is exact. Integer h corresponds to time 120h seconds.

Valid baseline · O(43200) time · O(1) space

Enumerate minute-hand revolutions

The minute hand may have made k full turns during the hour hand’s turn. Check 12h=m+360k for k=0..11.

Valid alternative · O(12) time · O(1) space

Derive the modular equation

Reduce the twelvefold angle directly. This is the contest solution and a model for replacing simulation with an invariant.

Recommended · O(1) time · O(1) space

Compare elapsed-time congruences

Translate angles into time: h degrees means 120h seconds, while m degrees of the minute hand means 10m seconds modulo one hour. This independent formulation is useful for testing the angle equation.

Alternative derivation · O(1) time · O(1) space

Generalize to coupled rotating gears

For integer speed ratio r, one gear angle determines the other modulo 360 in the same way. If the ratio is rational, retain the full revolution count before reducing; blindly multiplying a reduced angle loses information.

Enrichment · O(1) for an integer speed ratio time · O(1) space

Boundary checks

Do not restrict to whole minutes or integer hours. The hour hand moves continuously. The given integer angles make exact modular arithmetic preferable to floating point.
