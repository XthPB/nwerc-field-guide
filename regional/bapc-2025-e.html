Geometry and numerical methods · BAPC 2025-E

Excruciating Elevators

Choose initial phases for four elevators that move forever between floors 0 and H=10^6. Starting at floor 0, visit at most 35 prescribed floors in order and spend prescribed repair times there. Minimize total completion time.

The observation

Unfold an elevator’s up/down motion into motion around a cycle of length 2H. Then prove an optimal solution can be anchored by tight, zero-wait transitions between elevators.

Why it works

For fixed phases, the earliest elevator arriving in the needed direction gives the earliest arrival at the next required floor; arriving earlier never removes a future option. Normalize one useful elevator to depart floor 0 at time 0. If a group of remaining elevators has no tight dependency leading to this anchor, shifting that group earlier can reduce waiting until a new tight dependency appears. An optimum therefore has a rooted dependency structure. Relabel elevators so each new one attaches to an earlier one: 1·2·3=6 parent choices. Enumerating a repair transition for each of three attachments determines the phases, then simulation evaluates the candidate.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Enumerate all integral phases

After anchoring one elevator, try every phase for the other three. This gives a correct small-world test harness, but H=10^6 makes it unusable.

Tiny H oracle only · O((2H)^3·n·4) time · O(n) space

Sample or optimize phases numerically

Random phases produce upper bounds and help test the simulator. Local search may miss narrow better configurations. Keep this as an experiment that motivates the need for a structural proof.

Heuristic; no correctness guarantee · O(Rn) time · O(n) space

Derive an exact phase simulator

For a leg from a to b, the unfolded departure coordinate is a when going up and 2H−a when going down. Elevator e with phase s_e reaches it after (departure−s_e−time) mod 2H. Take the smallest wait, move |a−b|, then repair.

Essential subproblem · O(kn) time · O(n+k) space

Enumerate tight dependency trees

Choose the six increasing-parent structures and one repair transition for each non-anchor elevator. Each equality fixes a phase difference; simulate the resulting phases. This is the finite search justified above.

Recommended · O(k!·n^k), k=4 time · O(n+k) space

Deduplicate phases and prune simulations

Precompute phase deltas, remove identical candidate phase multisets if useful, and stop a simulation when elapsed time already exceeds the best known completion. These improve constants; they do not create a better asymptotic algorithm.

Engineering refinement · Same worst-case O(k!·n^k) time · O(n+k), or extra cache space space

Boundary checks

This is not a continuous ternary-search problem: the objective wraps and is not unimodal. Normalize negative modulo values. Use 64-bit time because repair durations are large. The proof reducing phases to finitely many tight configurations is the central difficulty.
