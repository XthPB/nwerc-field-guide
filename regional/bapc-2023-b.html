Constructive proofs and implementation · BAPC 2023-B

Battle Bots

A robot starts with a positive size. In one move you may halve its current size exactly, even if the result is fractional, or subtract one. Find the fewest moves needed to reduce its size to zero or below.

The observation

For a fixed number of attacks, the largest destroyable robot doubles with each extra early sword attack. All swords can come before one final claw.

Why it works

Consider the process backwards from size zero: undoing a claw adds 1 and undoing a sword doubles. The first useful reverse operation must add 1; after that, doubling is at least as good as adding 1 once the value is ≥1. Therefore t attacks can handle at most 2^(t−1), and that bound is achieved by t−1 swords followed by a claw. The answer is ceil(log2 n)+1.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Enumerate attack strings

Generate every sword/claw sequence up to the first successful length and simulate exact rational sizes.

Tiny n only · O(t·2^t) time · O(t) space

Reverse reachability DP

Track the maximum size destroyable in t attacks: start at 1 for one attack, then double. This derives the threshold structure directly.

Conceptual baseline · O(t) time · O(1) space

Repeated ceiling halving of the integer threshold

Although physical halving is real-valued, rounding the threshold upward tracks how many halvings are needed before size≤1. It is an exact integer implementation.

Recommended · O(log n) time · O(1) space

Bit length of n−1

ceil(log2 n) equals the number of bits in n−1. Guard n=1 explicitly.

Constant-time word operation · O(1) on 64-bit words time · O(1) space

Binary search attack count

Search the smallest t with 2^(t−1)≥n. This becomes useful when an attack model has a more complicated monotone capacity function.

Alternative generalization · O(log log n) time · O(1) space

Boundary checks

A sword does not floor n/2. Avoid floating-point logarithms near powers of two. n=1 needs one claw, and __builtin_clzll(0) is undefined.
