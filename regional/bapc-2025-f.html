Faulty Connection

Encode one of 600 messages as 30 distinct integers from 1 to 1,000. The receiver may see only two transmitted integers in arbitrary order and must recover the message.

The observation

Treat symbols as points and messages as lines. Two distinct points determine one line; two distinct lines share at most one point.

Why it works

Work over the finite field modulo 31. Assign a message k (zero-based 0..599) to (a,b)=(k mod 31, floor(k/31)). Send the 30 points (x, ax+b mod 31) for x=0..29, encoded as 1+x+31y. They lie in 1..961 and are distinct. Any received pair has different x values, whose difference is invertible modulo the prime 31. Recover a=(y2−y1)/(x2−x1) mod 31 and b=y1−a x1; then k=31b+a.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Disjoint blocks of symbols

Assigning 30 private symbols per message needs 18,000 symbols, beyond 1,000. This failed attempt reveals that messages must share symbols, but never a pair.

Impossible baseline · O(600·30) construction time · O(600·30) space

Random sets with collision checking

Generate a candidate 30-set, reject it if any pair has already occurred, otherwise commit all pairs. It enforces correctness for the codebook found, but there is no guarantee it will reach 600 messages quickly.

Heuristic search · Potentially unbounded time · O(number of used pairs) space

Finite-field lines with a decoding table

Use the line construction, but precompute a map from each possible received pair to its message. This avoids algebra in the receiver and makes the pair uniqueness easy to test.

Efficient alternative · O(600·30²) preprocessing; O(1) decode time · O(600·30²) space

Algebraic encoding and decoding

Decode directly with a modular inverse. Fermat’s theorem gives inverse(z)=z^29 mod 31. This stores no codebook and generalizes to a prime p whenever the alphabet and message bounds permit it.

Recommended · O(30) send; O(log 31) receive time · O(1) space

Projective-plane extension

A projective plane of order p has p²+p+1 points and the same number of lines, with p+1 points per line and one intersection per line pair. For p=31 this gives 993 messages of 32 symbols, within an alphabet of 993. This is a stronger code, unnecessary for the stated bounds; the linked theory explains the points at infinity.

Advanced enrichment · O(p) per message time · O(p²) incidence representation space

Boundary checks

The original uses a multi-pass send/receive protocol; a Codeforces mirror may wrap it interactively. Keep that protocol separate from the mathematics. Avoid 0 as an encoded symbol. Division modulo a prime means multiplying by an inverse, not integer division.
