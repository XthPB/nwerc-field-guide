Jacobi Numbers

You’re given a positive integer n at most 9,241. Output a list of at most 10,000 integers in the allowed range whose cubes sum to n. Any valid list earns full credit, regardless of how many other representations exist.

The observation

Read the output bound before solving a harder optimization problem. Since 1³=1 and n<10,000, n copies of 1 already satisfy everything.

Why it works

The unary construction uses n legal terms and sums exactly to n. More advanced variants are enrichment, not required to pass. A compact identity is (r+1)³+(r−1)³−2r³=6r. Choose c=n mod 6 and r=(n−c³)/6; cubes preserve residues modulo 6, so r is integral. Adding c³ gives n using five cubes, with bases comfortably within the bounds.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Use only ones

This is the simplest complete solution. Resist the temptation to solve the famous minimum sum-of-cubes problem that the statement never asks for.

Recommended · O(n), output-sized time · O(1) streaming space

Greedily remove positive cubes

Repeatedly subtract the largest positive cube not exceeding the remainder. The remainder decreases by at least one, so the term count is at most n. It is valid even though it need not minimize the count.

Valid alternative · O(number of terms·log n) time · O(number of terms) space

Coin-change DP on positive cubes

Minimize the number of nonnegative cubes only. Let dp[s] be the smallest count for sum s; remember which cube gives each optimum. This teaches unbounded knapsack, but optimizes a restricted version.

Enrichment · O(n·cuberoot(n)) time · O(n) space

Meet in the middle for four cubes

Choose a small base bound B, store all two-cube sums, and search for complementary sums. This may produce short representations but is not guaranteed for every n at a chosen B. Fall back to ones if none is found.

Bounded search with fallback · O(B² log B) time · O(B²) space

A five-cube algebraic identity

The identity in the proof gives a constant-size answer for every allowed n. This is a satisfying number-theory exercise after the easy solution, with no search or large table.

Advanced construction · O(1) time · O(1) space

Boundary checks

Do not confuse sum of integers with sum of cubes. Negative bases are allowed. Use 64-bit or __int128 when verifying cubes. Avoid claiming the positive-cube DP minimizes terms when negative cubes are also permitted.
