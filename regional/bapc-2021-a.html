Geometry and numerical methods · BAPC 2021-A

Arm Coordination

A circle has a known centre and radius. You may place a square around it at any angle, as long as the whole disk fits inside. Give the four corners of a square with the smallest possible area.

The observation

The axis-aligned square with corners (x±r,y±r) has side 2r and encloses the circle. Rotation cannot reduce the required distance between opposite parallel sides.

Why it works

Project the circle onto the normal of either pair of square sides. Every projection has width 2r, so any enclosing square has side at least 2r. Our construction meets that lower bound.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Search integer corner coordinates

One could enumerate squares and test containment, but coordinates are huge. A geometric lower bound removes the search.

Unnecessary baseline · Depends on coordinate range; infeasible here time · O(1) space

Optimise the rotation angle

Try orientations and derive the necessary side length from circle projections. Every orientation requires the same side 2r, revealing why numerical optimisation is wasted.

Redundant continuous model · O(I) time · O(1) space

Direct four corners

Use the bounding square and print clockwise or counterclockwise.

Recommended · O(1) time · O(1) space

Generate signs in cyclic order

Store the four sign pairs explicitly. A nested loop over signs can produce a crossing order, so order the pairs deliberately.

Implementation alternative · O(1) time · O(1) space

General support-function viewpoint

For a general convex shape, the width in direction u is max(p·u)−min(p·u). The circle’s constant width makes the problem trivial; polygons may need rotating calipers. This is a broader idea, not another required submission.

Geometry extension · O(1) per direction time · O(1) space

Boundary checks

Print the vertices in cyclic order so the edges form a square. Use 64-bit arithmetic for x±r and y±r. The problem permits any minimum enclosing square; a rotated sample does not force a rotated solution.
