Geometry and numerical methods · BAPC 2021-K

Kinking Cables

Construct a non-self-intersecting polygonal cable of a specified length between opposite corners of a rectangle, keeping nonconsecutive marked points at least one unit apart.

The observation

Create a long unit-spaced vertical snake. Follow a prefix of that snake to a movable point q, then connect q directly to the final corner. As q moves forward, total length changes continuously and never decreases.

Why it works

The prefix grows by distance δ while the final chord can shrink by at most δ, by the triangle inequality. The length starts at the rectangle diagonal and ends at a snake of length at least n·m, covering the promised range. All past snake columns lie on or to the left of q, while the final chord moves right to the destination, avoiding old segments. Unit column spacing and the adjacent-point exception provide the marked-point separation.

From a small instance to the contest constraints

Straight diagonal

The direct segment is optimal for the shortest allowed cable but cannot absorb extra length. It supplies the lower endpoint for the continuous construction.

Only the minimum-length case · O(1) time · O(1) space

Full grid snake

Alternate vertical trips at x=0,1,2,… and finish at (n,m). Its length exceeds every requested length, but printing the full snake overshoots most inputs.

Upper-bound construction · O(n) time · O(n) space

Binary search the snake-prefix parameter

Parameter a=k+f means keep vertices 0…k, move fraction f along segment k, then add the final endpoint. Evaluate this length and bisect a.

Recommended · O(nI), I≈100 time · O(n) space

Locate the final segment, then bisect

Precompute prefix lengths. Scan vertices for the first prefix-plus-chord length at least the target, then binary search only that segment; length evaluation becomes O(1).

More efficient alternative · O(n+I) time · O(n) space

Solve the last bend algebraically

Let R=wanted−prefixLength, D=finish−A and u the unit segment direction. From s+|D−su|=R, squaring gives s=(R²−|D|²)/(2(R−u·D)). Check the degenerate denominator and clamp only tiny roundoff errors.

Advanced numerical alternative · O(n) time · O(n) space

Boundary checks

Check the point separation rule, not merely segment nonintersection. Use at most about 2n+3 points, below 500. Remove duplicate consecutive points at an exact vertex. A generic interpolation between arbitrary paths need not preserve geometric validity.
