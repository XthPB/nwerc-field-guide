Sequences, scheduling and sweeps

Why does the smallest tail work for an increasing subsequence, while a weighted increasing subsequence needs a range maximum?

  • A fixed number of recent decisionsYou can collect coins from houses worth 4, 5 and 4, but adjacent houses have an alarm. What is the most you can collect?
  • Before, inside, and after an operationMultiply one consecutive block of an array by −2 to make a subarray sum as large as possible. How do you stop yourself starting a second block?
  • The cheapest tail for each lengthFind the length of a strictly increasing subsequence of 4, 1, 3, 2, 5.
  • Weights, counts, and two strict coordinatesChain points whose x and y coordinates both strictly increase, maximizing their total reward.
  • Sort by finish and jump to the last compatible predecessorThree jobs pay 5, 8 and 6. Their time intervals are [0,3), [1,4) and [3,5). Which compatible jobs pay the most?
  • Sort events and count open groupsSplit sorted heights into groups and charge each group its tallest height minus its shortest. How can you price unfinished groups?
  • Equivalent suffixes under gcd, AND, or ORFor each right endpoint, count which gcd values occur among subarrays ending there.
  • Find an order that is safe for every chosen subsetTwo apps need temporary installation space as well as permanent storage. In which order can they fit on a small phone?

Test your recognition
A problem awards 50 points for a large end value and 1 for a small one. Does keeping just the smallest tail per subsequence length still work?

Length alone loses profit. Keep the best reward by ending rank and query the prefix of strictly smaller ranks. Recover the source index if a witness is needed.

Groups pay maximum minus minimum. Why does the sweep remember the number of open groups rather than each group’s starting coordinate?

The cost incurred across the next coordinate gap is exactly the gap times the number open. Every completed span is the sum of the gaps it covers. Identities matter only through multiplicities of the next placement.
