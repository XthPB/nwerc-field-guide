Sequences, scheduling and sweeps

Read the sequence 4, 1, 3, 2, 5 from left to right. You may skip entries but must keep the ones you choose in their original order. What is the longest strictly increasing sequence you can build?

Start this topic in reading order → · See the whole reading path

  • A fixed number of recent decisionsA street has houses in a line, each holding a known number of coins. You may take coins from any houses you choose, but taking from two neighbors triggers an alarm. With 4, 5 and 4 coins in order, find the largest total you can take.
  • Kadane’s algorithm and the best subarray ending hereYou’re given a row of gains and losses. Choose one nonempty stretch of consecutive entries and maximize its sum. For the row −3, 4, −1, 2, −5, a stretch may include a loss if it connects two profitable parts.
  • Before, inside, and after an operationAn array contains positive and negative numbers. You may multiply one contiguous block by −2. After this operation choose a contiguous subarray with the largest possible sum. The multiplied block and the subarray you score need not have the same endpoints.
  • Count choices on both sides of a middle positionYou’re given a string of Qs and As. Choose three increasing positions that spell QAQ. The letters need not be consecutive, and two choices count separately if they use different positions. How many choices are there in QAQAQ?
  • The cheapest tail for each lengthYou’re given 4, 1, 3, 2, 5 in that order. You may skip entries but must keep the order of the entries you take. Find the largest number you can take so that each chosen value is strictly larger than the previous one.
  • Count all increasing subsequences by their last valueIn the array 1, 1, 2, choose any nonempty subsequence whose values increase strictly. Count all choices of positions. Taking the first 1 and the 2 is a different choice from taking the second 1 and the 2.
  • Weights, counts, and two strict coordinatesYou’re given points with coordinates (x,y) and a reward at each point. You may visit a sequence of points only if both coordinates strictly increase at every step. Find the greatest total reward. A point with the same x as the previous one cannot extend the chain.
  • Sort by finish and jump to the last compatible predecessorThree jobs occupy the time intervals [0,3), [1,4) and [3,5) and pay 5, 8 and 6. You can do only one job at a time, and may start a new job exactly when another finishes. Choose compatible jobs for the greatest total pay.
  • Find an order that is safe for every chosen subsetYour phone has limited free storage. Each app needs some temporary space to install and then occupies a possibly different amount permanently. You may choose apps and their installation order. Which order lets you test which selections fit without trying every permutation?
  • Sort stackable blocks before doing knapsackEach block has a weight, a value, and a strength specifying the maximum total weight it can support above it. You may choose and order blocks to form one tower. Maximize the total value while keeping every block within its strength limit.
  • Equivalent suffixes under gcd, AND, or ORYou’re given a sequence of positive integers. For each right endpoint r, consider every contiguous subarray ending at r. Find its possible gcd values and how many starting positions yield each one. Numerous starts can share the same gcd.
  • Sort events and count open groupsDivide labeled people with given heights into groups. Each group costs its tallest height minus its shortest. Count groupings that fit a stated total budget. Processing the sorted heights encounters the first and last members of a group at different times.

Test your recognition
A problem awards 50 points for a large end value and 1 for a small one. Does keeping just the smallest tail per subsequence length still work?

Length alone loses profit. Keep the best reward by ending rank and query the prefix of strictly smaller ranks. Recover the source index if a witness is needed.

Groups pay maximum minus minimum. Why does the sweep remember the number of open groups rather than each group’s starting coordinate?

The cost incurred across the next coordinate gap is exactly the gap times the number open. Every completed span is the sum of the gaps it covers. Identities matter only through multiplicities of the next placement.
