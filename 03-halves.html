Meet in the middle

List choices independently on either side of a cut. The important change from lesson to lesson is what makes two partial answers compatible.

Start this topic in reading order → · See the whole reading path

  • Choose the parameter before you splitForty-two items have huge weights. Two hundred items in a different task have a target of only 60,000. Which input should you split?
  • Join two lists of subset sumsWith weights 2, 2, 3 and 5, how many subsets total 5? What if forty weights are far too large for a sum table?
  • Closest, at most, or inside an intervalOne half already contributes 2 and the target is 6. The other half can contribute 0, 3, 5 or 8. How near can you get?
  • Profit under capacity, several resources, and exact sizeAfter choosing the left half of a collection, 3 units of capacity remain. Which right-half selection earns the most profit?
  • Meet on a grid, a hash, or a boundary stateCount right/down paths with a target XOR without enumerating every full path.
  • When the two halves interact through conflictsYou split a set of incompatible guests into two halves. Which information from the left determines whom you may still invite on the right?

Test your recognition
You have 40 large signed numbers and want exactly 12 of them with sum in [A,B]. What goes into the two lists?

Bucket each half’s subset sums by size. For a left sum x with c items, use two binary searches in the sorted right bucket 12−c for [A−x,B−x]. Keep duplicates for counting.

A two-half XOR-path count disagrees only on boards with nonzero diagonal entries. What is the likely failure?

Check whether both path halves include their common cell. If they do, join by right = target XOR left XOR cell and restrict the map to the same cell.
