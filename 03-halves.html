Meet in the middle

Four numbered items weigh 2, 2, 3 and 5. Count the subsets weighing exactly 5. Try making a list of sums for the first two items and another for the last two. What pairs of list entries add to 5?

Start this topic in reading order → · See the whole reading path

  • Choose the parameter before you splitYou’re given item weights and a target, and each item can be used once. Decide whether a subset hits the target exactly. One instance has 42 items with enormous weights. Another has 200 items but a target of only 60,000. The better search depends on which dimension is small.
  • Join two lists of subset sumsChoose any subset of the four labeled items weighing 2, 2, 3 and 5. Count how many subsets weigh exactly 5, keeping the two copies of weight 2 distinct. In the larger version there are around forty items and the target is too large for a sum-indexed table.
  • Closest, at most, or inside an intervalChoose a subset whose total is closest to a target of 6. Suppose you have split the items into two groups. One choice on the left weighs 2, and the possible right totals are 0, 3, 5 and 8. Which combinations come closest, including from above the target?
  • Profit under capacity, several resources, and exact sizeEach item has a weight and a profit, and can be taken at most once. You split the collection into two halves to maximize profit under a weight limit. One left-hand choice leaves 3 units free. Find which right-hand choice gives the most profit that still fits.
  • Meet on a grid, a hash, or a boundary stateThe board contains integers. Walk from the upper-left cell to the lower-right using right or down steps. XOR all values along each path, including both endpoints, and count paths matching a given target. The example has rows [1,2] and [4,8].
  • When the two halves interact through conflictsYou’re organizing a gathering. You know which pairs of guests refuse to attend together, and you want the largest invitation list containing no such pair. After selecting people from the first half, you must still enforce their conflicts with people in the second half.

Test your recognition
You have 40 large signed numbers and want exactly 12 of them with sum in [A,B]. What goes into the two lists?

Bucket each half’s subset sums by size. For a left sum x with c items, use two binary searches in the sorted right bucket 12−c for [A−x,B−x]. Keep duplicates for counting.

A two-half XOR-path count disagrees only on boards with nonzero diagonal entries. What is the likely failure?

Check whether both path halves include their common cell. If they do, join by right = target XOR left XOR cell and restrict the map to the same cell.
