Interval and partition DP

Try choosing the last cut, the last multiplication, or the last deletion. The example gets harder when independent parts can interleave or wait to merge across a boundary.

  • Exactly k consecutive piecesCut 1,2,1 into exactly two nonempty consecutive groups. Minimize the sum of squared group totals.
  • Split on the final merge, pivot, or triangleMerge neighbouring piles 3, 1 and 2 until one remains. Each merge costs the weight you combine. Which order costs less?
  • Choose a matrix parenthesization by the last multiplicationMultiply matrices of dimensions 10×30, 30×5 and 5×60. The result is fixed, but which grouping requires fewer scalar products?
  • Two ends, score difference, and forced turnsTwo players take turns taking a number from either end of 8,15,3,7. How many points can the first player guarantee?
  • Delete, paint, or merge matching endpointsYou may delete a whole consecutive run of equal letters in one move. How quickly can you erase aba?
  • Keep the group that will merge with an interval boundaryRemove consecutive equal colours and earn the square of the number removed. For a,b,a,a, should you immediately collect the final pair?
  • Noncrossing pairs, parsing, and interleavingPair four points on a line using arcs above the line that never cross. How many perfect pairings are possible?
  • Count deletion orders by interleaving independent regionsAt each move delete two adjacent equal letters. How many sequences of position-pair choices can erase aaaa?
  • Cut a circular instance at a justified boundaryAn operation may combine neighbouring pieces of a circular necklace, including its first and last entries. How can an interval DP see that join?

Test your recognition
A reward depends quadratically on how many equal-coloured blocks are removed together. Can the unit-cost deletion recurrence be reused?

Matching a distant block changes the eventual group size and thus its reward. Give the interval a third coordinate for attached equal blocks before attempting any speedup.

A problem counts feasible deletion orders. Fixing the final pivot leaves left and right independent. Is multiplying the two counts sufficient?

Only if the relative schedule between sides is fixed. If L and R moves can interleave arbitrarily, multiply by choose(L+R,L) as well.
