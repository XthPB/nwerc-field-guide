Optimizing transitions

Write the slow comparison first. Different speedups follow from different proofs, such as a monotone split, a line intersection, or a convex sequence of costs.

Start this topic in reading order → · See the whole reading path

  • Evaluate a moving segment cost instead of recomputing itA segment costs one for each pair of equal letters. How do you answer many related candidate-segment costs cheaply?
  • Why the best partition cut moves to the rightPartition a nonnegative array into groups, paying the square of each group's sum. How can you avoid trying every cut at every endpoint?
  • Bound the split between two shorter intervalsMerge adjacent nonnegative piles at a cost equal to their sum. Can previously solved intervals narrow the final split search?
  • A totally monotone transition matrixA DP layer asks for the cheapest column in each row of a large implicit cost matrix. When can most columns be discarded without evaluating them?
  • Turn each predecessor into a lineEach landing at height h pays a fixed fee plus the square of its height change. How can you find the best earlier jump?
  • An envelope with arbitrary insertions or query orderLines arrive in arbitrary slope order and x queries may move backwards. Which line is cheapest at each query?
  • Optimize a ratio by subtracting a trial priceSelect exactly two items to maximize total benefit divided by total time. Their (benefit,time) pairs are (6,2), (4,1), (7,4). How do you compare complete selections without storing all possible totals?
  • Trade a prescribed count for a penaltyA cheap optimizer chooses its own number of groups, but the judge demands exactly two. Can a price per group steer it?
  • Carry a convex piecewise-linear cost functionAdjust 5,1,2 to a nondecreasing sequence at minimum total absolute change. How can you avoid a table indexed by all possible adjusted values?

Test your recognition
Squared segment sums gave monotone split indices on nonnegative input. Can you reuse that proof when negative values enter?

The four-term proof contains a product of two prefix increments. With mixed signs it can be negative. Return to the baseline or prove a different property.

You priced each selected group and the optimal count skipped 6. May you immediately subtract the price times 6?

Only with a proved supporting segment at 6, such as discrete convexity on feasible counts and an appropriate tie penalty. Monotonicity of the chosen count by itself is insufficient.
