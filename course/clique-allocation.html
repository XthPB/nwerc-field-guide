Compress a continuous allocation onto a maximum clique

Distribute K units of a divisible resource among graph vertices. Edge uv earns xᵤxᵥ. Which allocations maximize the total? The input is a small graph but there are infinitely many allocations.

Work through the example

  1. On a three-vertex path A–B–C, moving resource between A and C leaves the gain from their common neighbor B unchanged. With K=4, concentrating two units at A and two at B gives score 4. A triangle can do better by splitting evenly among all three.
  2. In any allocation, pick two nonadjacent positive vertices. Moving resource between them changes the score linearly because there is no product between their two amounts. Move everything to whichever has the larger sum of neighbor amounts. Repeating leaves a clique supporting all positive mass.
  3. On a clique of size s the score is (K²−Σxᵢ²)/2. Equal allocation minimizes the sum of squares and gives K²(1−1/s)/2. This increases with s, so find a maximum clique. Split the vertices in half. Tabulate the largest clique in every allowed mask of the right half. For each left clique intersect its vertices' right-neighbor masks and look up the best compatible right clique.

Why it works

Eliminating a positive nonneighbor never lowers the objective, even on ties. Every left/right pair counted by the lookup is a clique, and the split of a maximum clique appears in the search. This gives both the optimal value and a constructive allocation.

When the conditions change

For right mask S pick one vertex v. The best clique either excludes v or has size 1 plus the best clique in S∩N(v). The smaller masks are already known. With word-sized half masks, subset tables take O(2^⌈n/2⌉) words and O(n·2^⌈n/2⌉) time by a straightforward implementation. Use distinct undirected edges and nonnegative allocations.

Try one change

Would the same proof allow splitting mass evenly on the largest clique if edges had different rewards?

Show the reasoning

The nonneighbor elimination still works. Inside the clique the weighted quadratic no longer simplifies to a single sum of squares, so equal shares can fail.

From idea to code

Precompute common right neighbors while extending each left subset. Look up the best clique contained in that right mask, even when the whole mask is not a clique.
