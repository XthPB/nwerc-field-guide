Probability, expectation and randomization · 13 of 15

Turn a randomized claim into a quantified guarantee

In a fixed query interval, some value x occurs in at least one quarter of the positions. You may sample positions uniformly, then check a candidate’s true frequency. Decide how many independent samples are needed to discover such a frequent value with a chosen confidence.

Work through the example

  1. One sample misses this particular x with probability at most 3/4.
  2. Twenty independent samples miss it with probability at most (3/4)²⁰, about 0.00317. Verify each candidate with exact occurrence counts before accepting it.
  3. For many queries use the union bound on their failure probabilities. If the task requires the smallest qualifying value, the bound must cover missing that particular value.

Why it works

Verification removes false positives; sampling can still miss a true answer. A deterministic count tree avoids this failure in the heavy-hitter problem CF 840D. Las Vegas algorithms instead verify correctness and randomize running time, as in randomized enclosing circle.

Pr⁡(miss in all t independent samples)=(1−p)t\Pr(\text{miss in all }t\text{ independent samples})=(1-p)^tPr(miss in all t independent samples)=(1−p)t

When the conditions change

The estimate assumes uniform independent draws and a fixed input. Identical pseudorandom choices across queries do not make those failures independent, although the union bound still works. A random hash match needs separate collision analysis.

Try one change

If a candidate passes the exact frequency check, have you proved it is the smallest valid candidate?

Show the reasoning

Only if all smaller candidates have been ruled out. Verifying one candidate proves its eligibility alone.
