Range queries and sweeps · 9 of 19

Pay for range modulo updates by counting lost bits

You maintain nonnegative integers in an array. An update can replace a single value or change every value in an interval to its remainder modulo a positive x. Other operations ask for the current sum of a range. You must support all three operations on the same array.

Work through the example

  1. On [2,9,14,3] with x=6, the endpoints already satisfy the update. Only 9 and 14 change, both to small remainders.
  2. Store the maximum and the sum in every segment-tree node. Skip a disjoint node or one with maximum below x. Otherwise descend to the remaining leaves, take the remainder, and recompute both summaries upward.
  3. Charge each changed leaf to a lost binary digit. If a≥x and a<2x, the remainder is a−x<a/2 unless the value is exactly zero after the change. If a≥2x, the remainder is below x≤a/2.

Why it works

A change more than halves the old positive value. One assigned value can therefore cause at most its bit length many later effective modulo changes. The occasional point assignment deposits a new budget of bits at just that leaf.

When the conditions change

For A bounding assigned values and U point assignments, total time is O((q+(n+U)log(A+1)) log n). Use nonnegative values and x>0. Maximum alone cannot reconstruct a range sum after a modulo operation.

Try one change

Can we prune a node whose maximum equals x?

Show the reasoning

No. At least one value becomes zero. The pruning test uses a strict inequality.

From idea to code

Add a maximum field beside the sum in the segment tree. Recompute both after descending into any child that can still change.
