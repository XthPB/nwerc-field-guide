Intersect constraints by keeping only supporting half-planes

A feasible point must satisfy several linear inequalities. Start with a known bounding square [0,3]² and impose x+y≤2. Which points survive?

Work through the example

  1. Clip each edge of the square. Keep an endpoint on the accepted side; whenever an edge crosses the boundary line, insert the crossing point in traversal order.
  2. The new feasible polygon is the triangle (0,0),(2,0),(0,2). A second constraint x≥1 leaves (1,0),(2,0),(1,1).
  3. For many constraints sort directed lines by angle, keeping the tighter of parallel same-direction constraints. A deque can remove boundary lines whose last intersection has become infeasible.

Why it works

Each inequality defines a convex set. Intersecting it with a convex polygon therefore preserves convexity, and a segment crossing the line has precisely one interior/exterior transition. The angle-sorted deque needs final front/back cleanup and explicit parallel-line handling.

When the conditions change

Sequential clipping is simple for bounded polygons; its cost depends on all intermediate boundary sizes. The standard half-plane intersection algorithm is O(n log n) under the chosen degeneracy conventions. Distinguish empty, unbounded, segment and point answers. CSES Point in Polygon is a prerequisite predicate exercise.

Try one change

Can adding one more inequality split this feasible triangle into two separate islands?

Show the reasoning

No. An intersection of convex sets is convex, even when it becomes empty or lower-dimensional.
