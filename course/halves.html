Meet at the boundary where two independent choices touch

Count assignments among forty decisions with a specified total. All 2⁴⁰ full assignments are too many; each half has about one million.

Work through the example

  1. For weights [1,4 | 2,3] and target 6, enumerate left sums 0,1,4,5 and right sums 0,2,3,5.
  2. Left 1 needs right 5; left 4 needs right 2. These are two distinct choices. Add multiplicities when repeated sums arise.
  3. In a grid XOR path, split at a middle diagonal instead of a list midpoint. Join by both the meeting cell and the required residual XOR.

Why it works

Every full choice has one particular left half and one right half. Group one side by the information needed to complete it. In CF 1006F, if both halves include the meeting cell, its value must be XORed once more during the join.

When the conditions change

Typically O(2ⁿᐟ² log(2ⁿᐟ²)) time with sorting and O(2ⁿᐟ²) memory. For modular maxima as in CF 888E, the complement query becomes a sorted boundary search. For many small sum values a pseudo-polynomial bitset can be cheaper.

Try one change

If three left subsets and two right subsets produce a compatible pair of sums, how much do they contribute?

Show the reasoning

Six when counting labeled subsets. Deduplicating the sums would lose five of them.
