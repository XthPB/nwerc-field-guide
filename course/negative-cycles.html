Distinguish any negative cycle from one affecting the target

A route should maximize score and edges may have either sign. An improving cycle might exist in a distant part of the graph. Does that make the requested s-to-t score unbounded?

Work through the example

  1. Negate rewards to turn maximum score into minimum cost. Mark vertices reachable from s and those that can reach t on reversed edges.
  2. A negative cycle reachable from s can lower a distance indefinitely. It affects this answer only if its effect can reach t.
  3. Relax V−1 rounds. A further improvement reachable from s identifies an affected region; propagate that reachability and intersect with the vertices that can reach t.

Why it works

Every optimal finite simple route uses at most V−1 edges. Another strict improvement after that budget certifies a reachable negative-cycle influence. To reconstruct a cycle from predecessor pointers, follow V predecessor steps after a final-pass relaxation before listing a cycle.

When the conditions change

Bellman–Ford costs O(VE). To detect any negative cycle add a super-source with zero arcs or initialize all distances to zero. CF 295B is a different negative/positive-safe relaxation pattern: restore vertices in reverse and allow each as a Floyd–Warshall intermediate.

Try one change

A beneficial cycle is reachable from s but has no route to t. What happens?

Show the reasoning

It cannot improve any completed s-to-t route. The target can still have a finite optimum.
