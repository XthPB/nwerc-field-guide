Number theory and algebra · 10 of 16

Process all divisors that give the same quotient together

Compute ∑ floor(12/i) for i=1…12. Many consecutive i give the same quotient; the number of groups is small even when n is huge.

Work through the example

  1. The quotients at i=1,2,3,4 are 12,6,4,3. At i=5 the quotient becomes 2 and stays 2 through i=6.
  2. Starting at l=7, q=1 and the last index with this quotient is floor(12/1)=12. Charge six terms at once.
  3. In general let q=floor(n/l) and r=floor(n/q). Add the contribution of [l,r] and continue at r+1.

Why it works

There are at most √n small indices and at most √n small quotient values for the rest. Weighted variants multiply q by an arithmetic sum over the block. CF 616E uses n mod i = n−i floor(n/i).

When the conditions change

O(√n) quotient groups. If summing to m>n, handle the zero-quotient tail separately before dividing by q. For divisor sums modulo an even modulus, divide an even factor as an integer before reducing.

Try one change

At l=4 for n=12, why does r equal 4 rather than 5?

Show the reasoning

floor(12/4)=3 and floor(12/3)=4. Starting at 5 the quotient is already 2.
