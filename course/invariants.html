Find a quantity every allowed move preserves

Two piles can lose (2,1) or (1,2) tokens per move. When can both become empty? Trying moves greedily can obscure the impossibility certificate.

Work through the example

  1. If the two move counts are x and y, initial piles must be a=2x+y and b=x+2y.
  2. Solve for x=(2a−b)/3 and y=(2b−a)/3. Both must be nonnegative integers. For (4,5), x=1,y=2 works.
  3. These are also sufficient: schedule the indicated moves. Every prefix consumes no more than the chosen total from either pile.

Why it works

Look for parity, residue, coloring, permutation sign, gcd or a conserved linear combination. An invariant proves impossibility; a witness construction or reversible move sequence proves sufficiency. In CF 1516C, remove common factors of two before choosing a partition-breaking witness.

When the conditions change

For rotations of a permutation (CF 1365C), each matched value votes for one offset, another invariant under a global shift. An invariant need not classify all reachable states. Try a tiny exhaustive search for missing constraints.

Try one change

Does divisibility of a+b by 3 suffice for (1,8)?

Show the reasoning

No. The larger pile exceeds twice the smaller one; one required move count would be negative.
