Test a large integer before trying to split it

You must output the prime factors of positive integers, with their exponents. One workload has many small values and another has just a few values near the 64-bit limit. Choose a method that fits the actual workload and still identifies primality correctly.

Work through the example

  1. For small bounded inputs, sieve smallest prime factors once. Factoring 84 becomes 2,2,3,7 by repeated table lookup.
  2. For a large odd n, write n−1=d·2ˢ and use strong probable-prime tests with a witness set proven for the numeric bound. Passing a single witness is insufficient.
  3. If composite, Pollard rho iterates a map such as x²+c mod n. A nontrivial gcd of differences can reveal a factor. Recurse on both factors, retrying a failed walk.

Why it works

Equal residues modulo an unknown prime factor make a difference divisible by that factor. Rho searches for such collisions without knowing the factor in advance. Wide modular multiplication and explicit small-prime cases are essential to correctness.

When the conditions change

SPF amortizes across bounded queries. Pollard rho has randomized running time and needs retries when gcd=n; validate divisors and primality on the way out. In CF 1225D complementary prime-exponent residues modulo k count compatible pairs; CF 576A uses prime powers as distinguishing queries.

Try one change

If a run of Pollard rho returns n, have you proved n is prime?

Show the reasoning

No. Restart with different parameters or seed and keep a separate primality test.
