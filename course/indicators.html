Probability, expectation and randomization · 1 of 15

Find the expectation without the whole distribution

Reveal three distinct cards in a random order. How many cards are larger than every previous card on average? The record events are not independent.

Work through the example

  1. The first card is always a record. The second is the largest of its prefix with probability 1/2.
  2. By symmetry the third is a record with probability 1/3. Add the three indicators to get 11/6 expected records.
  3. For a node removed by selecting an ancestor uniformly in a random elimination order, ask which ancestor is earliest. CF 280C gives a contribution reciprocal to the number of ancestors including the node.

Why it works

Expectation is additive for every finite sum of indicators, regardless of dependence. Identify the small event for one contribution before trying to describe entire random outcomes. For inversions, use an event for each ordered pair of positions.

E ⁣[∑iIi]=∑iPr⁡(Ii=1)\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_i I_i\right]=\sum_i\Pr(I_i=1)E[i∑​Ii​]=i∑​Pr(Ii​=1)

When the conditions change

For variance, covariance between indicators may matter. For conditional expectations, calculate event probabilities under the actual conditioning. Uniformity of one particular candidate is a proof obligation.

Try one change

If E[X]=E[Y]=1, can you infer E[XY]=1?

Show the reasoning

No. That product needs independence or a separate covariance argument.
