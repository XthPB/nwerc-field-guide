Constructive proofs and implementation · 10 of 12

Construct numbers from an invariant, then check every requirement

Choose distinct nonnegative integers with a prescribed XOR. The XOR equation is easy to satisfy; distinctness is the extra constraint that determines how to finish.

Work through the example

  1. For a simple demonstration, choose three numbers whose XOR is 5. Reserve fresh high bits 16 and 32. Pick 16, 32 and 16 XOR 32 XOR 5 = 53.
  2. Distinct high-bit patterns separate all three and their XOR is exactly 5. When a low-bit prefix is already chosen, substitute the residual XOR for 5.
  3. Check small requested set sizes separately, as well as output bounds and whether zero is permitted. Two distinct numbers cannot have XOR zero.

Why it works

Reserve enough unused degrees of freedom that the repair step enforces one equation without creating duplicate elements. CF 862C applies this construction. CF 1349A is a number-theoretic transfer: quantify pairs via the second-smallest prime exponent, taking absent factors as zero.

When the conditions change

The highest bits must stay within the problem’s allowed range and outside the fixed prefix. Give both an algebraic proof for the objective and a structural proof for distinctness or ordering.

Try one change

If the residual already contains your reserved high bit, is distinctness automatic?

Show the reasoning

No. Choose the high bits beyond all bits of the residual and of every previous value, then recheck the bound.
