Move a boundary only after proving what can be discarded

Find the longest nonnegative segment with sum at most 5 in [2,1,4,1]. Can you move both ends forward just once?

Work through the example

  1. At the third item the window [2,1,4] costs 7. Any future extension also costs at least 7. Discard its first 2.
  2. The surviving [1,4] costs 5. Appending the final 1 costs 6, so discard the older 1.
  3. Each right endpoint now has its earliest feasible start, which maximizes length for that end.

Why it works

The same logic works if validity is “at most k distinct values,” because adding a value cannot reduce the number of distinct values. Track frequency changes as elements enter and leave.

When the conditions change

O(n) pointer moves. When signed values are allowed, dropping an over-budget start is unsafe because a future negative value can rescue it. Use prefix sums and the relevant order statistic. CSES Subarray Sums I asks for an exact positive-array sum.

Try one change

With [6,−2], what mistake would the nonnegative proof make at the first element?

Show the reasoning

It discards the 6 immediately and misses the length-two sum of 4.
