Probability, expectation and randomization · 12 of 15

Aggregate retries while admitting destinations in cost order

At each attempt a ticket may be available to one or more destinations. You see which are available and may wait. Why would you ever reject an available destination?

Work through the example

  1. Suppose a fast destination has future cost 0 and is available with probability 1/2. If it is absent, you can retry tomorrow. Waiting for just that destination takes 2 attempts on average.
  2. Now suppose a slow destination always exists with future cost 10. Taking it on a failed fast attempt would cost more than the average restart value; waiting is better.
  3. Sort candidate destinations by their known expected future cost. For an accepted set, choose the cheapest available member. Keep the probability q that none are available and the weighted sum W of accepted future costs.

Why it works

First-step conditioning gives E=1+W+qE, hence E=(1+W)/(1−q). Adding independent availability p for a new worst-ranked candidate adds q·p·E_new to W and multiplies q by 1−p. It improves the tentative cost exactly when its future cost is lower than the old tentative value.

When the conditions change

CF 605E finalizes the smallest tentative expected time first, in a Dijkstra-like order, updating incoming choices. A dense implementation costs O(n²). Independence across tickets and fresh attempts are assumptions of the model. No available success probability gives an infinite estimate.

Try one change

With both example destinations, should the slow one be added to the accepted set?

Show the reasoning

No. Its future cost 10 exceeds 2. Numerically, accepting it produces expected cost 1+(1/2)·10=6.
