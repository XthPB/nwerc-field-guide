Find a common divisor by sampling one of its multiples

Maximize a divisor that divides at least half of the array. The values are much larger than n, so sieving every possible divisor is impractical. Why sample values when the desired answer is a divisor?

Work through the example

  1. In [12,18,30,25,49], the optimal divisor is 6. If you sample 18, every possible answer dividing the anchor belongs to {1,2,3,6,9,18}. Gcd the anchor with every value. A gcd divisible by 6 certifies a multiple of 6.
  2. Histogram these gcds over the anchor's divisors. For each candidate d sum the counts of histogram keys divisible by d. This can be done by a descending divisor-lattice sum along one prime dimension at a time. Keep the largest d meeting the exact threshold.
  3. Sample k anchors independently and take the best verified candidate. At least half the indices are multiples of an optimal answer, and each such anchor exposes that answer. Missing all of them has probability at most 2⁻ᵏ.

Why it works

Each candidate that passes has been counted against the entire input. Randomness affects whether the optimum enters the candidate pool. A false positive cannot result from unlucky sampling.

Pr⁡[all k anchors miss]≤(1−α)k\Pr[\text{all }k\text{ anchors miss}]\le(1-\alpha)^kPr[all k anchors miss]≤(1−α)k

At least an α fraction of array indices are divisible by the optimum. Here α is at least one half.

When the conditions change

Per anchor, n gcds, divisor enumeration or factorization, and O(τ(a)·ω(a)) divisor-lattice additions once the divisor grid is indexed. Use the ceiling of n/2 as the threshold. Uniform independent sampling with replacement yields the stated bound.

Show the reasoning

If an answer divides only an α fraction of indices, the probability all k anchors miss it becomes (1−α)ᵏ.

From idea to code

After factoring the sampled value, number divisors by their exponent vectors. A suffix sum along each prime dimension aggregates all multiples of that divisor.
