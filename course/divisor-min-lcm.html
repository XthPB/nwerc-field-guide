Find the minimum LCM by scanning multiples

You’re given an array of positive integers. Return two different positions whose values have the smallest possible least common multiple, the smallest positive number divisible by both. Equal values are eligible when they occur at different positions.

Work through the example

  1. On [6,10,15,21], for divisor 3 the two smallest divisible values are 6 and 15, giving LCM 30. For divisor 2 we get 6 and 10, also giving 30. Several best pairs are possible.
  2. For each d up to the maximum value A, scan d,2d,3d,… in increasing order until two occupied values have been collected. A value occurring twice can supply both indices. Evaluate their actual LCM and keep the best result.
  3. For the proof, take an optimal pair a,b and set d=gcd(a,b). The two smallest available multiples u,v of d have uv≤ab, while gcd(u,v)≥d. Therefore lcm(u,v)≤ab/d.

Why it works

The iteration containing the optimal gcd supplies a pair at least as good as the optimum. Other iterations may use a d smaller than the actual gcd of their selected pair. Computing the true LCM makes every reported candidate valid.

lcm(u,v)=uvgcd⁡(u,v)≤abd\mathrm{lcm}(u,v)=\frac{uv}{\gcd(u,v)}\le\frac{ab}{d}lcm(u,v)=gcd(u,v)uv​≤dab​

At the optimal gcd d, u and v are the two smallest available multiples and a,b are the optimal pair.

When the conditions change

O(n+A log A) time in the worst case and O(A) space. Retain two occurrence indices for duplicates. Divide by the gcd before multiplying, with a sufficiently wide integer type.

Try one change

Why not simply choose the two smallest input values?

Show the reasoning

In [10,11,20], 10 and 20 have LCM 20 while 10 and 11 have LCM 110.

From idea to code

A frequency array indexed by value supplies the two smallest occupied multiples. Store original indices separately to return the selected pair.
