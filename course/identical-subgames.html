Cancel pairs of identical piles before evaluating a split

A pile move can subtract one or split an even pile of size 2x into k identical piles of size x. Pile sizes can reach a billion. How do you avoid building a table up to the largest pile?

Work through the example

  1. Independent piles combine by XOR. Splitting into k copies of a position with Grundy value g yields zero if k is even and g if k is odd. For k even, the first values at sizes 0 through 4 are 0,1,2,0,1.
  2. For even k every odd size at least 3 has value zero and every even size at least 4 has value one. Prove the pair together by induction using mex, the smallest nonnegative value missing among legal successors.
  3. For odd k the values at sizes 0 through 4 are 0,1,0,1,2. Odd sizes at least 5 have value zero. At an even n≥6 the successors have values 0 and g(n/2), so g(n) is 2 when g(n/2)=1 and 1 otherwise. Recurse only on the half-size.

Why it works

At odd sizes there is just the subtract-one move. For odd k, once even piles of size at least four have positive Grundy values, the odd neighbors above them have value zero. The next even pile has a move to zero and so is positive, closing the induction.

When the conditions change

Start from the small sizes explicitly. Compute the XOR over the original piles; zero is losing under normal play. The cost is O(log A) per pile for odd k and constant for even k. This cancellation uses independent subgames.

Try one change

For odd k, does splitting into k identical piles always give Grundy value zero?

Show the reasoning

No. Every pair cancels, leaving one copy when k is odd.
