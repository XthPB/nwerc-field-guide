Games and interactive reasoning · 6 of 12

Look for independent pieces after a move

Players alternate taking two adjacent cells out of one remaining strip. Removing them leaves separate strips to their left and right, and later turns may choose any strip. A player unable to remove a pair loses. Determine whether the opening player can force a win.

Work through the example

  1. Lengths 0 and 1 have no move, so g=0. A length-2 strip can leave only (0,0), giving g(2)=mex{0}=1.
  2. At length 4, removing a central pair leaves lengths 1 and 1 with XOR 0. Removing an end pair leaves 0 and 2 with XOR 1. Its Grundy value is mex{0,1}=2.
  3. For every cut try the XOR of the two child Grundy values. Take the mex over those options; XOR again across strips that were independent before the turn.

Why it works

Removing cells reduces total playable length, so the local game is finite. The sum rule applies only if no later move can cross a removed gap. Prime-component and restricted-pile games use different move definitions but the same independence test.

When the conditions change

Direct tabulation to length N takes O(N²) time. Do not extrapolate a period from a few terms without a proof sufficient for the input range. CF 768E also restricts repeated move sizes within a pile, changing the state.
