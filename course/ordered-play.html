Do retrograde analysis on a product of two positions

Two tokens start at different vertices of a labeled directed graph. Players alternate moving their own token and must use a letter at least as large as the previous edge’s letter. One token position alone no longer specifies the game.

Work through the example

  1. Use state (u,v,c): the current player is at u, the other player is at v, and c is the minimum allowed letter.
  2. A move u→w with letter d≥c hands the turn to the other player, giving state (v,w,d). With no such move the current player loses.
  3. On the contest’s directed acyclic graph, both token moves advance along a topological order. The sum of those two orders increases, so the recursion terminates even when the same letter repeats.

Why it works

A winning state has at least one losing successor. The product state captures both locations and the shared lower bound. The tokens’ moves interact through the letter rule, so independent-game XOR is inappropriate.

When the conditions change

With alphabet A there are O(AV²) states, with a straightforward O(A V E) transition bound from scanning outgoing edges. On a cyclic graph you would need draw-aware analysis and a precise infinite-play rule.

Try one change

Why reverse u and v in the recursive call?

Show the reasoning

The perspective changes with the turn. The player now to move owns the previously stationary token.
