Strings and pattern matching · 2 of 11

Detect periods and compare cyclic shifts without copying every rotation

A circular word “ababab” has six starting positions. How many distinct rotations can it have and how do you search for them in a stream?

Work through the example

  1. The longest proper border has length 4, so the candidate period is 6−4=2. It divides the word length, so the word is three copies of “ab”.
  2. Starting at offsets 0 and 2 gives the same rotation. Only offsets 0 and 1 produce distinct rotations here.
  3. Every length-n rotation appears as a length-n substring beginning before n in word+word. An exact matcher or suffix structure can process those substrings without materializing all of them.

Why it works

For exact repetition, the prefix-function candidate p=n−π[n−1] works precisely when p divides n. Otherwise all n rotations may be distinct even though the linear word has a short border. CF 235C asks for the occurrences of distinct pattern rotations inside another text.

When the conditions change

O(n) to find the period. Minimum rotation can use a linear-time two-candidate elimination algorithm or suffix ordering on the doubled string with a length-n comparison. Handle periodic ties by the statement’s index convention.

Try one change

“ababa” has a border of length three. Is it a whole number of copies of a length-two word?

Show the reasoning

No. Two does not divide five. A linear period and an exact circular repetition are different requirements.
