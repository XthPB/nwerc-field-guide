Combinatorics and counting · 3 of 9

Count prescribed cycles, fixed points and inversions

How many permutations of four labeled items have no fixed point? Treating each item as having three independent choices ignores the bijection constraint.

Work through the example

  1. Start with 4!=24. For each chosen fixed position, the remaining positions permute freely. Subtract 4×3!=24.
  2. Add back 6×2!=12 for two forced fixed positions, subtract 4×1!=4 for three, and add 1 for all four. Result 9.
  3. For large n derive D(n)=(n−1)(D(n−1)+D(n−2)) by considering the partner of the first item and whether they make a 2-cycle.

Why it works

With a chosen set of f fixed positions, use a derangement on the rest when you require exactly f. Other permutation statistics need different insertions: adding the largest element can create any number of new inversions from 0 through n−1.

When the conditions change

One recurrence gives O(n) time and O(1) rolling memory. For restricted allowed positions use matching, inclusion–exclusion, or a subset DP depending on n and the restriction pattern. CF 285E counts positions with a specific displacement and uses structured inclusion–exclusion.

Try one change

Why does choosing one nonself destination per element give 3⁴ instead of 9?

Show the reasoning

The chosen destinations can repeat. A permutation must use each destination once.
