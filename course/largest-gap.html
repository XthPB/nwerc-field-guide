Range queries and sweeps · 2 of 19

Maintain the one gap you can leave uncovered

Cover a changing set of points on a line by two intervals of minimum total length. After an insertion or deletion you need the optimum again.

Work through the example

  1. At positions 1,4,10,12 the total span is 11. Leave the gap of length 6 between 4 and 10 uncovered. The two intervals [1,4] and [10,12] cost 5.
  2. Keep the positions in sorted order and the adjacent gap lengths in a multiset. Inserting 7 removes gap 6 and inserts gaps 3 and 3. The largest gap is now 3 and the cost becomes 8.
  3. For a deletion reverse the change. At an extreme there is only one neighboring gap. With zero, one or two points, two intervals of length zero suffice.

Why it works

Sort the two covering intervals and shrink their ends to covered points. They cover a prefix and a suffix of the sorted points, so the only length saved from the total span is one adjacent gap. Any chosen gap gives a valid split.

The positions are sorted, with at least two distinct points in this formula.

When the conditions change

O(log n) per edit. Erase just one occurrence of a gap length. Use a frequency map for coincident points if the problem permits duplicates.

Try one change

What if there are k intervals?

Show the reasoning

Subtract the k−1 largest adjacent gaps, or all gaps if there are fewer. To support many edits, maintain their total as well as their order.

From idea to code

Two ordered containers suffice. Use an ordered set for the points and a multiset for gap lengths, erasing by iterator to remove exactly one copy.
