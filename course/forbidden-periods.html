Strings and pattern matching · 5 of 17

Test every candidate period by finding incompatible distances

A string contains fixed V and K symbols and wildcards. For which shifts p can you assign the wildcards to make every position equal to the position p steps later, when that position exists?

Work through the example

  1. In V??K, the two fixed letters disagree at distance 3. Periods 1 and 3 force them into the same residue class. Periods 2 and 4 work. Checking only pairs exactly p apart misses the failure at p=1.
  2. Construct indicator arrays for fixed V and fixed K. Reverse one and convolve. Use both signs of the offset around the reversal center to mark every distance d at which opposite fixed letters occur.
  3. For each p inspect d=p,2p,3p,… below the string length. Any marked distance rejects p. Otherwise each residue class modulo p contains at most one kind of fixed letter and all of its wildcards can be filled consistently.

Why it works

Equality at shift p propagates through chains. Two positions belong to the same chain precisely when their difference is a multiple of p. Correlation detects all conflicting endpoint pairs in one multiplication.

p works  ⟺  ∀j≥1 with jp<n,conflict[jp]=0p\text{ works}\iff \forall j\ge1 \text{ with }jp<n,\quad\mathrm{conflict}[jp]=0p works⟺∀j≥1 with jp<n,conflict[jp]=0

A conflicting distance occurs between a fixed V and a fixed K.

When the conditions change

O(n log n) time from convolution and the harmonic multiples sweep, O(n) memory. Use a transform modulus larger than the largest coefficient for an exact presence test. A shift p need not divide n for this finite-string definition.

Try one change

Do two positions marked with different wildcard characters necessarily conflict?

Show the reasoning

Only fixed opposite letters create a constraint in this task. Unknown positions are free to take the value of their residue class.

From idea to code

A single V-against-reversed-K convolution gives both orientations. At distance d inspect coefficients on both sides of the zero-offset center.
