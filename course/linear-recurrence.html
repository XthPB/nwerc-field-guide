Combinatorics and counting · 10 of 11

Jump to a distant term with a companion matrix

A sequence has known initial values and each new term is a fixed linear combination of the last three. Compute its term at index 10¹⁸ under the problem’s arithmetic. The worked recurrence adds the previous term and the term three positions back.

Work through the example

  1. For F(n)=F(n−1)+F(n−3), keep the vector (F(n),F(n−1),F(n−2)).
  2. The first row of the transition is (1,0,1); rows two and three shift the history. Multiplying by this same matrix advances one step for any vector.
  3. Square the transition repeatedly and apply powers for the set bits of the jump length. Check small indices against the stated initial values.

Why it works

Fixed linear evolution composes associatively. CF 1117D gives a recurrence with a long jump. CF 621E evolves counts of decimal residues, with the digit choices encoded in the transition multiplicities.

When the conditions change

O(k³ log n) dense matrix time for order k. Polynomial reduction of xⁿ modulo the recurrence polynomial can give O(k² log n). Arbitrary max or min in the recurrence does not obey ordinary matrix arithmetic; some problems have a different semiring formulation.

Try one change

What changes for an affine update with a constant +7?

Show the reasoning

Append a permanent coordinate equal to 1. A column multiplying that coordinate supplies the constant.
