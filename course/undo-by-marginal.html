Greedy, sorting and search · 2 of 15

Buy the largest available decrease first

A weighted edge lies on the route to c leaves. Halving its weight w saves c·(w−floor(w/2)) units of total root-to-leaf distance. How many operations reach a budget?

Work through the example

  1. For w=7,c=2, the first saving is 8, then 4, then 2. For w=8,c=1, the first saving is 4.
  2. Each edge produces a nonincreasing stream of marginal savings. Its next saving becomes available only after the current one.
  3. Use a max-heap of current stream heads. Taking 8 then 4 is at least as good as postponing 8. Stop once the needed cumulative saving is reached.

Why it works

Any unseen gain farther down a stream is at most its current head. Thus the largest available head is also a largest remaining gain overall, and taking it unlocks a legal next step. A subtree leaf count supplies the multiplier.

When the conditions change

O((n+operations)log n), plus a traversal to count leaves. If each operation costs one or two coins, prepare cumulative savings separately by cost class and search over their combination. A single heap ordered only by absolute gain then loses the cost constraint.

Try one change

Could an increasing marginal-gain stream break the argument?

Show the reasoning

Yes. A small head might unlock a huge future gain, so taking the largest currently visible head could miss the best combination.
