Know when an exponent may be reduced modulo a period

Why can reducing an exponent modulo φ(m) change a modular power? Consider 2⁴ modulo 8. The exponent 4 is zero modulo φ(8), but 2⁰ is 1 while 2⁴ is 0.

Work through the example

  1. Separate prime powers dividing the modulus. Modulo pᵃ, write the base as pᵗ·u with u coprime to p.
  2. If t>0 and t·e≥a, the power vanishes modulo pᵃ. Otherwise you need the real small exponent for its p-adic part. For coprime u, a valid multiplicative period reduces its exponent.
  3. For 2ᵉ mod 8 you need only distinguish e=0,1,2 from e≥3. Combine prime-power residues with CRT.

Why it works

Compute both a modular exponent and enough capped magnitude to decide when noncoprime factors vanish. This is the missing information in a naive totient-chain solution. CF 906D applies the principle to power towers.

When the conditions change

A positive modulus of 1 returns zero immediately. Handle base 0 and exponent 0 according to the problem convention. CF 1182E converts a multiplicative recurrence to linear recurrences on exponents under its modular assumptions.

Try one change

Why is keeping just e mod 4 insufficient for 2ᵉ mod 8?

Exponents 0 and 4 share that residue, but their powers are 1 and 0.
