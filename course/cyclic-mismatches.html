Index the exceptional days in two periodic sequences with CRT

Two periodic sequences display colors. Each list contains no repeated color within itself. Find the day of the kth mismatch. Their common period can be too long to simulate.

Work through the example

  1. Use zero-based days. For A=[red,blue] and B=[blue,red,green], red coincides when t≡0 mod 2 and t≡1 mod 3, giving t=4 mod 6. Blue coincides at t=3 mod 6. Thus exactly four of the first six days mismatch.
  2. For each shared color, find its two positions i,j. Solve t≡i mod n, t≡j mod m. A solution exists only when gcd(n,m) divides j−i. When it exists, record its residue in [0,lcm(n,m)). Sort those exceptional residues.
  3. For the first D days, count complete periods plus residues strictly below D modulo the period. Subtract the match count from D and binary-search the smallest D whose mismatch count reaches k. The answer in one-based day numbering is D.

Why it works

Inside one period each shared color has at most one coincidence. Two different colors cannot own the same day. Counting the sparse exceptional matches and taking a complement can be much cheaper than enumerating mismatches.

When the conditions change

For h shared colors, O((n+m)+h(log max(n,m)+log h)+log D·log(h+1)) time with hashed position maps. Prevent overflow in the period and search bound. A generalization with identical infinite sequences has no kth mismatch.
