Match two half masks by a vector of differences

You’re given several nonnegative 30-bit integers. Choose one mask and XOR it into every input. Afterward all the results must contain the same number of set bits. Find such a mask or report that none exists. The common popcount is not specified in advance.

Work through the example

  1. For 4-bit inputs 0000,0011 and 1100, split into 2+2 bits. A low mask 01 produces low-half counts (1,1,1). A high mask 01 also produces counts (1,1,1). Their concatenation 0101 gives total counts (2,2,2).
  2. Enumerate low masks. For each, compute the counts for all inputs and store the vector of differences from input 0, together with one producing mask. Do the same for each high mask and look up the negated vector.
  3. Concatenate a matching high and low mask. The two difference vectors cancel coordinate by coordinate. You never had to guess the final popcount, and one witness per low vector suffices.

Why it works

Popcount is additive across disjoint bit positions. Subtracting a common reference eliminates the unknown target. Meeting on a vector enforces simultaneous agreement with every other input.

(ℓi−ℓ0)+(hi−h0)=0(1≤i<n)(\ell_i-\ell_0)+(h_i-h_0)=0\qquad(1\le i<n)(ℓi​−ℓ0​)+(hi​−h0​)=0(1≤i<n)

ℓ and h are the low- and high-half popcounts after applying their respective masks.

When the conditions change

At 30 bits there are 2¹⁵ masks per side. With exact vectors as hash-table keys, expected O(n·2¹⁵) time and O(n·2¹⁵) storage. Verify actual vector equality after hashing. A single unchecked fingerprint may mistake unequal constraints for equal ones.

Try one change

Would equal sums of the difference vector entries suffice?

Show the reasoning

No. Differences +1 and −1 would cancel while leaving two inputs with different bit counts.

From idea to code

Replace the scalar half-sum key by an exact vector of popcount differences. Keep one low mask for each key to reconstruct a witness.
