Greedy, sorting and search · 14 of 15

Put gains first and order losses by the exit requirement

You start with a limited energy supply. Each quest requires a minimum energy to begin and changes your energy when finished. You want to complete as many quests as possible. If one needs 7 energy and consumes 4 and another needs 5 and consumes 1, their execution order can decide whether both fit.

Work through the example

  1. With 8 energy, attempt the first quest then the second. Energy drops to 4 and the second becomes impossible.
  2. Reverse the order. Starting at 8 gives 7 after the second quest and then 3 after the first. Both fit.
  3. For a loss d and requirement a, raise a to at least d if finishing must be nonnegative. Compare loss jobs by decreasing a−d.

Why it works

For two loss jobs in either feasible order, the stricter exit cushion a−d can go first without increasing their required starting energy. Do reachable nonnegative-gain jobs by increasing requirement, then process losses in this order. If skipping a costly loss is allowed, keep the highest remaining energy at each number completed.

When the conditions change

The maximum-count version uses a DP over count after sorting. This is a frequent greedy + DP combination: fixing an order removes permutations, and DP still chooses the subset.
