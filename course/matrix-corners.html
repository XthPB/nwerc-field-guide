Range queries and sweeps · 6 of 19

Recognize row-plus-column matrices after rectangle edits

Can you match two submatrices by adding arbitrary constants to individual rows and columns? The matrices also receive rectangle additions between queries. What local pattern would every legal match preserve?

Work through the example

  1. Use the difference D=A−B. The block [1 3; 4 6] has bottom-right mixed difference 6−4−3+1=0. It equals a row offset plus a column offset. Change the 6 to 7 and the mixed difference becomes 1, certifying failure.
  2. At each cell compute E[i,j]=D[i,j]−D[i−1,j]−D[i,j−1]+D[i−1,j−1]. Adding x to rows a..b and columns c..d changes only (a,c), (b+1,c), (a,d+1), (b+1,d+1), with signs +,−,−,+. Ignore out-of-grid corners.
  3. Store E exactly and keep a 2D Fenwick tree of indicators that E is nonzero. Query those indicators in rows a+1..b and columns c+1..d. A count of zero accepts. Update the tree only when a corner switches between zero and nonzero.

Why it works

Zero mixed differences make adjacent horizontal differences identical in all queried rows. Starting from the top row and left column therefore reconstructs every cell as the sum of a row constant and a column constant. Corners on the top or left boundary of the query impose no constraint.

When the conditions change

O(n²) memory, O(log² n) per edit or query. Initialize with prefix sums or point additions in O(n² log² n). A one-row or one-column query always succeeds. Summing signed E is insufficient because two failures can cancel.

Try one change

Can the two matrices differ in every cell and still match?

Show the reasoning

Yes. A nonzero constant on every cell is a legal row or column adjustment and creates zero internal mixed differences.

From idea to code

With the half-open FenwickGrid companion, a zero-based inclusive query (a,c)..(b,d) uses sum(a+1,c+1,b+1,d+1) for the interior.
