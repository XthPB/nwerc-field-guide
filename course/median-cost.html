Range queries and sweeps · 11 of 19

Keep enough information to price the distance from a median

A window of fixed width moves through a numeric array. In each window, choose one target and change every entry to that target, paying its absolute distance moved. Return the smallest total cost for each window. Start with values [1,4,9].

Work through the example

  1. Moving the target right by one raises the cost for values on the left and lowers it for values on the right. A median balances the two counts; here m=4.
  2. Maintain two ordered multisets split at a median and keep each side’s sum. The left cost is m·countLeft−sumLeft. The right cost is sumRight−m·countRight.
  3. For [1,4,9] the answer is 3+5=8. Remove 1 and insert 10, rebalance, and [4,9,10] costs 5+1=6 at m=9.

Why it works

The discrete slope of the absolute-deviation sum changes sign at a median. With weighted items use a weighted median. With a quadratic cost, the mean becomes relevant and this median proof no longer applies.

When the conditions change

Each insertion, removal and rebalance costs O(log n). A compressed Fenwick pair for counts and sums gives the same queries. Use a unique ID in an ordered set or erase only one duplicate.

Try one change

For an even-sized window do you have to output the arithmetic mean of the two middle values?

Show the reasoning

Every target between them minimizes the total absolute distance. Follow the task’s convention for reporting the median itself.
