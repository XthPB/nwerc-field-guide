Recover all feasible widths with one convolution

Two horizontal rails are connected by vertical rungs at given x coordinates. A lap is a simple closed route. For each race length, find its largest divisor that could be a lap length.

Work through the example

  1. With rungs at 0,2,5 and height 3, the possible positive widths are 2,3,5. The lap lengths are therefore 10,12,16. For a race of length 60 the answer is 12.
  2. Put a 1 at each rung in a coordinate indicator A. Reverse a copy around the maximum coordinate X and convolve. Coefficient X+d counts pairs of rungs a distance d apart. For each positive d with a nonzero count mark 2(d+height) feasible.
  3. Enumerate divisors per query, or sweep multiples of each feasible length up to the largest race length and remember the largest feasible divisor of each total.

Why it works

A simple cycle must switch between the rails at the two endpoints of a horizontal interval. The inside rungs cannot create an extra detour without revisiting a vertex. Reversing one indicator converts ordinary sums of indices into original coordinate differences.

When the conditions change

For coordinate span X, the convolution costs O(X log X) time and O(X) space. Use an NTT modulus large enough that a positive coefficient cannot vanish, or use exact convolution. The divisor stage has its own cost, bounded by O(M log M) for a multiples sweep to M.

Try one change

Why exclude distance zero?

Show the reasoning

Reusing one rung cannot produce a simple lap. An indicator correlates every occupied position with itself at zero.

From idea to code

For coordinate span X read coefficient X+d after reversing one indicator. Convert only positive widths to lap lengths before looking at divisors.
