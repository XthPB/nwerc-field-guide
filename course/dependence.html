Probability, expectation and randomization · 6 of 15

Distinguish independent samples, exclusive outcomes and posterior information

A bag holds two red cards and one blue card. Draw two cards uniformly without putting the first back. Find the probability both are red. Then ask for the chance of red on the second draw after observing the first card’s color.

Work through the example

  1. Unconditionally the first card is red with probability 2/3. Given a red first card, the second is red with probability 1/2. Multiplying gives 1/3 for two reds.
  2. The marginal chance that the second is red is still 2/3 by symmetry, but multiplying the two marginals would give the wrong intersection.
  3. For indicators X,Y, E[XY] is this intersection probability. Their covariance is 1/3−4/9=−1/9.

Why it works

Condition on the observation before updating counts. In general Bayes weights each possible hidden state by prior × likelihood and renormalizes by their total. A sum’s variance includes twice each pairwise covariance. A distribution or second moment can retain what an average loses.

When the conditions change

For batches without replacement use hypergeometric probabilities; for independent Bernoulli trials use binomial or Poisson-binomial according to whether probabilities match. CF 261B uses a random permutation and subset counts; CF 626D explicitly combines independent selections.

Try one change

Given that at least one of the two cards is blue, can both be red?

Show the reasoning

No. The observation removes all red-red sample outcomes and changes the conditional distribution.
