Split an expected retry time at a moving checkpoint

A route advances one station at a time. Each failed attempt sends you to the latest checkpoint. A query inserts or removes a checkpoint. Which numbers summarize a stretch of route?

Work through the example

  1. Take two stations, each passing with probability ½. An attempt to pass both spends one step for sure and a second step half the time. Its expected length is A=3/2 and its success probability is P=1/4.
  2. Without an internal checkpoint, failure restarts the same stretch, so E=A+(1−P)E and E=A/P=6. Add a checkpoint between the stations. Each station now takes two steps on average, bringing the total down to four.
  3. Precompute products and sums for interval evaluation, or store pairs (P,A) in a segment tree. Joining L then R gives P=PᴸPᴿ and A=Aᴸ+PᴸAᴿ. Maintain checkpoint indices in an ordered set. A toggle replaces one interval cost by two or two by one.

Why it works

The right stretch is attempted only when the left stretch succeeds. This proves the merge rule. Once a checkpoint has been reached, a later failure cannot erase the time already needed to reach it, so expected costs add across checkpoints.

When the conditions change

The contest has positive probabilities represented modulo a prime. In real arithmetic a zero success probability makes the required retry time infinite. Each toggle needs O(log n) time with range queries and an ordered set. Join segments in travel order.

Try one change

If the first station always passes and the second passes with probability ½, what is the value of placing a checkpoint between them?

Show the reasoning

Without it each failed second step replays the first, giving expected time 4. With it the expectation is 1+2=3.
