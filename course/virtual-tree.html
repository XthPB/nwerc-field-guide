Trees and paths · 13 of 14

Retain only marked vertices and the LCAs that join them

A large tree is fixed across many queries. A query lists k important vertices and asks about how their connecting paths fit together. Identify the small subtree structure linking them, retaining where paths meet and the original length of each compressed path.

Work through the example

  1. Sort the marked vertices by DFS entry time. Add the LCA of each adjacent pair. In a query with marked leaves from two branches, this inserts their splitting junction.
  2. Sort and deduplicate again. Sweep the list with a stack of ancestors, popping until the top contains the next vertex. Connect that nearest ancestor.
  3. Compress a chain of irrelevant degree-two vertices into one edge, retaining distance or path information relevant to the query.

Why it works

Every branching junction of the minimal connecting subtree separates two consecutive marked DFS groups, so some adjacent LCA recovers it. The resulting tree has O(k) vertices.

When the conditions change

O(k log k + k log n) with binary lifting. In CF 613D adjacent protected vertices can make separation impossible. CF 1328E is a simpler ancestor-order drill: first move each permitted vertex one parent step and check one deepest chain.

Try one change

If edges carry minimum deletion costs, can a compressed edge store only its length?

Show the reasoning

No. Store the cheapest permitted deletion or whatever summary the original chain contributes.
