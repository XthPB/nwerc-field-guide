Graphs, connectivity and flow · 1 of 27

Put tomorrow’s legal moves in the vertex

You can pay half price on one edge. Two routes reaching the same airport at cost 8 may still have different futures because only one still has the coupon.

Work through the example

  1. For edges S→A at 8 and A→T at 20, using the coupon early costs 4+20=24.
  2. Keeping it at A costs 8 so far but finishes for 18. One distance per airport loses that option.
  3. Create (airport,unused) and (airport,used). Ordinary edges preserve the flag; a discounted edge goes from unused to used.

Why it works

Every original journey has a path of the same cost in the expanded graph and vice versa. Select BFS for equal weights, a deque for 0/1 weights, and Dijkstra for nonnegative general weights. For CF 59E the previous vertex determines which next edges are forbidden.

When the conditions change

Count reachable states and generated transitions separately. A state (previous,current) may have many outgoing neighbors. When expanding every member of a group repeatedly, a shared auxiliary vertex can replace a dense set of edges.

Try one change

Can arrivals with and without a coupon be merged if their costs differ?

Show the reasoning

Keeping the cheaper arrival alone is unsafe. An unused coupon may save more than its current extra cost.
