Merge savings when operations have two different prices

Each edge can be halved repeatedly. Some halvings cost one token, others cost two. How do you buy enough total distance reduction at minimum cost?

Work through the example

  1. First count the leaves below each edge. Halving weight w saves (w−⌊w/2⌋) times that leaf count. On any one edge the successive savings never increase.
  2. Imagine the class costing one token has successive best gains 5 and 4, while the class costing two has one gain 10. To save 14, one cheap and one expensive operation save 15 for three tokens.
  3. Generate the savings for each price separately. Sort each list in descending order and prefix-sum it, starting at zero. Enumerate the number chosen from the first list and binary-search the second list for the remaining target.

Why it works

At a fixed price and a fixed number of operations, a largest available marginal saving is always safe. Sorting all marginal gains gives a legal prefix when equal gains on one edge are taken in their original order. Enumerating the split considers every possible total cost.

min⁡i,j{ i+2j: S1(i)+S2(j)≥required saving }\min_{i,j}\{\,i+2j:\ S_1(i)+S_2(j)\ge \text{required saving}\,\}i,jmin​{i+2j: S1​(i)+S2​(j)≥required saving}

S₁ and S₂ are cumulative savings in their respective price classes. Include the empty prefix in both.

When the conditions change

A single greedy rule based on gain divided by price can overshoot expensively. If T savings are generated, sorting and combining take O(T log T) time and O(T+n) space, with T bounded by the total number of weight halvings.

Try one change

One cheap saving is 6 and one expensive saving is 10. The target is 10. What can a ratio rule do?

Show the reasoning

It can take the cheap 6 first and then the 10, paying three. Taking the expensive operation alone costs two.
