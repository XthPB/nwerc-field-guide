Orient bridge cuts to satisfy many directed routes

You may direct every undirected edge once. Several ordered pairs must each have a directed route. Where can two requests force contradictory choices?

Work through the example

  1. Contract the components obtained after deleting all bridges. Inside each connected bridgeless block a strong orientation is possible. The remaining bridges form a forest.
  2. On the block path A–B–C, requests A→C and C→B both need bridge BC. One needs B→C and one needs C→B. No orientation works.
  3. For a request u→v in one tree, split its path at w=LCA(u,v). Mark the u-to-w branch as upward and the w-to-v branch as downward using two difference arrays. Sum marks from leaves to roots. A bridge with positive demand in both directions is impossible.

Why it works

Every walk between opposite sides of a bridge uses that bridge. If each bridge can take its demanded direction, concatenate these bridge crossings with directed paths inside the strongly oriented blocks. This produces a route for every request at once.

Ue>0 ∧ De>0 ⟹ conflict on eU_e>0\ \land\ D_e>0\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{conflict on }eUe​>0 ∧ De​>0 ⟹ conflict on e

U and D count requests that cross this bridge upward and downward.

When the conditions change

For an upward request increment u and decrement w. For a downward request increment v and decrement w in a separate array. Reject demands between distinct connected components. With binary lifting the cost is O((V+E+Q) log V). Use edge IDs in multigraph low-link code.

Try one change

What if both ends of a request are in the same bridge block?

Show the reasoning

Its difference marks cancel. The strong internal orientation supplies the whole route.
