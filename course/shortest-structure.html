Graphs, connectivity and flow · 2 of 27

Find everything a shortest path can use

You’re given a directed graph with positive road lengths, a source s and a destination t. Identify every edge that belongs to at least one minimum-length s-to-t route. If two routes tie you must account for both.

Work through the example

  1. Use edges S→A and S→B of cost 1, then A→T and B→T of cost 2. Both routes cost 3.
  2. Compute distances from S, then distances to T on reversed edges. For A→T the three pieces total 1+2+0=3.
  3. Keep each tight edge satisfying the same equality. With strictly positive weights these edges go from smaller to larger distance.

Why it works

Shortest prefix plus this edge plus shortest suffix constructs an optimal route when the equality holds. For positive edges, count, minimize, or maximize route properties in increasing distance. For mandatory edges use an exact cut/bridge or dominator argument appropriate to the tight graph.

When the conditions change

Two Dijkstra runs cost O((V+E) log V). Zero-weight cycles can destroy this DAG order; decide whether the task counts simple paths or walks before compressing components. CF 567E also asks how much to reduce an edge weight.

Try one change

Does choosing the first parent on a tie tell you whether an edge is mandatory?

Show the reasoning

No. Removing a chosen parent may leave an equally short route through a different predecessor.
