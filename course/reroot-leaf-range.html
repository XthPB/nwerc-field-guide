Reroot distances while keeping leaf labels queryable

In a fixed weighted tree, eligible leaves have numeric labels. A query gives a starting vertex v and a label interval [L,R]. Return the shortest distance from v to an eligible leaf in that interval. The starting vertex and interval may both change between queries.

Work through the example

  1. Take a root A with children B and C at edge lengths 3 and 5. The leaf distances at A are 3 and 5. Walk across AB. The distances should become 0 and 8.
  2. On crossing a parent-to-child edge of weight w, add w to all leaf distances, then subtract 2w from the leaves inside the child's subtree. In the example add 3 everywhere, then subtract 6 from B.
  3. Keep the distances in a range-add/range-min tree in label order. Bucket queries by their requested root and answer them during a DFS. Undo both updates when returning across the edge.

Why it works

For a leaf below the traversed edge, moving the root removes that edge from the path. For all other leaves it adds the same edge. The contest's preorder-compatible labels put a subtree in one interval while preserving the requested label ranges.

When the conditions change

O((n+q) log n) time and O(n+q) space. You can store only original leaves, with binary search on their sorted labels. A subtree interval in DFS order need not be contiguous in an arbitrary external labeling.

Try one change

Does a vertex become a new eligible leaf when you reroot there?

Show the reasoning

The eligible leaves are defined by the original rooted tree. The traversal changes the distance source only.

From idea to code

Adapt the lazy tree to a minimum aggregate. Save the signed update on each DFS entry and apply its inverse on exit.
