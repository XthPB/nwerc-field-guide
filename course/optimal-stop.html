Probability, expectation and randomization · 7 of 15

Decide with the information available at the time

A fair six-sided die is shown. Either take the displayed value or reroll once and accept the new value. When should you stop?

Work through the example

  1. With no retries left, a fresh roll is worth 3.5 on average. After seeing a value v, compare v with 3.5.
  2. Keep 4,5,6 and reroll 1,2,3. Before seeing the first die, the optimal value is (3×3.5+4+5+6)/6=4.25.
  3. If another retry is available, the new continuation threshold is 4.25. Now keep only 5 and 6.

Why it works

Choose max after conditioning when the choice occurs after seeing the outcome. Move max outside the expectation only if the action must be chosen before the observation. In CF 739E the allocation is planned in advance; overlap reduces a second tool’s marginal contribution. CF 908D practices first-step conditioning.

When the conditions change

Finite independent retries give one backward step per remaining retry, often with a threshold. Random observations that reveal hidden information also require the posterior in the state. A self-loop under a policy needs an absorption or finite-value check.

Try one change

If you must decide whether to reroll before seeing the first value, what is the expected score?

Show the reasoning

Either decision uses a single unseen fair die. The expectation is 3.5; the option to react after observing was worth 0.75.
