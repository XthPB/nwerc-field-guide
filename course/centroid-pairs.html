Trees and paths · 9 of 11

Count a pair at its first separating centroid

Count pairs of vertices three edges apart. At a centroid C one branch has vertices at distances 1 and 2, another has one at distance 1.

Work through the example

  1. Start the seen depth table with count[0]=1 for C. Query all distances from the first branch against the table, then insert them.
  2. At depth 1 in the next branch, look up count[3−1]=count[2]. This finds a pair whose path goes through C.
  3. Recurse inside the separate branches. Pairs within the same branch have not been charged at this centroid.

Why it works

At the first centroid separating two endpoints, the path passes through it and distances add. For nearest marked-node queries, instead store a minimum distance to a mark for each centroid ancestor and query over the vertex’s ancestors.

When the conditions change

Depth-frequency enumeration costs O(n log n) with bounded array resets. Reinitializing an n-entry array per component can erase that bound. CF 161D also admits O(nk) tree DP when k is small. Deletions in a nearest-mark structure require removable minima.

Try one change

Why not insert half a branch and query its other half?

Show the reasoning

Two vertices from that branch might meet below the centroid. Their true distance would be overestimated and the pair could be charged again.
