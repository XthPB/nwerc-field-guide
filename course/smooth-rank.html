Find a ranked product of primes without listing every product

You’re given distinct primes and an integer k. List in increasing order the positive integers whose prime factors all come from that set, including 1. Return the kth integer, allowing each prime any nonnegative exponent.

Work through the example

  1. For primes {2,3}, split the primes into the two groups {2} and {3}. Up to 12 the products in sorted order are 1,2,3,4,6,8,9,12. At threshold 10 there are seven. We can calculate this count without building the combined list.
  2. Generate and sort admissible products for each prime group up to the answer bound U, including 1. For a trial threshold T, scan one list in increasing order and move a pointer down the other list until b≤⌊T/a⌋. Add the number of admissible b values.
  3. Binary-search the least T whose count reaches k. Spread small primes between both sides because they allow many more exponents. Stop a generation branch before its next multiplication exceeds U.

Why it works

Unique prime factorization gives one and only one left/right product representation. The admissible right prefix can only shrink as the left multiplier increases. This makes the count monotone both within the scan and across threshold guesses.

When the conditions change

For generated list sizes A and B, sorting costs O(A log A+B log B), and threshold searches cost O((A+B)log U). Memory is O(A+B). Counts can be saturated at k. Check x≤U/p before multiplying by p.

Try one change

Why do distinct primes matter?

Show the reasoning

If a factor is shared between the groups, the same integer can have multiple decompositions and the rank count overcounts.

From idea to code

Include the empty product in both half lists. In the counting scan use division by the left product before comparing the right product.
