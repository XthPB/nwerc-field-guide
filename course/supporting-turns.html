Commit to a turn by putting all unused points on one side

Visit every point with a non-self-intersecting polygonal chain whose successive turns spell a given L/R string. The next yet-unselected point seems to determine whether today's turn works.

Work through the example

  1. Start at a hull extreme, for instance the lexicographically leftmost point. For a square starting at (0,0), choosing the bottom edge toward (2,0) puts both remaining vertices to its left. Whichever vertex comes next, the first turn is left.
  2. At the current endpoint, pick an unused point so every other unused point lies left of the directed edge when the next required turn is L, or right when it is R. Scan candidates with a cross-product comparison. After all symbols are consumed, append the final point.
  3. In the square a following R can be prepared from (2,0) by choosing (0,2). The last point (2,2) is on the right of that new edge. The resulting chain turns left, then right.

Why it works

The current endpoint is on the boundary of the remaining hull. Each of its two supporting directions leaves the rest on a single side. Its next endpoint is again on the boundary. Every future segment stays strictly on the retained side of an earlier supporting line, except at its adjacent endpoint.

When the conditions change

O(n²) time, O(n) space, with exact signed cross products. The no-three-collinear promise avoids ambiguities over a point lying between two others on the same support.

Try one change

Why can choosing an arbitrary interior starting point fail?

Show the reasoning

Looking from inside a convex hull, the other points can surround you. Neither required supporting direction may exist.

From idea to code

The convex-hull orientation predicate is the needed operation. Starting at an extreme, compare candidates by that predicate once per remaining point.
