Store the effect of a road on the clock

A line of roads has travel times that depend on the time of entry. A road with period p takes two ticks when entered at a multiple of p and one tick otherwise. Queries traverse consecutive roads and updates change individual periods. Return the time a trip takes after the latest edits.

Work through the example

  1. A road with period 2 takes two ticks when entered at an even time and one tick otherwise. A period-3 road behaves similarly at multiples of 3. Six possible entry residues describe either road exactly.
  2. Start at time 0. On roads [2,3], take 2 ticks then 1 tick, for 3 total. On [3,2], take 2 then 2, for 4 total. Reversing the order changes the answer.
  3. Store a table of elapsed times for each interval. To join L then R, look up L at entry time t, advance the time by that amount, then look up R at the new residue.

Why it works

This composition is associative because it describes running the same consecutive roads. Use it as the ordered merge of a segment tree. A leaf can be rebuilt from its road period.

When the conditions change

P is the common multiple of all allowed periods; the contest’s periods 2…6 give P=60. Preprocessing and memory are O(nP), an edit costs O(P log n), and a query from a known time can apply O(log n) ordered canonical nodes in O(log n). The approach needs a manageable P.

Try one change

Why can two independently computed travel times from time zero give the wrong route time?

Show the reasoning

The second road sees the arrival residue from the first road. Starting it again at zero charges a delay at the wrong instant.

def road_table(period, clock=60):
    """The clock must be divisible by the period, which is at least two."""
    return tuple(1 + (t % period == 0) for t in range(clock))


def join_roads(left, right):
    size = len(left)
    return tuple(delay + right[(time + delay) % size] for time, delay in enumerate(left))
