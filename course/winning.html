Mark forced losses, forced wins and draws

A token starts at a vertex of a directed graph. Players take turns choosing an outgoing edge for it. A player unable to move loses, and a cycle may let play continue indefinitely. Classify each starting position as a forced win, a forced loss, or a draw.

Work through the example

  1. Mark terminal vertices losing. A vertex with an edge to a losing vertex is winning.
  2. Each time a successor is proved winning, decrement the parent’s unresolved-move count. If every move leads to a win for the other player, that parent is losing.
  3. Push changes through reverse edges. Unclassified vertices can remain in the unresolved region under suitable play; in the ordinary infinite-play-is-draw model these are draws.

Why it works

The queue computes the forced finite-horizon outcomes in increasing proof depth. For alternating move sets, include the turn or player in the graph vertex. CF 936B has its own win/draw condition and asks for a witnessing path when possible.

When the conditions change

O(V+E). On a finite DAG the same win/lose recurrence works backwards. The draw classification depends on what the game statement says about infinite play. CSES Stick Game is the finite subtraction version.
