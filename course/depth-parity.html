Trees and paths · 3 of 14

Answer subtree questions about one depth with two prefix XORs

Each vertex of a rooted tree carries one letter. A query chooses a subtree and an absolute depth measured from the original root. Take the letters on just those vertices and decide whether they can be rearranged into a palindrome.

Work through the example

  1. Suppose the relevant depth has letters a,b,a in DFS order. The parity mask flips the a bit twice and the b bit once, leaving only b. A palindrome can put b in the middle.
  2. During DFS, record the entry and exclusive exit times of each vertex. For each depth store the sorted entry times and the prefix XOR of one-hot letter masks.
  3. For a query, find the two boundaries of its subtree in the depth's time list by binary search. XOR the two prefix masks. Zero or a power of two means at most one letter has odd frequency.

Why it works

Every subtree is one DFS interval. Intersecting that interval with a depth list takes a contiguous slice. XOR counts each letter modulo two, exactly the information needed to pair off all but a possible center.

m=Pd[r]⊕Pd[l],popcount⁡(m)≤1m=P_d[r]\mathbin{\oplus}P_d[l],\qquad \operatorname{popcount}(m)\le1m=Pd​[r]⊕Pd​[l],popcount(m)≤1

P is the depth-specific prefix parity array, and l and r are indices in that depth layer.

When the conditions change

O(n) preprocessing and O(log n) per query for a word-sized alphabet. An empty slice succeeds. A query for actual counts requires a different aggregate, and dynamic letter changes require an updateable structure per depth.

Try one change

Why are two letters with odd frequency enough to reject a query?

Show the reasoning

Each odd letter needs an unpaired position, but a palindrome has at most one middle position.
