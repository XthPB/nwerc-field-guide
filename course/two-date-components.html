Schedule two-choice jobs by orienting a pseudoforest

Each exam can be taken on either of two given days. Only one exam fits per day. Find the earliest day by which you can finish all exams.

Work through the example

  1. Use days as vertices and exams as undirected edges. Assigning an exam to a day orients its edge toward that endpoint. In the chain 2–5–9, orient away from 9. The exams use days 2 and 5, leaving the most expensive day free.
  2. Count vertices V and edges E in each connected component. E>V makes scheduling impossible. If E=V−1, the component is a tree. Choose its largest day as the unused root and direct all edges away from it.
  3. If E=V every day must be used. Orient the unique cycle consistently, and every attached tree away from the cycle. The whole schedule's finish time is the maximum of the components' required days.

Why it works

Indegree at most one means E different chosen endpoints are required. A tree can leave exactly one arbitrary vertex unused. A connected unicyclic component can use every vertex once. These are all possible connected cases with E≤V, including a two-edge cycle formed by parallel choices.

When the conditions change

Compress the day labels. DSU plus per-component counts and the two largest distinct dates gives O(n log n) total time with sorting. Store the actual day values for the answer. Dates absent from every exam do not constrain the finish.

Try one change

How does this compare with general matching?

Show the reasoning

Each exam has exactly two choices, turning it into one edge. Three choices per job lose the tree/unicyclic orientation shortcut.

From idea to code

When uniting day vertices retain edge count, vertex count and the two latest dates. Count parallel exams as separate edges.
