Range queries and sweeps · 19 of 19

Turn a quadratic transition into a line query

Each prior decision offers a linear cost m·x+b for serving a future request of size x. As choices become available, answer the minimum cost at each requested x. The offers 2x+1 and −x+7 tie at x=2 and favor different choices on opposite sides.

Work through the example

  1. At x=0 the first line costs 1 versus 7. At x=3 it costs 7 versus 4, so the second line now wins.
  2. A Li Chao node keeps the better line at its midpoint. Since two affine lines cross at most once, the losing line can still win on at most one child interval.
  3. Insert it recursively only there. Query all nodes along the root-to-x path.

Why it works

An affine DP transition gives one line per earlier state. When slopes and query x values arrive in suitable sorted orders, a convex-hull deque can be faster. Without those orders use a Li Chao tree on the actual coordinates.

When the conditions change

Coordinate-compressed Li Chao takes O(log M) per add/query. Widen products and handle identical slopes. CF 932F asks about descendant lines, combining envelopes from child subtrees; CF 1083E uses a geometric rectangle ordering.

Try one change

If two candidate curves can cross three times, does keeping the midpoint winner still justify a single recursive branch?

Show the reasoning

No. The other curve can win in disjoint pieces on both sides.
