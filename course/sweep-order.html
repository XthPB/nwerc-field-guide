Count intersections by watching an ordering change

Each of several straight segments has one endpoint on a left vertical line and the other on a right vertical line. Count pairs that cross strictly between the two lines. An intersection at a shared endpoint is excluded from this count.

Work through the example

  1. At the left boundary let the vertical order be A,B,C. At the right let it be C,A,B.
  2. C changes order with A and B, giving two interior crossings. A and B keep their order and never cross in the open strip.
  3. Sort by left coordinate, replace each segment by its right-order rank, then count inversions with a Fenwick tree. Process equal coordinates in batches when touches should be excluded.

Why it works

The difference of two segment heights is affine in x. Opposite strict signs at the two boundaries imply one crossing between them. NWERC 2021 Flatland Olympics uses an analogous change of angular order around two endpoints. CF 598C supplies the polar-order building block.

When the conditions change

O(n log n). The argument does not directly apply to curves that may cross twice. Fully general segment-intersection sweeps must handle event order, exact predicates and coincident geometry separately. CF 429D is a complementary sweep/divide-and-conquer distance exercise.

Try one change

If two segments have the same right endpoint height, should that be a strict inversion?

Show the reasoning

No for interior crossings alone. Decide the boundary convention before compressing tied ranks.
