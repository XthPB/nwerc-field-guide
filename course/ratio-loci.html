Intersect equal-angle loci before optimizing a viewpoint

Three circular objects should appear equally wide to an observer. If several positions work, choose the one with the largest viewing angle. Which quantity can you equate without computing three angles?

Work through the example

  1. For a center at distance d and radius r, the half angle θ satisfies sin θ=r/d by the right triangle through a tangent point. Matching apparent sizes therefore fixes d₁/r₁=d₂/r₂=d₃/r₃.
  2. For two unequal radii, squaring the ratio equation gives a circle. For equal radii it gives the perpendicular bisector. As a numerical example, centers (0,0),(4,0),(0,3) of equal radius 1 have the two bisectors x=2 and y=1.5, so the unique viewpoint is (2,1.5).
  3. Intersect the pair loci for objects (1,2) and (1,3). For each admissible common point compute its three distances, check the equality to numerical tolerance, and choose the smallest common d/r. This maximizes the angle.

Why it works

Expanding |P−A|²=k²|P−B|² leaves a quadratic with equal x² and y² coefficients when k≠1. Its center is (A−k²B)/(1−k²) and radius k|A−B|/|1−k²|. For k=1 the quadratic terms cancel.

P is the observer, Cᵢ is a center, rᵢ is the corresponding radius, and θᵢ is half its viewing angle.

When the conditions change

O(1) intersections. Separate line–line, line–circle and circle–circle cases. Non-collinear centers in the task avoid coincident pair loci. Handle tangency, exterior feasibility and the task's empty-output convention. Use consistent scale-aware tolerances.

Try one change

Why can optimizing distance to one center break a tie correctly?

Show the reasoning

On feasible candidates its radius is fixed and all three distance-to-radius ratios agree, so minimizing that single distance maximizes the common angle.

From idea to code

Use the circle and line intersection routines on the two pair loci. Evaluate all returned candidates because both intersections can satisfy the equal-angle equations.
