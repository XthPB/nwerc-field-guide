Split a range LCM into maxima and distinct prime factors

Answer range LCM queries modulo a prime. The endpoints depend on earlier answers, so offline reordering is unavailable. How can small and large prime factors do different jobs?

Work through the example

  1. Let the maximum input value be 30. In [12,14,21], small primes 2,3,5 contribute maximum powers 4,3,1. The large prime 7 divides both 14 and 21 but contributes once. The LCM is 4×3×7=84.
  2. For each prime p≤√A store range maxima of its exponent, or sparse occurrence lists with range maxima. For primes above √A, a value ≤A can contain at most one such prime and only to exponent one.
  3. Build versions of a range-product tree by right endpoint. On seeing a large prime p again, reset its previous position to 1 and set the current position to p. In version R a product query on [L,R] picks each large prime present exactly once. Multiply by the small maximum powers.

Why it works

A range LCM takes a maximum independently for each prime. At large primes the maximum is just a presence bit. A last-occurrence version represents all these presence bits for every possible left endpoint at once.

When the conditions change

With s=π(√A), straightforward exponent segment trees use O(ns) space and O(s log n) per query, plus O(n log n) persistent nodes. A static RMQ can trade preprocessing memory for faster maxima. Use modular products in the persistent tree and preserve the previous answer for decoding.

Try one change

Could we divide two prefix LCMs?

Show the reasoning

When the same prime's largest power occurs on both sides of the split, the prefix summaries cannot identify the maximum remaining on the right.

From idea to code

Adapt the persistent tree’s segment aggregate to modular product with identity one. Old roots must survive the two point changes caused by a repeated large prime.
