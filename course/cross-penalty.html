Separate row and column choices, then price their interaction

In a matrix, picking a row or column earns its current sum and reduces each selected cell by p. Pick exactly k times. Why does splitting the work into two heaps almost solve the problem?

Work through the example

  1. For a row with m columns, picking it again reduces its future sum by m·p. If only rows were allowed, repeatedly take the largest current row sum. Do the same independently for columns.
  2. For a mix with i row picks and k−i column picks, each row-pick/column-pick pair crosses at exactly one cell. Whichever operation comes later loses p from that earlier operation.
  3. For a 2×2 all-ones board with p=1 and one pick of each kind, the two independent gains 2+2 require a correction of 1. Actual gain is 3.

Why it works

Precompute the best total for each number of row-only picks and column-only picks. Try all splits and subtract p·i·(k−i). It works even when the same row or column is picked repeatedly because pairs of operations are counted with multiplicity.

When the conditions change

O(nm+(n+m+k)log(n+m)) suffices. The correction depends only on the counts. If intersections had cell-specific penalties you would need information about the actual chosen rows and columns.

Try one change

Why would subtracting the cross penalty twice be wrong?

Show the reasoning

In a cross pair only the later pick observes the reduction from the earlier pick.
