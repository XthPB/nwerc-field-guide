Turn a polygon walk into signed area and lattice counts

A lattice triangle has corners (0,0),(4,0),(0,2). How many integer points lie strictly inside without enumerating all points?

Work through the example

  1. Walk the boundary and sum cross products of consecutive vertices. The doubled area is 8.
  2. The number of lattice steps on each side is gcd(|dx|,|dy|): 4,2,2. Summing gives 8 distinct boundary points, counting vertices once over the cycle.
  3. Pick’s formula gives (8−8+2)/2=1 interior point, namely (1,1).

Why it works

Signed triangles from any origin add by cancellation even for a concave simple polygon. For a simple lattice polygon, 2A=2I+B−2 converts area and boundary data into the interior count.

2A=∣∑i(xiyi+1−yixi+1)∣,I=A−B2+12A=\left|\sum_i (x_i y_{i+1}-y_i x_{i+1})\right|,\qquad I=A-\frac B2+12A=​i∑​(xi​yi+1​−yi​xi+1​)​,I=A−2B​+1

A is ordinary area. For a simple lattice polygon, B counts boundary lattice points and I counts interior lattice points.

When the conditions change

O(n log C) including gcds. Self-intersections or holes require an appropriate different formula. CF 1C is a separate regular-polygon angle/area transfer; its vertices need not be lattice points.

Try one change

If you reverse the order of the vertices, which number changes sign?

Show the reasoning

The signed area does. The boundary count and geometric area remain the same.
