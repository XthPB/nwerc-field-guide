Keep the independent bits of a whole set

You’re given integers such as 6, 5 and 3. You may take each one at most once and XOR the chosen values. Determine which results are achievable, with the empty subset giving zero. Different subsets can produce the same result.

Work through the example

  1. Write 6=110 and 5=101. Reducing the second by the first gives 011 as a new pivot.
  2. Now reduce 3=011 by that pivot; it becomes zero. It adds no new XOR result.
  3. The rank is two, so the possible XORs are {0,3,5,6}. With three independent binary choices of inclusion and rank two, each reachable target has two preimages.

Why it works

Eliminate from highest bit down and keep one pivot per leading bit. To maximize, take a pivot if it increases the current value. In an undirected XOR-weighted graph, tree-path XORs give one solution and fundamental cycles provide the basis of alternatives.

When the conditions change

O(B) bit-word XOR steps per insertion for B-bit values. In range queries, one prefix-basis variant keeps the latest usable pivot index. Counting reachable values differs from counting subsets that realize one value.

Try one change

Why does a cycle affect an XOR path calculation?

Show the reasoning

Taking a detour around the cycle toggles its edge XOR. Combining available cycles changes the base route by a vector from their span.
