Turn a choice or its opposite into reachability

You must choose Boolean settings. The rules are (A or B), (not A or B), and (A or not B). Can the choices coexist?

Work through the example

  1. Turn A or B into not A → B and not B → A. Apply the same conversion to the other two rules.
  2. From not A you force B, then A. From A you force B. The choice A=B=true satisfies every clause.
  3. If a literal and its negation ever lie in one strongly connected component, either assumption would force its opposite.

Why it works

Every clause of size two has two implications. Compute SCCs in linear time; a satisfying assignment follows the component DAG in reverse topological order, assigning a component true if neither it nor its opposite is assigned, and its opposite false. Respect the SCC numbering returned by the chosen library.

When the conditions change

An equality or exclusive-or can be written with two clauses. An arbitrary three-way OR cannot simply be split into pairs. For dense “at most one” groups, auxiliary prefix variables can avoid a quadratic set of exclusions.
