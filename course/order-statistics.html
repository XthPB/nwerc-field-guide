Convert a maximum into tail probabilities

Roll two fair six-sided dice. What is the expected maximum? Tracking 36 ordered pairs works, but it scales poorly with more dice.

Work through the example

  1. For k between 1 and 6, both dice are at most k with probability (k/6)².
  2. The probability the maximum equals 5 is (5/6)²−(4/6)²=9/36.
  3. Add P(max≥j) for j=1…6, obtaining 161/36. With n independent dice simply replace the exponent 2 by n.

Why it works

For a nonnegative integer random variable, each outcome of size m contributes one to exactly m tail events. Interchanging the sums gives the tail formula. Sorting comparable outcomes can likewise turn pair or triple events into frequency sums.

When the conditions change

Independence is needed for the product CDF; linearity of the tail sum itself needs none. Use stable numerical evaluation when subtracting nearly equal probabilities. In CF 626D the distribution of positive pair differences feeds a comparison of three independent selections.
