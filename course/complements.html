Combinatorics and counting · 1 of 11

Count by a first forbidden event or a complement

Distribute 7 identical tokens among three labeled boxes. Each box holds at most 3, and every token must be placed. Count distinct triples of box quantities. Arrangements within a box carry no additional choices.

Work through the example

  1. Unrestricted, choose two divider positions among nine, giving 36.
  2. Force one chosen box to take at least four. Three tokens remain freely distributable, giving ten assignments per choice of overflowing box.
  3. Two boxes cannot both receive four. Subtract 3×10 from 36 to get 6.

Why it works

For multiple simultaneous violations use inclusion–exclusion over subsets of violations. Subtract the minimum forced tokens before computing the binomial factor. CF 451E has a small number of boxes and large individual bounds.

When the conditions change

O(2ⁿ) subsets times binomial-evaluation cost. For many prime divisibility tests, subsets can be generated as square-free products; for mask constraints use a subset transform as in CF 449D.

Try one change

If the target were 10, why add a pair-overflow term?

Show the reasoning

Assignments with two overflowing boxes were subtracted once for each box. Inclusion–exclusion restores them once.
