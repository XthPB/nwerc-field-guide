Combinatorics and counting · 8 of 11

Multiply independent choices with a generating function

A red item contributes either 0 or 2 units and a blue item either 0 or 3, depending on whether you take it. Count selections producing exactly five units. With more items you may want counts for every target at once.

Work through the example

  1. Write the choices as 1+x² and 1+x³. Their product is 1+x²+x³+x⁵. The coefficient of x⁵ is one.
  2. If two distinguishable factors both allow weight 2, the coefficient of x² is two. Keeping only reachability changes what the coefficients mean.
  3. Raise a choice polynomial to a power for ordered repeated choices; multiply groups for independent choices.

Why it works

The exponent adds when choices combine, so convolution collects exactly the pairs giving the same total. NTT handles modular coefficients with a suitable primitive root and supported transform length. For exact integer counts bound coefficients before using floating-point FFT or several modular primes.

When the conditions change

A length-L convolution costs O(L log L) under the transform modulus. Pad to at least the full product length to avoid cyclic wraparound. Reduce after each step if the goal is Boolean reachability, and justify the conversion from modular to nonzero.

Try one change

If a modular coefficient is zero, does that prove the true count is zero?

Show the reasoning

No. A positive integer count divisible by the modulus also maps to zero.
