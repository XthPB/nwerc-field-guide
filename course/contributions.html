Combinatorics and counting · 4 of 9

Count the objects containing one fixed element

Sum the maximum value over every subarray of [2,1,3]. Instead of listing subarrays, how much does each position contribute?

Work through the example

  1. Value 3 wins every subarray ending at position 3. There are 3 starts, so it contributes 9.
  2. Value 2 wins [2] and [2,1] for 4. Value 1 wins only [1] for 1. Total 14.
  3. For each position, find its nearest dominating neighbor on each side. The product of its allowed left and right choices counts subarrays owned by it.

Why it works

Choose one side strict and the other non-strict when equal maxima occur. This gives every subarray exactly one owner. A monotone stack computes both boundaries. CF 817D subtracts the analogous minimum contributions.

∑[l,r]max⁡(al,…,ar)=∑iai #{subarrays owned by i}\sum_{[l,r]}\max(a_l,\ldots,a_r)=\sum_i a_i\,\#\{\text{subarrays owned by }i\}[l,r]∑​max(al​,…,ar​)=i∑​ai​#{subarrays owned by i}

For the subarray-maximum example, position i contributes only to the subarrays it owns after ties are resolved.

When the conditions change

O(n), and the product of two counts and a value may exceed 64-bit depending on bounds. In CF 675E, a dominant reachable stop similarly groups many destinations, but the counted quantity is shortest journey length.
