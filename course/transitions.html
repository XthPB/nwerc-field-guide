Probability, expectation and randomization · 8 of 12

Power a small transition model over many steps

A light stays unchanged with probability 3/4 and flips with probability 1/4 each second. Starting off, what is the chance it is on after two seconds?

Work through the example

  1. After the first second the off/on distribution is (3/4,1/4).
  2. After the second, on probability is (3/4)(1/4)+(1/4)(3/4)=3/8. The two routes to on are disjoint.
  3. A fixed transition matrix T advances any distribution by one step. Repeated squaring gives Tᵏ for huge k.

Why it works

With row distributions, D₀Tᵏ has the final probabilities. You can merge microstates only when each state in a class has identical total transition probability to every other class. CF 1151F groups positions by the number of misplaced items.

When the conditions change

Dense O(s³ log k) for s states. Nonuniform time-dependent transitions need ordered products. CF 24D is an absorbing spatial walk with solvable row equations; CF 148D uses finite population counts and alternating turns.

Try one change

Would you still get 3/8 if the same single random coin determined both seconds’ flips?

Show the reasoning

No. Flipping twice or staying twice both end off. Independence between steps was part of this transition model.
