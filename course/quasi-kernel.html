Reach every vertex in two steps from an independent set

In a directed graph, select mutually nonadjacent vertices so every unselected vertex can be reached from a selected one with at most two arcs. Greedily grabbing vertices can create conflicts. Which choices can you postpone?

Work through the example

  1. In A→B, B→C, C→A, scan A first. Record A as a candidate and discard A and its outgoing neighbor B from the forward scan. The next live candidate is C.
  2. Read candidates backward. Keep C and mark C and every vertex it points to. A is already marked, so skip it. C reaches A in one step and B in two.
  3. In general, eliminate each live candidate with its out-neighbors, then keep an unmarked candidate in reverse order and mark its outgoing neighborhood. The second pass needs the original adjacency lists.

Why it works

An earlier candidate has no arc to a later one, or the later one would have been discarded. The reverse pass prevents an arc from a later chosen vertex to an earlier chosen one. Every discarded vertex was an out-neighbor of a candidate that is either chosen or one step from a chosen vertex.

When the conditions change

O(n+m) time and space with two different marking arrays. Respect arc direction. A two-step certificate is essential even on the directed triangle.

Try one change

Would eliminating incoming neighbors give the same reachability promise?

Show the reasoning

It constructs the reversed promise. Each vertex can reach a chosen one within two arcs.

From idea to code

Scan the original outgoing adjacency lists in both passes. Use a fresh marking array when switching from eliminating candidates to selecting them.
