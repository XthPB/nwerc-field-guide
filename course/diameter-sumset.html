Trees and paths · 6 of 14

Find every possible overlap of two tree diameters

In an unweighted tree of odd diameter, find every possible number of shared edges between two diameter paths. The paths may be equal. How can you combine choices made far apart?

Work through the example

  1. Take centers A–B. Give each center two leaf children. Every diameter has length 3 and crosses A–B. Two diameters share 1 edge if both ends differ, 2 if one end agrees, and 3 if both ends agree.
  2. Find a diameter by two breadth-first sweeps and cut its central edge. Root each side at its center. Mark vertices with descendants at maximum side depth. Two deepest endpoints on that side share a root path of length d precisely when their LCA is at depth d. Valid LCAs have at least two marked child branches, or are deepest endpoints paired with themselves.
  3. Record the possible depths on each side as zero-one coefficient arrays. Multiply the polynomials. A positive coefficient at j means two diameters can overlap in 1+j edges. On the example both arrays represent 1+z, so their product has nonzero degrees 0,1,2.

Why it works

An odd diameter has two centers, and every maximum-length path joins a deepest endpoint on each side. Its intersection with another such path consists of the central edge plus the common rooted prefix on each side. Endpoint choices on the two sides are independent.

shared edges=1+depth(LCAL)+depth(LCAR)\text{shared edges}=1+\mathrm{depth}(\mathrm{LCA}_L)+\mathrm{depth}(\mathrm{LCA}_R)shared edges=1+depth(LCAL​)+depth(LCAR​)

The two LCA depths are measured from their respective endpoints of the central edge.

When the conditions change

O(n log n) time with an NTT, O(n) space. Compare with the maximum depth separately in each side. Each coefficient counts at most n distinct depth splits, so a supported prime greater than n preserves the positivity test. Pad the transform for ordinary convolution.

Try one change

Why does the same-edge proof need odd diameter?

Show the reasoning

For even diameter all diameters use one common center, but they may use different pairs of its branches. The two independent sides disappear.

From idea to code

Store a one-bit presence coefficient per achievable depth, even when many vertices produce that depth. Read the ordinary product starting at coefficient zero.
