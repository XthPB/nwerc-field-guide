Turn collision of two convex objects into point containment

A convex robot translates without rotating around a convex obstacle. Can a proposed displacement t cause an intersection?

Work through the example

  1. A collision means r+t=o for some robot point r and obstacle point o. Rearrange to t=o−r.
  2. Negate the robot around the origin, then take the Minkowski sum of the obstacle and the reflected robot. For two axis-aligned boxes, the resulting forbidden displacement region is another box.
  3. For convex polygons in boundary order, start from consistent extreme vertices and merge edge directions by angle to form the sum. Handle parallel edges together.

Why it works

The set of all sums of two convex polygons is convex. As a supporting direction rotates, each polygon’s support point changes in cyclic order, which explains the linear edge merge. CF 87E adds three polygons and scales a queried average point before containment.

When the conditions change

O(n+m) for a normalized convex sum plus O(log h) per convex containment query. Strict separation and touching produce different boundary rules. Reflection matters for collision queries; simple sums suffice for averaging.

Try one change

Why multiply an average query by three when there are three polygon choices?

Show the reasoning

The achievable averages are precisely one third of the Minkowski sum. Scaling the query avoids fractional vertices.
