Trees and paths · 5 of 14

Answer farthest-distance questions with two endpoints

In an unweighted tree, distance is the number of edges on the unique connecting path. Each vertex wants to know the greatest distance from it to any other vertex. Return one answer per vertex, including those that are not endpoints of a longest path.

Work through the example

  1. Find a farthest vertex A from an arbitrary starting vertex. A second traversal from A reaches a diameter endpoint B. Store distances from A and B.
  2. On the path A–u–v–B, the farther endpoint answers each interior vertex. A side branch adds the same initial distance to routes through its attachment point.
  3. For any x, max(dist(x,A),dist(x,B)) gives its eccentricity. A branch extending farther than both would produce a longer diameter by comparison at its attachment.

Why it works

Tree paths are unique. Thinking in terms of the projection of a branch onto the diameter proves the endpoint property. For joining two tree components by a freely chosen optimal edge, the unweighted radius is ceil(diameter/2).

When the conditions change

O(n) preprocessing and O(1) per eccentricity lookup. Two sweeps need a tree with nonnegative edge lengths; arbitrary graphs and negative weights need different reasoning. CF 455C uses optimal joins and a DSU; CF 1805D groups vertices by eccentricity.

Try one change

If the joining edge endpoints are prescribed instead of freely chosen, do the two diameters alone determine the new diameter?

Show the reasoning

No. You also need the eccentricities of the specified endpoints to evaluate a crossing path.
