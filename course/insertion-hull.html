Maintain a convex boundary while points arrive

Points arrive one at a time. Between insertions you must say whether a query point lies inside or on their convex hull. How can old boundary work be reused?

Work through the example

  1. Picture an upper chain through (0,0), (2,1), (4,0). Inserting (2,3) replaces the old top at x=2. The point (2,1) is now strictly below the new boundary.
  2. Keep an upper chain ordered by x and a lower chain with the opposite turn condition. Compare the inserted point with the segment between neighboring x-values. On the upper chain, ignore it if it is already on or below the segment. Otherwise insert it and erase adjacent vertices until every turn is again a right turn.
  3. To query, bracket its x on each chain and use orientation tests. It must be within the horizontal extent, below or on the upper chain and above or on the lower chain. Same-x entries retain the relevant extreme on each chain.

Why it works

Insertion only expands the hull. A vertex that becomes internal during an insertion can never return to the outer boundary while later operations only add points. This pays for the neighbors erased during repairs.

When the conditions change

Ordered sets give amortized O(log n) insertion and O(log n) membership. Use exact wide cross products for integer coordinates. Check single points, vertical segments and boundary equality when generalizing beyond the contest input.

Try one change

Can the same deletion rule handle removing arbitrary input points?

Show the reasoning

A previously hidden point can return to the boundary after removal. An insertion-only structure discarded the information required to restore it.
