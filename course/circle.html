Classify circle intersections before taking square roots

Two circles both have radius 5 and their centers are 6 units apart. Find where their boundaries intersect. Then ask what your calculation should return if the centers move so the circles touch once, never meet, or coincide.

Work through the example

  1. The midpoint of their common chord is 3 units along that line. The half-chord length is √(25−9)=4.
  2. With centers (0,0),(6,0), the intersections are (3,4) and (3,−4). For unequal radii, subtract the two circle equations to find the chord line.
  3. Compare center distance with the sum and absolute difference of radii first. Disjoint, contained, tangent and coincident circles lead to different outputs.

Why it works

The line of centers bisects a common chord at right angles. For overlap area, each circular segment is a sector minus a triangle. Containment gives the smaller disk’s whole area.

When the conditions change

O(1) per pair. Handle coincident centers before dividing by distance. Clamp a tiny negative radicand or an acos argument only after classifying the geometric case. Randomized minimum enclosing circle uses at most three boundary points, with expected linear time under a random permutation.

Try one change

Two equal circles have exactly the same center. How many intersections does the two-point formula return?

Show the reasoning

It is undefined there. The geometric answer is infinitely many boundary intersections for a positive radius.
