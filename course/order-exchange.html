Compare two adjacent jobs to derive the sort key

An unfinished job costs its weight each time unit. Job A takes 3 and weighs 5. Job B takes 2 and weighs 4. Which should run first to minimize weighted completion time?

Work through the example

  1. A then B costs 5×3 + 4×5 = 35.
  2. B then A costs 4×2 + 5×5 = 33. Work completed before this pair adds the same constant to both orders.
  3. In general A first is better when duration(A)×weight(B) ≤ duration(B)×weight(A). Sort by that cross product and never divide.

Why it works

Every inverted adjacent pair can be exchanged without worsening the objective. Repeated exchanges lead to the sorted order. For CF 1151D the two sides of a position have different costs; expand a neighboring swap to find the relevant difference of coefficients.

When the conditions change

O(n log n). Use strict comparison for sorting, including a deterministic tie rule if needed. With prerequisites, the improving swap must also remain legal. Exact wide integers avoid floating ratio errors.

Try one change

If all weights are equal, what order remains?

Show the reasoning

Shortest processing time first. Each short job finished early also reduces the waiting time paid by later jobs.
