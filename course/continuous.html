Bracket a numerical answer with the right proof

A closest approach varies with time. Should you use binary search, ternary search, or solve the intersection directly? Each assumes a different shape of the unknown.

Work through the example

  1. Two cars at positions 0 and 10 move toward one another at unit speed. “Have their paths met by t?” changes once, at t=5. Binary search works on that event.
  2. The distance at time t is |10−2t|. It decreases then increases, so ternary search for a minimum is justified by unimodality. A direct equality gives t=5 exactly.
  3. For an accumulated area under a curve, neither test computes the integral. Split at known changes and integrate the function, analytically if possible.

Why it works

Prove the monotone predicate or the unimodal objective before narrowing an interval. For variable-speed travel CF 1117C asks whether a destination is reachable by a deadline; periodic drift is computed separately. CF 24E searches for a first collision in a system of moving particles.

When the conditions change

For floating bisection justify a fixed iteration budget using initial width divided by 2ᵏ. Squared distances avoid square roots in comparisons. Adaptive Simpson uses a local error estimate and needs care near singularities or discontinuities.

Try one change

Could a distance function with two separate local minima be optimized safely by ternary search?

Show the reasoning

No. Without unimodality the discarded side could contain the global minimum.
