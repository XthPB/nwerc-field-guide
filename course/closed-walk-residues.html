Decide which odometer residues a closed walk can reach

A directed road adds its length to an odometer modulo t. Starting at a vertex and reading s, can you return there reading zero? Trying to track t different readings is too expensive.

Work through the example

  1. For edges A→B of weight 2 and B→A of weight 4, every complete lap adds 6. Modulo 10 you can cancel a reading of 4 in one lap. You can never cancel 3. The relevant divisor is gcd(6,10)=2.
  2. Inside each strongly connected component, choose a root and assign d[v] as the length of one directed root-to-v tree route. For every internal edge u→v of weight w collect the discrepancy d[u]+w−d[v]. Take the gcd g of their absolute values.
  3. A query at a vertex in that component succeeds precisely when s is divisible by gcd(g,t). Ignore edges between components, since no closed walk can cross one and return.

Why it works

Discrepancies telescope around any closed walk. Conversely, append a common v-to-root return path to the two routes of lengths d[u]+w and d[v]. Their closed-walk lengths differ by the discrepancy. Modulo t, the residues of closed walks form a subgroup because a walk may be repeated. Strong connectivity gives the same subgroup at every starting vertex.

Use only edges within the starting vertex’s strongly connected component.

When the conditions change

O(n+m) graph traversals plus integer gcd costs. Use sufficiently wide signed distances. If g=0 only reading zero modulo t works, including the empty walk.

Do we need shortest root routes?

Show the reasoning

Any directed traversal tree inside the component suffices. A change of potentials preserves the subgroup of closed-walk residues.

From idea to code

Run the SCC routine first. Accumulate one gcd per component in a single pass over its original internal edges.
