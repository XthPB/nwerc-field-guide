Trees and paths · 1 of 14

Turn a subtree into one interval

Each vertex of a rooted tree carries a value or color. A query names a vertex and asks about its entire subtree, including that vertex and all its descendants. In the sum version, individual values can change, and you must report the current subtree total.

Work through the example

  1. Root edges 1–2, 1–3, 2–4, 2–5 at 1. A preorder is [1,2,4,5,3].
  2. Vertex 2 and all descendants occupy positions [1,4) in this zero-based order. DFS finishes that whole branch before visiting 3.
  3. Store values at entry times. Update one position for a vertex change; query [tin[v],tout[v]) for its whole subtree.

Why it works

The DFS call that enters a vertex visits exactly its descendants before returning. The same flattening supports lazy range assignment of a color mask. For relatives at an exact depth, sort the entry times at each depth and binary search two endpoints.

When the conditions change

Tour construction O(n), standard updates/queries O(log n). A path between arbitrary vertices is generally several intervals. Rerooted subtrees sometimes become the complement of one child interval.

Try one change

If the new root is inside v’s original subtree, which vertices leave v’s new subtree?

Show the reasoning

The original child branch of v that contains the new root. Everything else remains below v, unless v itself is the new root.
