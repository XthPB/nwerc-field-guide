Range queries and sweeps · 3 of 19

Find the first position passing a range predicate

Hotels are listed in a fixed order and have remaining capacities [2,5,1,6]. A group of four must stay together. Assign it to the first hotel with enough beds, then reduce that hotel’s capacity before processing the next group.

Work through the example

  1. Store a maximum at each segment-tree node. The root maximum 6 passes. Its left child maximum 5 also passes, so descend left.
  2. Inside [2,5], the left leaf 2 fails. Go right to the hotel with capacity 5. Subtract four beds and repair the ancestors.
  3. For a kth living element, store counts instead. If the left count is smaller than k, subtract it before moving right.

Why it works

At every split the left summary is a certificate of whether the left half contains an answer. Associative summaries and a monotone predicate support analogous first-failure searches on ranges. CF 558E stores character frequencies and repaints intervals in sorted order.

When the conditions change

O(log n) for one descent. Binary searching with an O(log n) range query at each step costs O(log² n). For signed prefix sums, a plain segment total cannot tell whether some earlier prefix exceeded a target; store a maximum prefix as well.

Try one change

Why would choosing the child with the larger maximum be wrong?

Show the reasoning

The left child may already contain a feasible hotel even when the right child has more capacity.
