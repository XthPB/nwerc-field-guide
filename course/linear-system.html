Eliminate in the correct field

Three binary switches x, y and z control two lights. The first requires x XOR y to equal 1, and the second requires y XOR z to equal 0. How many settings meet both conditions? With many more lights, you may also need to produce one setting or detect that none can work.

Work through the example

  1. Make y free. Then x=1 XOR y and z=y. This gives (1,0,0) and (0,1,1).
  2. A pivot variable is determined by free variables. A row with no coefficients and right side one proves inconsistency.
  3. On a graph, the matrix-tree theorem instead turns the number of spanning trees into a determinant of a Laplacian cofactor.

Why it works

Row addition preserves the solution set. On binary switches use XOR, over a prime field invert nonzero pivots, and over reals choose stable pivots and a scale-aware tolerance. CF 662A turns equalities of XOR outcomes into rank and probability.

When the conditions change

Dense elimination O(n³); bitset row XOR accelerates GF(2). A determinant counts weighted trees only with the correct Laplacian. CF 429A is a simpler tree-parity drill where traversal replaces full elimination.

Try one change

In a consistent system over GF(2) with five variables and rank three, how many solutions are there?

Show the reasoning

Four. Two free variables each have two possible values.
