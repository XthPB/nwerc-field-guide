Range queries and sweeps · 1 of 12

Choose the stored aggregate before choosing the tree

A query asks for a bracket subsequence in any substring. A scalar count of opening brackets loses their order. What must two adjacent summaries tell each other?

Work through the example

  1. For “(()” store one matched pair and one unmatched opening. For “)(” store one unmatched closing and one unmatched opening.
  2. Joining the segments can match one opening from the left with one closing from the right. No other new pair crosses the boundary.
  3. Add the old matched pairs and that cross-boundary count. Remove the paired amounts from the unmatched totals. Doubling the resulting pair count gives the subsequence length.

Why it works

Concatenation is associative, so any correct summary of concatenation works in a segment tree. Prefix subtraction needs an invertible operation. Overlapping sparse-table blocks need idempotence, as with min or gcd.

When the conditions change

Segment queries O(log n). A static idempotent sparse table preprocesses in O(n log n) for O(1) queries. Preserve left-to-right order in an iterative query for noncommutative summaries. CF 339D combines by level rather than one associative operation.

Try one change

Does a single net bracket balance distinguish “()” from “)(”?

Show the reasoning

Both balance to zero but have different longest regular subsequence lengths. Two summaries must preserve unmatched directions.
