Use a fan to locate one triangle inside a convex polygon

A convex polygon is given in cyclic order. Hundreds of thousands of point queries make scanning every edge expensive.

Work through the example

  1. Use one corner P as the fan origin. The first and last rays bound the wedge inside the polygon. Points outside that wedge are immediately outside.
  2. For a point inside the wedge, binary search the last ray whose direction is at or before the query direction. The point must lie in the neighboring fan triangle if it is inside.
  3. Test its side of the triangle’s outer edge. Handle equality using the question’s boundary convention.

Why it works

Convexity makes the fan angles monotone and makes every fan triangle lie entirely inside. For an arbitrary simple polygon instead count crossings of a ray, tracking whether the point lies exactly on an edge.

When the conditions change

Convex queries take O(log h) after normalizing orientation and redundant collinear vertices. General ray casting costs O(n) per query and uses a half-open endpoint rule to count a shared vertex once. CSES Point in Polygon is the general version.

Try one change

Would the fan argument still work for a concave polygon shaped like a crescent?

Show the reasoning

No. A triangle of the fan may cross the exterior even if its endpoints are polygon vertices.
