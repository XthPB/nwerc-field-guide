Count spanning trees by deleting one row and column

You’re given a connected undirected graph and want to count its spanning trees. You may also want a breakdown by use of a particular edge. A triangle has three spanning trees. How many include one specified edge?

Work through the example

  1. Put each edge weight on the two endpoint diagonals of the Laplacian and its negative in the two off-diagonal entries.
  2. For edges of weights x,1,1 a cofactor can be [[x+1,−x],[−x,x+1]]. Its determinant is 2x+1.
  3. The coefficient of x is 2 because two trees use the marked edge. The constant 1 counts the tree avoiding it.

Why it works

The weighted matrix-tree theorem sums the product of edge weights over all spanning trees. Marking a class of edges by a polynomial variable records how many are used. In CF 917D this tracks edges shared with a given tree.

τ(G)=det⁡L(i),L=D−A\tau(G)=\det L^{(i)},\qquad L=D-Aτ(G)=detL(i),L=D−A

D is the degree matrix, A is adjacency and L⁽ⁱ⁾ deletes row and column i from the Laplacian.

When the conditions change

One numeric determinant is O(n³). Recovering multiple coefficients by evaluating and interpolating needs sufficiently many distinct points in the chosen field, with the corresponding time cost. A zero determinant can also arise modulo a prime for a nonzero integer count.
