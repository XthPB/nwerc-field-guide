Decide orientation and boundary cases exactly

A traveler walks from A=(0,0) toward B=(4,0). Given a point C such as (2,3), decide whether it lies left of the direction of travel, right of it, or on the same line. If it is on the line, also decide whether it is between A and B.

Work through the example

  1. The signed cross product (B−A)×(C−A) is 4×3−0×2=12. Positive means left in ordinary Cartesian coordinates.
  2. For D=(6,0) the cross product is zero. Its x coordinate lies beyond 4, so it is outside the segment.
  3. Two closed segments intersect properly when both pairs of endpoints straddle the other line. Check zero orientations separately using a bounding box.

Why it works

The sign is twice the signed triangle area. It survives translation and avoids ambiguous slopes or division by zero. For angle sorting compare half-planes first, then the cross-product sign, and settle collinear ties consistently.

When the conditions change

O(1) per predicate. Widen before multiplication and even before subtracting coordinates if inputs approach type limits. A comparator based on an arbitrary floating epsilon can violate transitivity. CF 598C asks for nearest directions.
