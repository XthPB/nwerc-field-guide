Work backwards when the last move leaves a certificate

A rectangular grid contains zeros and ones. In one move choose a 2 × 2 square and flip exactly three of its four cells. Output a sequence that clears every 1 within the operation budget. A flip changes 0 to 1 as well as 1 to 0.

Work through the example

  1. Fix rows from top to bottom. Use a 2×2 overlapping the next row to clear unwanted ones in the current row while changes below remain available.
  2. With two rows left, fix columns from left to right by the same principle, until only a 2×2 square remains.
  3. In that square, 3 ones clear in one move, and 4 become 1 by flipping three ones. From 1 or 2 ones, flip one one and two zeros to increase the count by one until it reaches 3.

Why it works

Each stage preserves a growing solved region, and a small explicit finishing procedure handles the boundary. In reverse reconstruction, look for an operation that necessarily exposes an identifiable endpoint, color group or leaf.

When the conditions change

O(nm) operations with a constant-size finish; check the stated move budget as well as the final state. In CF 1204C the output is a compressed walk: retain enough waypoints that expansion follows shortest-path distances.
