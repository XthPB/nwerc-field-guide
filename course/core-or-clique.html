Read a clique certificate while peeling a graph

Given an undirected graph and a number k, produce either a group where everyone has at least k neighbors within the group, or exactly k vertices where every pair is adjacent. You may return either valid witness. Report failure if neither exists.

Work through the example

  1. At k=3 a triangle attached to a leaf has no 3-core. Remove the leaf. A triangle vertex now has degree 2, and it and its two remaining neighbors form a size-three candidate.
  2. Repeatedly remove a live vertex of degree below k. Just before removing one of degree exactly k−1, test whether every pair in its closed live neighborhood is adjacent. A successful test produces a clique.
  3. If peeling stops with vertices left, return all survivors as a k-core. If it empties the graph and every clique check failed, neither certificate exists.

Why it works

No member of a k-core can be the first member removed while its k internal neighbors survive. For a k-clique, look at its first removed vertex. Its k−1 clique neighbors all survive, and its degree is below k, so its candidate is exactly that clique.

When the conditions change

Skip pair tests when k(k−1)/2 exceeds the total edge count. Across tested vertices their k−1 incident live edges are charged once as they disappear. With hashed adjacency, the extra work is O(mk) expected and O(m√m) when tests are possible. Handle k=1 directly. An ordered adjacency representation adds a logarithmic factor to lookups.

Try one change

Why ignore a vertex of degree k−2?

Show the reasoning

It cannot be the first deleted vertex of any size-k clique.

From idea to code

Maintain live degrees with a queue of eligible vertices. Test candidate neighbor pairs against the original adjacency sets.
