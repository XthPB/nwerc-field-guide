Price the next unit of flow in the residual network

Workers A,B have assignment costs (1,2) and (2,100) for jobs X,Y. The cheapest first assignment A→X costs 1. How do you still reach the global optimum?

Work through the example

  1. After sending A→X, a new route can send B→X for 2, reverse X→A for −1, then send A→Y for 2.
  2. The second unit increases total cost by 3. The final assignments B→X and A→Y cost 4 together.
  3. Reverse residual edges carry the negation of the forward cost, so even an initially nonnegative cost network acquires negative edges.

Why it works

Use shortest augmenting paths under the standard feasible-potential condition. Reweight each residual arc by cost+potential[u]−potential[v]. Every s-to-t path changes by the same constant; Dijkstra works if these reduced costs are nonnegative. Update reachable potentials by computed distances.

When the conditions change

With F unit augmentations and valid initial potentials, O(F E log V) for heap-based shortest paths. If original arcs have negative cost, initialize potentials accordingly; negative-cost circulations or demands may require a more general formulation. For dense square assignment Hungarian runs in O(n³).

Try one change

Can a maximum-cardinality matching algorithm choose the best of two equal-cardinality assignments by itself?

Show the reasoning

No. It must account for costs or be followed by a valid cost-optimization procedure.
