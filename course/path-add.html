Trees and paths · 7 of 14

Count many paths by marking only their boundaries

You’re given a tree and a list of journeys, each following the unique path between its endpoints. Return how many journeys traverse each edge. On edges 1–2, 2–3, 2–4, start with the journeys from 1 to 3 and from 3 to 4.

Work through the example

  1. For journey 1→3 mark +1 at 1 and 3 and −2 at LCA=1. For 3→4 mark +1 at 3 and 4 and −2 at 2.
  2. The combined marks at (1,2,3,4) are (−1,−2,2,1). Collect children upward. Subtree totals at 3 and 4 are 2 and 1.
  3. At 2 the total is −2+2+1=1. The answers on edges 2–3, 2–4 and 1–2 are 2,1,1.

Why it works

For a cut just above a subtree, both endpoints inside cancel against their LCA. Exactly one endpoint inside leaves one unit. For vertex usage instead mark both endpoints +1, LCA −1, and its parent −1 when it exists.

When the conditions change

O((n+q) log n) with binary-lifting LCA and one postorder. Online path updates interleaved with arbitrary queries need a different structure such as heavy-light decomposition.

Try one change

A journey starts and ends at the same vertex. Which counts increase?

Show the reasoning

No edge count increases. The vertex-count formula increases just that vertex by one.
