Probability, expectation and randomization · 9 of 15

Move probability mass between sets of survivors

A tournament begins with all contestants present. Each round chooses a pair of survivors uniformly at random. The given probabilities for that pair decide which contestant is eliminated. What is the chance that each original contestant is the last one left?

Work through the example

  1. Put probability 1 on the full survivor set. For three contestants, each unordered pair is chosen with probability 1/3 if pair selection is uniform.
  2. If i beats j with probability pᵢⱼ, add mass[M]·pᵢⱼ/3 to M without j. The reverse result removes i.
  3. Process masks by decreasing size. When the mask is a singleton its mass is that contestant’s final survival probability.

Why it works

Every fight decreases cardinality, giving an acyclic order even when the underlying pairwise preferences cycle. Equal types can be grouped if their future transition law is determined by population counts, as in CF 540D.

When the conditions change

O(n²2ⁿ) for the individual mask method. In the population version, encounters that change nothing create self-loops; condition on an effective encounter when reporting eventual outcomes. Absorbing singleton masses should sum to one.

Try one change

Can you choose the loser uniformly at random instead of choosing a pair then its outcome?

Show the reasoning

Only if the pairwise probabilities induce that distribution; generally stronger contestants are less likely to disappear.
