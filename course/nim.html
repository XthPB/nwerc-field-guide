Replace an independent component by its Grundy value

Several independent impartial games are played together. Each turn makes one move in exactly one component, and the player with no move loses. The worked position has component Grundy values 1, 2 and 3. Decide whether the player to move can force a win.

Work through the example

  1. Compute 1 XOR 2 XOR 3=0. Any move changes one component’s Grundy number, so the XOR leaves zero.
  2. If the XOR is nonzero, choose a component with the highest differing bit and reduce its Grundy number to make the XOR zero.
  3. The mex definition guarantees a move to each smaller Grundy number. In each finite acyclic component set g=the smallest nonnegative integer absent from its successor values.

Why it works

The disjoint-sum theorem applies to impartial normal-play positions. In CF 850C prime exponents form independent games after identifying what a move can change. A pile with arbitrary positive removal has Grundy equal to its size.

When the conditions change

Fill one state per distinct position or use proven periodicity to compress it. Misère rules, shared resources, forced simultaneous moves, and player-specific moves can invalidate ordinary Nim XOR.

Try one change

Can a component with Grundy 4 always move to Grundy 5?

Show the reasoning

No. Mex guarantees moves to 0,1,2,3 and forbids a move to 4. Larger options depend on the game.
