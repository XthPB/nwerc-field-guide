Graphs, connectivity and flow · 5 of 27

Collapse strongly connected regions only after checking what survives

Roads are directed and each vertex offers a nonnegative reward you can collect once. You can revisit a vertex to continue traveling, but you cannot collect its reward again. Find the maximum reward achievable on a route when several vertices may be reachable from one another.

Work through the example

  1. Vertices A and B reach one another. From B travel to C, and C cannot return. Starting in A, collect both A and B, then choose the exit from B.
  2. Contract maximal mutual-reachability groups. A cycle among contracted groups would mean they were one SCC, so the new graph is acyclic.
  3. If every vertex reward is nonnegative and revisiting is allowed, all rewards in the chosen component can be collected before exiting. Run a longest-path DP on the condensed graph.

Why it works

Reachability survives contraction unconditionally; rewards and time-dependent behavior require their own proof. CF 427C selects the cheapest representative per component. CF 949C seeks a small sink component under its edge rule. CF 1137C first expands the graph by day of the week.

When the conditions change

O(V+E) for SCCs. When several product states refer to the same museum, prove how to avoid rewarding one museum several times before summing SCC weights.

Try one change

Would the sum-of-rewards argument survive a rule forbidding revisits?

Show the reasoning

No. Mutual reachability can rely on returning along a branch, and a simple path may fail to collect every reward.
