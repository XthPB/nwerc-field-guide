Count tree paths by their exact gcd

Every vertex carries a positive integer. Count the simple paths, including single vertices, whose labels have gcd exactly d, for every d that occurs.

Work through the example

  1. On the chain 6–10–15 the singleton paths contribute gcds 6,10,15. The two short edges give gcds 2 and 5. The full chain gives gcd 1.
  2. First count G[d], paths whose labels are all divisible by d. Keep only such vertices and edges. In a component of size s there are s(s+1)/2 unordered paths including singletons, because each endpoint pair determines one path.
  3. Now process divisors from largest to smallest. Subtract the exact counts already found at proper multiples of d from G[d]. To build the forests efficiently, bucket an edge by the gcd of its endpoint labels and process those buckets whose index is a multiple of d.

Why it works

A path survives exactly for divisors of its gcd. Thus G[d] equals the sum of exact counts over all multiples of d. Connectedness of the filtered forest certifies that every interior vertex satisfies the same divisibility test.

When the conditions change

Avoid clearing an n-entry DSU for every d. Count singletons from value frequencies and use timestamps or reset only touched endpoints. For maximum label A, there are O(A log A) value-bucket visits plus one edge visit per divisor of its endpoint gcd, with a DSU factor for unions.

Try one change

Why can't a general cyclic graph use the same component-size formula for counting simple paths?

Show the reasoning

Two vertices in a cyclic component can have several different simple paths. The endpoint pair only identifies a unique path in a forest.
