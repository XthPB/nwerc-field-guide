Move a divisibility condition onto prime exponents

You’re given a positive integer such as 72. Count how many different positive integers divide it exactly, including 1 and the number itself. For many inputs you want to count without testing every smaller integer individually.

Work through the example

  1. Factor 72 as 2³·3². A divisor chooses exponent 0,1,2,3 for 2 and 0,1,2 for 3.
  2. These choices are independent. There are 4×3=12 divisors. The largest common divisor of two integers takes the smaller exponent at every prime.
  3. If there are many inputs bounded by M, precompute their smallest prime factors once. If a query groups all values divisible by a prime, sweep over its multiples instead.

Why it works

Unique factorization turns multiplication into addition of exponents, gcd into a componentwise minimum and lcm into a componentwise maximum. In CF 385C each present prime contributes the frequencies of its multiples to a later range sum.

When the conditions change

Trial division costs O(√x) for one x. A sieve handles many bounded values and deterministic 64-bit Miller–Rabin plus Pollard rho handles isolated large composites. Multiply only after checking overflow for lcm. The divisor formula is for positive integers.
