Use a distance bound to limit geometric neighbors

You have many points in the plane. Return the smallest squared Euclidean distance between two distinct input points. Comparing every pair is too expensive. A current best squared distance D rules out a pair whose squared horizontal separation alone is at least D.

Work through the example

  1. Sort by x. In divide and conquer, solve both halves and take the smaller current distance d.
  2. In the strip within d of the split, visit points by y. Stop once the y difference reaches d. Within each half no two points are closer than d, so a small d-by-2d box holds only a constant number.
  3. Merge the halves’ y-sorted lists in linear time per recursion level. A duplicate point gives distance zero immediately.

Why it works

The strip packing bound removes the quadratic cross-pair search. In CF 429D a subarray’s change in prefix sum and index difference become the two coordinate differences of a pair of prefix points.

When the conditions change

O(n log n) when y order is merged, O(n) extra space. Compare squared integer distances whenever possible. For the farthest pair first discard interior points using the hull, then use rotating calipers.

Try one change

Could you compare only neighboring points in x order?

Show the reasoning

No. A third point with intermediate x but extremely distant y may separate the true closest pair in that order.
