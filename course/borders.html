Strings and pattern matching · 1 of 11

Reuse the longest suffix that is also a prefix

You have matched “ababa” in a stream and the next character breaks the pattern. How much of the work still describes a valid beginning?

Work through the example

  1. The proper borders of “ababa” are “aba” and “a”. The longest has length 3.
  2. If the next character fails after that border, fall back again to length 1. Following prefix-function links lists every remaining possible overlap.
  3. To count occurrences of all prefixes, count visits to each matched length, then push its count to its failure parent from larger to smaller lengths.

Why it works

When a current match fails, any suffix that can still be a prefix must be a border of the previous match. The KMP failure chain enumerates exactly these candidates. The Z-function instead records match length against the full prefix at each starting position.

When the conditions change

O(n+m) matching one pattern in a text. A separator used in pattern+separator+text must be outside the alphabet. CF 126B additionally needs an occurrence in the interior, beyond the prefix and suffix copies.

Try one change

In “aaaa”, can the longest proper border “aaa” occur elsewhere strictly between its prefix and suffix occurrences?

Show the reasoning

No. Its only starts are positions 1 and 2. The shorter “aa” does occur at an interior start.
