NWERC

  • 1995 · Block Voting

    Parties cast all their seats together. For each party, count the coalitions in which its support changes the outcome from failing to reach a majority to winning.

    Statement-derived comparison with 2024 D. Here one specified party must swing a vote. Count sums of all other parties in its pivotal window. A single all-party count polynomial can be divided by the selected party’s factor to recover that row.

    Read the lesson · 0/1 knapsack
  • 1996 · Dividing coins

    Divide all the given coins between two people. Each coin must go whole to one person. Find the smallest possible difference between their total amounts.

    Statement-derived. Two piles always have totals s and S−s. A closest-to-half reachable sum solves a fairness objective with one Boolean axis.

    Read the lesson · Bitset subset sum
  • 1997 · Mendel

    A family tree records known or possible inherited traits. Each parent passes on one of two copies at random. Use the family information to find the probability of the specified visible trait in the descendant.

    Statement-derived. A tiny probability vector at each tree node summarizes the possible outcomes below it. Combine independent parental contributions at the next node.

    Read the lesson · First-step expectation and probability · Rooted tree DP and rerooting
  • 1998 · Numbers & Letters

    Use any of five available numbers once each and ordinary arithmetic with integer intermediate results. Reach the target exactly if possible, otherwise get as close as possible from below.

    Statement-derived. For each small mask store the integers obtainable by expressions on exactly those leaves. Split by the root operator and test both operand orders where relevant, including exact division.

    Read the lesson · Enumerate submasks
  • 1999 · Chess x Tetris

    You have an 8-by-8 board and a given stock of five tetromino shapes. Find a way to cover the board using the pieces, allowing rotations and reflections and forbidding overlaps.

    Statement-derived modelling exercise. Filling the first uncovered square canonically avoids generating all placement orders. A memoized frontier also needs the remaining stock of each shape.

    Read the lesson · Enumerate submasks
  • 2000 · Railroads

    A timetable lists trains and stops. Among journeys you can start after a given time, find the earliest arrival at your destination. If several journeys arrive then, choose the latest possible departure.

    Statement-derived. Among connections reaching the same dated station event, the later initial departure dominates for the stated tie-break. Event time supplies an order, with simultaneous transfers handled consistently.

    Read the lesson · Topological order and DAG DP · Two-state DP with rolling layers
  • 2001 · Number Game

    Two players take turns selecting numbers from 2 through 20 under rules that also exclude multiples and sums generated by earlier choices. From a specified set of available numbers, identify moves that force the opponent eventually to run out.

    Statement-derived. The legal set lies in a small fixed universe. Memoize winning and losing positions by availability, updating all newly forbidden numbers after each move.

    Read the lesson · Enumerate submasks · Grundy values on a finite game
  • 2002 · Euro Efficiency

    Buyer and seller have unlimited coins of a given set of denominations. For prices from 1 to 100, minimize the number of coins changing hands, including change. Report the average and worst of those minimum counts.

    Statement-derived. Giving change permits negative as well as positive coin moves in the net amount. A one-directional unlimited-coin recurrence would omit cheaper overpayment-and-change combinations.

    Read the lesson · Dijkstra on an expanded graph
  • 2003 · Bridging signals

    Two rows of ports face each other. Each signal connects a specified port on the first row to one on the second. Find the largest set of signals that can be routed on the surface without crossing.

    Statement-derived reduction. After ordering one shore, two links cross exactly when their endpoint order reverses. The largest noncrossing collection is an LIS. A geometric picture hides a sequence state.

    Read the lesson · Strict longest increasing subsequence
  • 2004 · Minimax Triangulation

    Draw noncrossing internal diagonals that cut a simple polygon into triangles. Choose a triangulation that makes the largest triangle area as small as possible.

    Fix a boundary chord and the third vertex of its triangle. Combine the two remaining subpolygons by their worst triangle, minimizing over valid pivots. A nonconvex polygon also needs a validity test.

    Read the lesson · Interval DP in increasing length · Exact integer geometry
  • 2005 · Declaration of Content

    Food labels list ingredients in descending order and give some exact percentages. All percentages are integers totaling 100. For a queried ingredient, which products could contain the most or the least?

    Statement-derived. A total of 100 and an ordering of ingredient percentages give small states for the previous percentage and remaining total. Prefix/suffix feasibility can bound the possible amount of a specified ingredient.

    Read the lesson · 0/1 knapsack · Two-state DP with rolling layers
  • 2006 · The Bookcase

    Place books on exactly three nonempty shelves. A shelf is as high as its tallest book and as wide as their total thickness. Minimize the area given by the sum of shelf heights times the widest shelf.

    Statement-derived. Process in descending height so opening a shelf fixes its maximum height. Two accumulated shelf widths determine the third from the processed total. Track how many shelves have opened and minimize paid heights for identical widths.

    Read the lesson · 0/1 knapsack · Two-state DP with rolling layers
  • 2007 · Obfuscation

    A sentence lost its spaces and the interior letters of each word were shuffled. With a dictionary and the surviving text, decide whether the sentence can be reconstructed uniquely, ambiguously, or not at all.

    Statement-derived. Dictionary words with equivalent first/last letters and interior counts can share a match signature. A prefix DP can saturate the number of decodings at two when only impossible, unique, or ambiguous matters.

    Read the lesson · Two-state DP with rolling layers · Trie of strings
  • 2008 · Easy Climb

    A line of terrain heights must be adjusted so neighboring heights differ by at most a given limit, while the two endpoints stay fixed. Minimize the sum of absolute height changes.

    The allowed height change gives each current height a window of predecessor heights. The jury first restricts the candidate heights, then maintains minima as the window moves.

    Read the lesson · Monotone stack and sliding deque · Coordinate compression
  • 2009 · Mountain Road

    Cars arrive at both ends of a narrow mountain road. Cars traveling in opposite directions cannot pass, and cars in the same queue retain their order. Schedule the crossings so the last car finishes as early as possible.

    Separate two ordered streams. Their consumed prefix lengths determine the remaining work, but the direction of the last car affects the timing of the next batch.

    Read the lesson · Two-state DP with rolling layers
  • 2010 · Free Goodies

    Two people alternate picking gifts with different values to each person. Petra takes her favorite, breaking ties in Jan’s favor. Jan wants the best final haul for himself, then for Petra. Given who starts, find both final totals.

    Sort by one player’s preferences first. A DP by items already allocated and number taken by the other player then becomes possible. The jury also gives a faster greedy route.

    Read the lesson · Two-state DP with rolling layers · 0/1 knapsack
  • 2011 · Tichu

    You have a hand of thirteen cards. One turn plays a single legal combination from that hand. Find the fewest turns in which you can play every card, and identify the combinations.

    Thirteen cards fit a mask. Precompute legal plays for every subset and partition a remaining hand into as few legal subsets as possible.

  • 2012 · Beer Pressure

    Several pubs already have votes. Each new voter chooses a pub with probability equal to its current share of the votes. After all votes are cast, ties for first are broken uniformly. Find each pub’s chance of winning.

    Aggregate by a vector of current counts, propagate probability on the resulting lattice, then sum terminal outcomes. Distinct histories with the same counts have the same future law.

  • 2013 · Jingle Balls

    Balls hang on the leaves of a binary decoration. At each branch the two sides must end up with ball counts differing by at most one. Find the fewest balls you must move to balance the decoration, or decide it is impossible.

    At a given tree depth, a balanced target distributes totals by floor or ceiling division. Those two possibilities replace an unconstrained count axis in the recursive subproblems.

  • 2014 · Cent Savings

    Purchases arrive in a fixed order. You may split the bill into a limited number of consecutive transactions, each rounded to the nearest ten cents. What is the smallest total you can pay?

    The cost of a rounded group is local once its endpoints are fixed. A last cut gives the baseline prefix DP. Rounding depends on a small residue, allowing a further compression.

  • 2015 · Debugging

    One of n lines fails. You may insert diagnostic prints, then run the program to learn which interval contains the failure. Prints and runs each cost time. Find the smallest time a strategy needs to locate the bad line in the worst case.

    Balanced splitting recurses on a rounded quotient of the size. Memoize the resulting sizes instead of building an independent search tree for every number of splits.

    Read the lesson · Two-state DP with rolling layers
  • 2016 · Arranging Hat

    You have equal-length decimal strings in a fixed list. Change individual digits so the strings become lexicographically nondecreasing. Make as few changes as possible.

    The jury’s alternative indexes prefix length and edit budget, keeping the smallest possible final number. That representative leaves the most room for later nondecreasing numbers.

    Read the lesson · Two-state DP with rolling layers
  • 2017 · Installing Apps

    A phone has limited storage, and each app needs space both during download and after installation. Choose an installation order that fits and leaves as many apps installed as possible.

    The temporary download and permanent footprint create an exchange order. In that order, store the least occupied space that attains each number of installed apps and reconstruct the picks.

  • 2017 · English Restaurant

    Groups of random sizes arrive one at a time and take the smallest available table that fits, leaving if no table fits. Given the table sizes and number of arrivals, find the expected number of seated people.

    The last occupied table separates left and right intervals. When averaging over orders, include the number of interleavings of their earlier occupancies.

  • 2018 · Fastest Speedrun

    A game’s levels award special items that can make other levels faster. You must finish every level. Choose their order and which available items to use to minimize total playing time.

    Preprocessing exposes functional cycles and removes mandatory costs. Only after that reduction does a DP or shortest-path calculation capture the remaining choices.

  • 2019 · Disposable Switches

    Every road has a base length and an unknown common nonnegative surcharge per edge after scaling. Which vertices can never occur on a cheapest route between the two terminals, however that surcharge is chosen?

    Keep the cheapest base length for each exact edge count. A parameter adds the same surcharge per edge, making each edge count a candidate line.

    A speedrun has tricks that can fail and cost time. After seeing a failure you may continue or restart. Choose a strategy that minimizes the expected real time until you complete a run faster than the record.

    A restart refers back to the unknown starting expectation. The jury fixes a trial value, evaluates easier downstream states and uses consistency at the start to guide a search.

    Arrange an alphabetical list of words in columns, retaining reading order. Given the total width available and a gap between columns, minimize the number of rows needed.

    Fix a height and let a prefix DP minimize the required width. Height feasibility is monotone, so the outer optimization becomes a binary search.

  • 2024 · Limited Library

    Books have heights and shelves have height limits. Every shelf can hold either a larger or a smaller number of books. How many shelves can use the smaller capacity while all the books still fit?

    A book that fits a shelf also fits every taller shelf. For a proposed number of reduced-capacity shelves, reduce the shortest ones and match books greedily in sorted order. Binary search finds the largest feasible number. The separate range-query lesson shows what changes when eligibility is an arbitrary interval.

    Every party has a fixed seat count. Count groups holding more than half the seats in which removing any single member loses that majority. Parties with equal seats are still different parties.

    Requiring every selected party to be necessary collapses to removing the smallest. Count exact prefix sums in the narrow window that crosses a strict majority when this party enters.

  • 2025 · Bisecting Bargain

    An ATM must pay a fixed total in its available denominations. Find a way of dispensing the money such that its coins and notes cannot be split into two equal-value piles, or decide that every payout splits.

    A bitset tests one proposed collection of notes. Enumerating all collections remains too slow. Exchange equivalent denominations first to reduce the outer search.

    Numbered positions lie on a line, each with a reward. Start at the first, jump only to the right, and finish at the last. Each jump may be at most as long as the preceding one. What is the greatest total reward you can collect on the positions you visit?

  • 2024 · Even Odd Game

    Cards carry additions or multiplications. Two players alternate applying unused cards to the same running number until the cards run out. One wants the final result even and the other wants it odd. Find a winning strategy.

    Reduce every operation to its effect on parity. Only a few card types remain, so counts of types can replace masks of labeled cards in the game DP.

  • 2024 · Jigsaw Present

    You’re given integer vectors in the plane. Find two distinct subsets with the same vector sum, or decide that there are none. After shared vectors cancel, the two sides are disjoint.

    Encode two disjoint chosen sets using signs −1,0,+1 and join signed vector sums across a split. A pigeonhole bound is also needed to limit how many input vectors the exponential search uses.

  • 2025 · Buggy Rover

    A rover follows its highest-priority legal direction at each step, but its direction priorities may change unexpectedly. Given its observed moves on a map, find the fewest changes needed to explain the route.

    Only 24 direction priorities are possible. The authors’ alternative DP stores a priority and the fewest necessary changes for the observed prefix, competing with a direct greedy filter.

    Small scores admit an unbounded reachability table. Beyond a proved threshold, arithmetic structure reduces feasibility to divisibility, and digit DP handles the large decimal bound.
