How the same idea comes back

Start with what a decision leaves for the next one. Each row connects that question to problems with different stories.

  • Can the compulsory bundles reach half?

    Dividing coins can use each coin once. In Unmerge, first find the blocks that cannot be separated. After that discovery, the two problems ask the same subset-sum question.

    NWERC 1996 · Dividing coins · CF 1382D · Unmerge
  • Do crossings or nesting reduce to an increasing sequence?

    Ordering one shore turns compatible bridges into increasing endpoints. For nesting envelopes both dimensions must increase, so equal first coordinates need special tie handling.

    NWERC 2003 · Bridging signals · CF 4D · Mysterious Present
  • Do the legal earlier positions move together?

    Height limits in Easy Climb, overlapping requirements in Museum Visit, and a moving spectator in Watching Fireworks all leave a sliding interval of predecessors. Read its minimum or maximum with a deque.

    NWERC 2008 · Easy Climb · BAPC 2024 · Museum Visit · CF 372C · Watching Fireworks is Fun
  • Where did the last group begin?

    Once the last cut is fixed, earlier groups no longer affect its cost. Cent Savings charges rounding on the last group. The Bakery counts distinct values and updates many previous cuts when a new item arrives.

    NWERC 2014 · Cent Savings · CF 833B · The Bakery
  • How much of a budget should each child receive?

    A connected group containing its top vertex can choose a size in each child branch. A tree of discounts adds a condition at the parent, but still has to distribute the purchase count across children.

    BAPC 2023 · Jungle Job · CF 815C · Karen and Supermarket
  • What state remains after the next random event?

    Crashing Competition Computer needs the expected work after a restart. Bag of mice conditions on the next draws, then reads a smaller state. In both, give each successor its conditional probability before simplifying the equation.

    BAPC 2022 · Crashing Competition Computer · CF 148D · Bag of mice
  • Which digits of a huge upper bound still constrain the suffix?

    Classy Numbers needs a count of nonzero digits. The World Finals score problem first reduces large attainable scores arithmetically, then uses a decimal-bound DP. The tight flag serves the same purpose in both.

    World Finals 2025 · Score Values · CF 1036C · Classy Numbers

Widely solved Codeforces examples

These are the four most-solved regular problemset IDs tagged dp in each of five rating bands on 23 November 2026, plus the most-solved at exactly 3000. Each has an individually checked observation, lesson and code link. Some easier problems simplify to a greedy choice or a prefix sum. Solve counts measure practice on Codeforces.   • NWERC 2004 · Minimax Triangulation

    Fix a boundary chord and the third vertex of its triangle. Combine the two remaining subpolygons by their worst triangle, minimizing over valid pivots. A nonconvex polygon also needs a validity test.

    Split on the final merge, pivot, or triangle · Interval DP in increasing length · Exact integer geometry
  • World Finals 2025 · Buggy Rover

    Only 24 direction priorities are possible. The authors’ alternative DP stores a priority and the fewest necessary changes for the observed prefix, competing with a direct greedy filter.

    A fixed number of recent decisions · Two-state DP with rolling layers
  • NWERC 2024 · Limited Library

    A book that fits a shelf also fits every taller shelf. For a proposed number of reduced-capacity shelves, reduce the shortest ones and match books greedily in sorted order.     Binary search the answer with a DP feasibility test · Binary search on the answer · Look up a boundary in sorted values
  • NWERC 2024 · Dutch Democracy

    Requiring every selected party to be necessary collapses to removing the smallest. Count exact prefix sums in the narrow window that crosses a strict majority when this party enters.

    Which voting groups need every member? · Count minimal winning coalitions · 0/1 knapsack
  • NWERC 2025 · Bisecting Bargain

    A bitset tests one proposed collection of notes. Enumerating all collections remains too slow. Exchange equivalent denominations first to reduce the outer search.

    Can the cashier make an unsplittable payout? · Find a payout that cannot be split · Bitset subset sum
  • BAPC 2023 · Jungle Job

    Count connected subsets once by their highest vertex and by size. Multiplying child options performs the size split automatically.

    Merge a child’s size or budget distribution · Rooted tree DP and rerooting · 0/1 knapsack
  • BAPC 2026 · Flippin’ Cards

    Learning the shared card bias changes the price of each action. The counts of cards seen and effectively upside down determine the chance of the next bad card. A suffix flip exchanges good and bad in the effective history.

    Learn an unknown probability as you make decisions · Expected cost with a shared unknown card-flip probability · First-step expectation and probability
  • SWERC 2021–22 · Gastronomic Event

    At a candidate centroid, sizes of hanging subtrees become weights. The orientation objective prefers two near-equal totals. A direct bitset still repeats too many shifts until multiplicities are compressed.

    Repeated weights and the square-root bound · Centroid decomposition · Bitset subset sum
  • GCPC 2024 · Jigsaw Present

    Encode two disjoint chosen sets using signs −1,0,+1 and join signed vector sums across a split. A pigeonhole bound is also needed to limit how many input vectors the exponential search uses.

    When the two halves interact through conflicts · Meet in the middle for sums
