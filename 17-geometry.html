Geometry inside a DP

Draw a diagonal inside a convex quadrilateral to form two triangles. You pay the sum of both triangle perimeters, so the shared diagonal is counted twice. Which diagonal gives the smaller bill, and how does the choice extend to more vertices?

Start this topic in reading order → · See the whole reading path

  • Geometry can determine the price of a combinatorial moveA courier starts at a depot. On each trip they visit one or two uncollected points, then return to the depot. Traveling between two locations costs their squared Euclidean distance. Collect every point with the smallest total travel cost.
  • Four directional path tables can meet around a crossingTwo travelers cross a rectangular grid from different corners, each restricted to moves toward their own opposite corner. Cells have rewards. The two routes must meet once, and the meeting cell earns no reward. Maximize the total collected on the two routes.
  • Replace strict geometric containment with a chainEnvelopes have (width,height) pairs (2,3), (2,4) and (3,5). One may be placed inside another only if both dimensions grow strictly, with no rotation. Find the greatest number that can form one nested chain.
  • Turn a reachability cone into coordinate inequalitiesEvents occur at specified times and positions on a line. One walker can move at most one unit of distance per unit of time and may skip events. Choose the largest feasible sequence of events without claiming two locations that are too far apart in time.
  • Compute the cheapest Manhattan delivery to every grid cellSeveral cells of an unobstructed rectangular grid offer a product at different prices. Delivery costs 1 per horizontal or vertical step. For every cell, find the smallest purchase price plus delivery cost among all sources. The cheapest source can differ from cell to cell.
  • Absolute distance can turn all predecessors into two sweepsSuppliers occupy positions along a straight road. A supplier at j charges its starting price plus the distance |i−j| to deliver to position i. For every position i, find the cheapest delivered price, including the option to buy from a supplier at i.
  • Choose the triangle incident to a boundary chordYou’re given the vertices of a convex polygon in boundary order. Split it into triangles using noncrossing diagonals. A triangulation’s score is the area of its largest triangle. Find the smallest possible score over all triangulations.
  • Two monotone chains replace a visited setYou’re given points with distinct x coordinates. A tour must go from the leftmost point to the rightmost along one chain of increasing x, then return along another, visiting every point once before closing. Find the shortest tour obeying those restrictions.

Try the two diagonals of a quadrilateral

Fix boundary side 0–1

Every triangulation contains exactly one triangle touching this boundary side. Its third vertex is either 2 or 3.

Try third vertex 2

This draws diagonal 0–2, of length √20. The two triangle perimeters together contain it twice.

Try third vertex 3

This draws diagonal 1–3, of length √17. Boundary length is identical, so this choice minimizes total triangle perimeter.

Two envelopes have the same width but different heights. How should a strict nesting sweep handle them?

Sort equal widths by descending height for length-only LIS, or query their whole batch before submitting any updates in weighted DP.

What part of the convex-polygon triangulation recurrence requires extra work for a concave polygon?

The boundary chord and proposed incident triangle must be legal and lie inside. Boundary order alone no longer certifies this.
