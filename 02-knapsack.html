Knapsack and subset sum

There are three physical items with weights 2, 3 and 3. You can take each once. Can you make exactly 5, and how many different selections make it? Those two questions read the same input but ask your table for different answers.

Start this topic in reading order → · See the whole reading path

  • Which sums can you make using each item once?You have a list of item weights and a target weight. You may use each physical item once, and you want to know whether some selection totals the target exactly. If the only item weighs 3 and the target is 6, using the same item twice would give a wrong answer.
  • Identical weights, distinguishable items, and multiplicityYou have three labeled tickets A, B and C, each worth 2. Count the selections totaling 4 when each ticket can be used once. Then consider how the answer changes if a selection records only how many identical coins you took.
  • Reuse, bounded stock, or one choice per groupYou have a budget and a shop lists the cost and benefit of each upgrade. Find the largest total benefit you can afford. In the worked shop two alternatives belong to the same group, so you may buy at most one of them. Other shops allow repeated purchases or give a stock limit.
  • Count bounded allocations with one sliding sumYou have three identical candies to distribute to two named children. One child can receive at most two and the other at most three. Count allocations that use every candy. Giving two to the first child and one to the second is different from giving one to the first and two to the second.
  • When do all subset sums form one interval?You have coins with positive integer values, and each coin can be spent once. Find the smallest positive amount that no selection can pay exactly. For coins worth 1, 2, 2 and 7, the two coins worth 2 are separate pieces.
  • Retrieve the selected items from a compressed sum tableA bag must weigh exactly 5 and the items weigh 2, 3 and 3. Each can be used once. You must return the indices of one valid selection, so you need to recover which physical items made the total after computing reachability.
  • Update many reachable sums with one shiftYou have three items weighing 2, 3 and 3. Each can be chosen at most once, and choosing nothing is allowed. Find every reachable total weight. Two different selections reaching the same weight light up just one answer.
  • When the capacity is huge, index by profitYour bag holds weight 7. The available items have (weight, profit) equal to (8,3), (3,2) and (4,2), and each may be taken once. Find the largest profit that fits. In the larger version the capacity can be huge even when the total profit is small.
  • Cardinality, two budgets, and resource couplingYou have items weighing 2, 3 and 5. Can you reach weight 5 using exactly two items? Then consider a version with a separate money budget as well as a weight limit. Each condition must hold for the same selection.
  • Negative weights, balance, residues, and nonempty subsetsEach listed transaction can be accepted or skipped once. Accepting it adds its signed amount to your balance, initially zero. Can you accept at least one transaction from +5, −3 and −2 and finish back at zero? Doing nothing does not qualify.
  • Partition, complements, gcd, and smallest missing sumA wallet contains positive coins of values 1, 2, 2 and 7. Any subset may be spent once. Find the first positive amount that cannot be paid exactly, and decide whether this particular question requires storing every reachable sum.
  • Remove a shared weight before indexing the capacityEach item has a profit and a very large weight, but all weights lie between W₀ and W₀+3. Choose each item at most once and maximize the profit under a capacity limit. For weights 1,000,000 and 1,000,002, even a budget of 2,000,001 cannot take both.
  • Solve a small geometric instance first, then knapsack its cost curvePainting a cell costs one. A completed row or a completed column earns a point, and their intersection needs painting only once. On an empty 2 × 3 board, find the minimum cost of earning two points. Later you can distribute a points target across several boards.
  • Which voting groups need every member?In a council, the three parties hold 4, 3 and 2 seats. A coalition wins if its members hold at least 5 seats together. Count the winning coalitions in which losing any one member would also lose the majority. Parties, even those with equal seat counts, are distinct.
  • Can the cashier make an unsplittable payout?A cashier must pay 12 cents and can choose the denominations used for the payout. Two people will then try to divide those exact coins into two piles worth 6 cents each. Can the cashier choose a payout that makes an equal split impossible? Compare paying with 5,5,1,1 and paying with 10,1,1.
  • Remove a large multiplicity with a residue-class dequeA shop sells an item of weight 2 and a given value, with stock limited to two copies. For each bag capacity, improve the best profit you already had from earlier items. You may buy zero, one or two copies and must respect the same stock limit at every capacity.
  • Build the knapsack of everything except one itemYour bag has capacity 4 and three items have (weight,value) pairs (2,3), (3,5) and (1,2). For each item separately, pretend it is unavailable and report the best value attainable with the others. Every report uses the original inventory minus just that one item.

Test your recognition
You now need the number of ways to use six identical tickets of value 2. Can you replace the stock with 1,2,3-ticket bundles and count 0/1 subsets?

No. Three tickets are represented by {3} and by {1,2}, although both represent one original choice. Count a bounded type using an old-row sum along its residue class.

A 10⁹ budget comes with 200 items, each earning at most 50 points. How would you recover the optimal score?

Index exact score up to 10000, minimizing weight per score. Scan the scores from high to low for one whose minimum weight fits.
