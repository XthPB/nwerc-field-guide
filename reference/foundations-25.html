Probability, expectation and honest randomisation

Expectation often removes the need for independence

If X counts successful events, write X as a sum of indicator variables. Then E[X]=ΣP(event_i), even if events are dependent. This is linearity of expectation. Independence is needed for multiplying probabilities, not for adding expectations.

In a random permutation of distinct values, position i is a record maximum with probability 1/i. Therefore the expected number of records is the harmonic number H_n≈log n+γ. Jagged Skyline uses this to pay for expensive binary searches only at random-order records.

Condition on one step and keep self-loops

Suppose a process takes one time unit, succeeds with probability 1−p, and on failure pays r and restarts. Then E=1+p(r+E), so E=(1+pr)/(1−p).

For a block of k unsaved characters, completing the previous k−1 characters is lost on a crash at the final attempt. The correct recurrence is:

E[k] = E[k−1] + 1 + p(r+E[k])
E[k] = (E[k−1]+1+pr)/(1−p)

Moving the self-reference to the left is the key step. A naive “expected attempts per character” sum ignores the destruction of earlier unsaved work.

Linearity inside arithmetic expressions

Accidental Arithmetic averages over random separators. Instead of enumerating every expression, track sufficient expected contributions or compute each digit's expected coefficient. This is the same habit as DP state design: identify which aggregate survives the next random choice.

A useful sanity test is to enumerate every random outcome for strings of length 1–6 and compare the formula. This catches missing factors and off-by-one exponents that a large random numeric test may hide.

Three different uses of randomness

Monte Carlo: bounded runtime, possible wrong answer. Hash equality without verification is an example.

Las Vegas: always correct after verification, random runtime. Gyrating Glyphs retries random codebooks until all responses are provably distinct, then decoding is exact.

Random-input algorithm: performance or success depends on a distribution promised by the statement. Evolutionary Excerpt and Lowest Latency explicitly provide such promises. Applying the same method to adversarial inputs changes the guarantee.

Random-order record search has a small probability of exceeding a strict query budget. An expectation below the budget is not a proof that every run fits. A tail bound, fallback, or accepted probabilistic risk must be discussed separately.

Error probability across many comparisons

If one independent bad event has probability at most p and you make Q opportunities for failure, the union bound gives failure probability at most Qp, without requiring independence. This explains why a hash modulus that looks large for one comparison can be uncomfortable for millions of comparisons.

For simulation, report uncertainty and sample size. A numerical judge expecting 10^-6 relative accuracy is rarely satisfied by an informal Monte Carlo estimate with a few thousand trials.

Checkpoint: does E[X]<12000 prove X≤12000?

No. A random variable may occasionally be much larger than its expectation. Expected query count and a worst-case query cap are different claims; Jagged Skyline is a good place to practise stating that distinction.
