Geometry: exact predicates before coordinates

You’re given endpoints of two line segments and must decide whether they intersect, including cases where an endpoint lies on the other segment. Before trying to calculate a crossing point, determine what the signs, ordering and input coordinates already let you decide exactly.

Turn the picture into signs

For vectors a and b, the dot product detects angle: a·b=0 means perpendicular. The 2D cross product ax·by−ay·bx detects orientation: positive is a left turn, negative a right turn, zero collinear. Squared lengths avoid square roots when only comparisons matter.

using i128=__int128_t;
struct P {long long x,y;};
i128 cross(P a,P b){return (i128)a.x*b.y-(i128)a.y*b.x;}
i128 dot(P a,P b){return (i128)a.x*b.x+(i128)a.y*b.y;}

Cast before multiplication. Even if coordinates fit 32-bit, differences and products may not. Exact integer predicates are preferable whenever the input is integral and the relevant quantity can be expressed algebraically.

Segment intersection

For non-collinear segments, endpoints must lie on opposite sides of the other segment's supporting line. Collinear cases also require overlapping coordinate projections. Decide whether touching at an endpoint counts as an intersection in the problem; geometric templates often hide this policy.

A single universal epsilon is not a cure for numeric uncertainty. An absolute epsilon of 10^-9 has very different meaning at coordinate scale 1 and 10^12. Prefer exact predicates; when computing real coordinates, use scale-aware comparisons and verify the final constraints.

Convex hull and rotating calipers

Andrew's monotone chain sorts points, then builds lower and upper hulls by removing a middle point when the turn is invalid. O(n log n) time. Strict versus non-strict cross-product tests decide whether collinear boundary points remain. Remove duplicates and handle fewer than three distinct points.

Rotating calipers exploits monotonic movement of supporting lines around a convex polygon to find diameter, minimum width, or related extrema. The hull is useful because interior points cannot maximise a linear projection.

Continuous constructions

Kinking Cables asks for a path of an exact real length. Construct a family of valid paths indexed by a scalar; prove every member respects geometry, prove the length varies continuously, and bracket the requested value. Binary search then chooses a member. Searching coordinates without a valid-family invariant is much harder to trust.

Spatial buckets and packing

A distance threshold D suggests cells of side comparable to D. A point need only compare with a bounded number of neighbouring cells. But a cell may contain many points unless an input condition, a maintained separation invariant, or a distribution promise bounds occupancy. Coherency uses object sizes and non-overlap; Lowest Latency explicitly promises random points.

Projection counting in higher dimensions

Linguistic Labyrinth contains perpendicularity constraints such as (B−A)·(P−A)=0. For a fixed direction, points with equal dot products share a plane orthogonal to that direction. Counting equal projections can replace enumerating every quadruple. This is geometry becoming a frequency table.

Checkpoint: why compare squared distances instead of distances?

Square root is strictly increasing on nonnegative values, so it preserves comparisons. Squared integer distances can be exact and avoid unnecessary floating-point work. Ensure the sum of squared coordinate differences fits the chosen type.
